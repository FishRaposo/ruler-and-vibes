## Task

Below are seven dated sources reporting on a disputed art find, the "Esker
Hollow sketch." Several sources explicitly say where they got their
information — trace who is repeating whom before treating agreement between
sources as independent corroboration.

Produce `provenance-map.md` with three parts:

1. A **derivation map**: for every source that explicitly attributes its
   information to an earlier source, state the derivation edge (which
   source it derives from) and quote or paraphrase the attributing phrase
   it uses. State which sources are NOT derived from any other listed
   source.
2. An **independent-origin count** for the central claim that the sketch is
   an authentic work by the painter Corentin Vasseur: how many genuinely
   independent origins assert this attribution (after collapsing any
   derivation chains), and which sources those are. Separately, identify
   any source that does NOT assert the attribution claim at all, and name
   the different detail it reports instead.
3. A **first-appearance timeline**: order all seven sources by their stated
   dates and identify which source is earliest / originates the
   attribution claim.

### Sources

#### S1 — Hallcross Auction House newsletter, dated 3 June

Specialists cataloguing the contents of a recent estate clearance near
Esker Hollow say they've identified a significant work: a small oil sketch
on panel, now referred to informally as the Esker Hollow sketch. Based on
the brushwork, palette, and panel construction, the house's specialist
team attributes the sketch to the little-known nineteenth-century painter
Corentin Vasseur. A fuller technical catalogue note is planned ahead of
the autumn sale, but the team says the attribution is consistent with
other studies from the same estate lot.

#### S2 — Continental Art Authentication Institute bulletin, dated 7 June

Our own examination of the Esker Hollow sketch, conducted independently
of the auction house's public statements, supports an attribution to
Corentin Vasseur. The underdrawing and pigment mixtures are consistent
with Vasseur studies our institute has catalogued in prior surveys, and
we are confident in this attribution based on our own review of
raking-light photographs and a paint cross-section sample provided by
the estate's executor.

#### S3 — Cotebridge Notebook (regional arts blog), dated 11 June

As first reported by the Hallcross Auction House, the sketch uncovered
during the Esker Hollow estate clearance has been attributed to Corentin
Vasseur. This is a welcome addition to the painter's thin known catalogue,
and we'll continue following the story as more details emerge from the
auction house's specialists.

#### S4 — University conservation laboratory technical note, dated 16 June

Following our own physical examination of the Esker Hollow sketch ahead
of a proposed cradling treatment, we can report that the panel measures
31.5 cm across, somewhat wider than the auction listing's photographs
suggested. This measurement is based on direct examination on our lab
bench and has not been previously reported elsewhere.

#### S5 — Follow-up wire piece, dated 20 June

Citing the same Hallcross account that broke the story, wire services
continue to report that the Esker Hollow sketch has been attributed to
Corentin Vasseur. The sketch has drawn interest from several regional
collectors since the initial report.

#### S6 — Regional arts podcast transcript, dated 25 June

Host: So this sketch from the Esker Hollow estate — remind listeners what
we know.

Guest: Right, so following the report on the Cotebridge Notebook, the
sketch has been attributed to Corentin Vasseur. It's a promising addition
to his catalogue, assuming that holds up once the fuller technical note
comes out.

#### S7 — Interview with the estate's liquidator, dated 30 June

Interviewer: You handled the clearance — what can you add beyond what's
been reported?

Liquidator: Most of the coverage so far has focused on the attribution,
but I think the more interesting open question is custody — where the
sketch has actually been since it left the house. Based on my own
inventory log, it went straight from the estate to the auction house's
storage vault, then to the university conservation lab, without passing
through any of the intermediate hands some early rumors suggested. I
kept that log myself, so I can speak to it directly.

## Deliverables

- `provenance-map.md`: the derivation map, the independent-origin count
  (with the sources counted and the sources excluded), and the
  first-appearance timeline.

## Constraints

- Every derivation edge must be paired with the attributing phrase the
  source itself uses.
- The independent-origin count for the Vasseur attribution claim must be
  a single number with the specific sources named, not left as a range
  or omitted.
- Any source that does not assert the attribution claim must be
  explicitly excluded from the count, with the different detail it
  reports named instead.
- The timeline must include all seven sources in date order.
