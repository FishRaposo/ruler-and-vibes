## Task

Below are four fictional sources on a "remote-work productivity" review
and five candidate claims (C1-C5). Build a triangulation matrix, decide
which claims are genuinely corroborated, and then write one synthesized
theme sentence.

Produce `triangulation.md` with:

1. A **matrix** with rows C1-C5 and columns S1-S4, where each cell is
   exactly one of `asserts`, `silent`, or `contradicts`.
2. For each claim, a verdict of exactly `CORROBORATED` or
   `NOT-CORROBORATED`. A claim is CORROBORATED only if at least two
   **independent** sources assert it — check whether any asserting
   source explicitly says it is drawing on another source in this set
   before counting it as a separate vote.
3. An explicit note identifying any source that is not independent of
   another, and which claim's verdict that affects.
4. Exactly one **synthesized theme sentence** that draws only on
   claims you have marked CORROBORATED.

### Sources

#### S1 — Company-run productivity survey

Our internal survey of 400 employees found that staff working remotely
at least three days a week self-reported higher productivity than
fully in-office peers. We also found that employees with scheduled,
meeting-free "deep work" blocks reported better output regardless of
where they worked, and noted fewer sick days taken by remote workers
compared to in-office staff. Separately, we found that employees whose
managers checked in with them regularly reported higher perceived
productivity than those with infrequent manager contact, and this held
true across both remote and in-office groups.

#### S2 — Independent academic study

This study, based on our own original survey of a separate sample of
office workers across several industries, found that employees working
remotely at least three days a week reported higher self-rated
productivity than in-office peers, consistent with prior industry
reporting. We also found that scheduled deep-work blocks improved
output more than remote-versus-office status alone did — in fact, deep
work blocks were the single strongest predictor of self-reported
output in our sample. Frequent manager check-ins also correlated with
higher perceived productivity, regardless of work location, in our
data as well.

#### S3 — Trade publication feature

Drawing on the S1 review of remote-work outcomes, this feature
highlights that remote employees working three or more days a week
reported higher productivity than office-based colleagues, and that
remote workers took fewer sick days on average. The piece also
discusses manager check-in frequency as a factor worth watching,
echoing points raised in the same underlying review, though it does not
present new data of its own on this point.

#### S4 — HR consultancy benchmarking report

Our benchmarking work with client companies found that scheduled
deep-work blocks improved output more than remote-versus-office status
alone — a pattern that showed up consistently across our client base.
We also found that onboarding new hires remotely took measurably longer
than in-office onboarding, a friction point several clients specifically
flagged. Finally, frequent manager check-ins correlated with higher
perceived productivity in our client data regardless of whether the
employee worked remotely or in-office.

### Candidate claims

- **C1**: Employees working remotely at least three days a week
  reported higher self-rated productivity than fully in-office peers.
- **C2**: Scheduled deep-work blocks improved output more than
  remote-versus-office status alone.
- **C3**: Remote workers took measurably fewer sick days than in-office
  peers.
- **C4**: Onboarding new hires remotely took measurably longer than
  in-office onboarding.
- **C5**: Manager check-in frequency correlated with perceived
  productivity regardless of work location.

## Deliverables

- `triangulation.md`: the 5x4 matrix, the corroboration verdicts, the
  independence note, and one synthesized theme sentence.

## Constraints

- The matrix must have exactly 5 claim rows and 4 source columns, with
  every cell exactly one of `asserts`, `silent`, or `contradicts`.
- Every claim verdict must be exactly `CORROBORATED` or
  `NOT-CORROBORATED`.
- The independence note must name which source is not independent of
  which other, and which claim's verdict this affects.
- Exactly one synthesized theme sentence must appear, and it must not
  rely on any claim marked NOT-CORROBORATED.
