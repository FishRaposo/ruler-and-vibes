## Task

Below are seven dated sources reporting on a claimed archaeological
find, the "Verrow tablet." Several sources explicitly say where they got
their information — trace who is repeating whom before treating
agreement between sources as independent corroboration.

Produce `provenance-map.md` with three parts:

1. A **derivation map**: for every source that explicitly attributes its
   information to an earlier source, state the derivation edge (which
   source it derives from) and quote or paraphrase the attributing
   phrase it uses. State which sources are NOT derived from any other
   listed source.
2. An **independent-origin count** for the central claim that the
   tablet dates to the 3rd century: how many genuinely independent
   origins assert this date (after collapsing any derivation chains),
   and which sources those are. Separately, identify any source that
   does NOT assert the date claim at all, and name the different detail
   it reports instead.
3. A **first-appearance timeline**: order all seven sources by their
   stated dates and identify which source is earliest / originates the
   date claim.

### Sources

#### S1 — Ledsham Gazette, dated 3 March

Excavators working a coastal dig near Ledsham have uncovered what the
lead archaeologist is calling a significant find: a carved stone tablet,
now referred to locally as the Verrow tablet. Based on the site's
stratigraphy and the carving style, the excavation team dates the
tablet to the 3rd century. The team plans a fuller technical report in
the coming months, but says the dating is consistent with other finds
recovered from the same layer at the site.

#### S2 — Regional archaeology bulletin, dated 5 March

Our own examination of the Verrow tablet's lettering style, conducted
independently of the excavation team's public statements, supports a
3rd-century date. The letterforms are consistent with regional
inscriptions from that period that our bulletin has catalogued in prior
surveys, and we are confident in this dating based on our own review of
photographs and rubbings provided by the site's registrar.

#### S3 — Regional heritage blog, dated 9 March

As first reported by the Ledsham Gazette, the newly uncovered Verrow
tablet has been dated to the 3rd century. This is an exciting addition
to the region's known archaeological record, and we'll continue
following the story as more details emerge from the excavation team.

#### S4 — Museum curator's technical note, dated 12 March

Following our own physical examination of the Verrow tablet ahead of its
planned conservation, we can report that the inscription survives to a
length of 14.2 cm, somewhat longer than initial photographs suggested.
This measurement is based on direct calipered examination in the
conservation lab and has not been previously reported elsewhere.

#### S5 — Follow-up wire piece, dated 15 March

Citing the same Gazette account that broke the story, wire services
continue to report that the Verrow tablet has been dated to the 3rd
century. The find has drawn interest from several regional museums
since the initial report.

#### S6 — Regional history podcast transcript, dated 20 March

Host: So this tablet, the Verrow tablet — remind listeners what we know.

Guest: Right, so following the report in the Regional heritage blog, the
tablet's been dated to the 3rd century. It's a nice find for the
region, assuming that holds up once the fuller technical report comes
out.

#### S7 — Interview with the excavation's lead surveyor, dated 25 March

Interviewer: You were on site when the tablet was found — what can you
add beyond what's been reported?

Surveyor: Most of the coverage so far has focused on the dating, but I
think the more interesting open question is custody — where the tablet
has actually been since it came out of the ground. Based on my own site
log, it went straight from the trench to the registrar's temporary
store, then to the regional museum's conservation lab, without passing
through any of the intermediate hands some early rumors suggested. I
kept that log myself, so I can speak to it directly.

## Deliverables

- `provenance-map.md`: the derivation map, the independent-origin count
  (with the sources counted and the sources excluded), and the
  first-appearance timeline.

## Constraints

- Every derivation edge must be paired with the attributing phrase the
  source itself uses.
- The independent-origin count for the 3rd-century date claim must be a
  single number with the specific sources named, not left as a range or
  omitted.
- Any source that does not assert the date claim must be explicitly
  excluded from the count, with the different detail it reports named
  instead.
- The timeline must include all seven sources in date order.
