## Task

Below are eight sources covering the fictional fire that destroyed the
Hollowfen Mill in the town of Thistlewick. Read them carefully — some dates
conflict between sources, one date is written ambiguously, and one source's
stated total does not match its own itemized figures.

Produce `dossier.md` (at most 1,100 words, whole file) with three parts:

1. A **timeline table** of 8 key events, each with a date and at least
   one `[S#]` citation, resolving any date conflicts between sources.

### Sources

#### S1 — News dispatch, filed the day of the partial fire

Word reached the county seat today of a partial fire at the Hollowfen
Mill. According to early reports carried in by cart from Thistlewick, a
fire broke out in the carding room on 24 August amid a run of hot, dry
weather. No injuries have been reported among the mill's overnight crew.
Details remain sparse at this hour, and this account is based on
secondhand reports relayed by a Thistlewick merchant who first raised
the alarm at the county seat; a fuller account is expected once the
telegraph line to the mill is restored, as the fire reached the roof
and burned through the wire during the night.

Neighboring millowners have already voiced concern about lost production
capacity while Hollowfen is out of commission, and several have offered
to take on rush orders for Hollowfen's regular customers in the
meantime. This dispatch will be updated as more reliable information
reaches the county seat; given the disrupted communication line, readers
should treat today's figures and dates as provisional pending a fuller
account from Thistlewick or the relevant fire authorities.

Several buyers altered their standing orders overnight on the strength
of word-of-mouth warnings alone, underscoring how quickly news of this
kind travels along the valley even without formal notice. This
publication will continue to follow developments at Hollowfen and will
issue a correction promptly if any detail in this early account proves
inaccurate once fuller reporting reaches us from the mill.

#### S2 — Official inquiry summary, Thistlewick Fire Board

This summary presents the Thistlewick Fire Board's findings following
its inquiry into the fire at the Hollowfen Mill. The partial fire in
the carding room occurred on 22 August, not 24 August as an early
dispatch reported; the Board's timeline is based on the foreman's
contemporaneous logbook and on the volunteer fire brigade's records. The
foreman of record at the time of the fire was Alder Marsh, who had
managed the mill for the preceding eight years. Following the partial
fire, smoldering deterioration continued in the wall cavities despite
emergency shoring efforts, and the remaining structure suffered full
destruction on 30 October. The site was formally condemned by the town
council on 15 November, on the Board's recommendation, and will not be
rebuilt on its original foundation given the deterioration of the mill
leat's stone footings identified during the inquiry.

The Board's investigation included a review of the foreman's logbook, a
review of the fire brigade's records, and site visits by a structural
engineer retained by the town specifically for this inquiry. The Board
wishes to thank Foreman Marsh for cooperating fully throughout the
inquiry, including providing the original logbook for review and
answering the Board's follow-up questions in writing during the weeks
following the full destruction. This summary represents the Board's
final findings on the sequence of events and is not expected to be
revised absent new evidence coming to light.

#### S3 — Insurance assessor's memo

This memo records my assessment of the insured losses at the Hollowfen
Mill following its destruction by fire. The structure carried a
pre-fire insured value of 555,000. My itemized assessment of the
primary damage claim breaks down as follows: building and structure
damage, 238,600; machinery and looms, 176,900; raw wool and cotton
stock loss, 84,750. The total claim on this primary assessment is
541,000. Separately, I have prepared an itemization for the
ancillary-structures claim, covering outbuildings not part of the
primary mill structure: dye house, 63,400; gatehouse, 28,600; for an
ancillary-structures total of 92,000. Both claims have been forwarded
to the underwriter for review and payment determination.

I conducted my site visit on 27 August, several days after the partial
fire, and returned twice more in the following weeks as the structure
continued to deteriorate ahead of its full destruction. The primary
damage figures above reflect the cost of full reconstruction at current
material and labor rates, not depreciated replacement value, consistent
with the policy's terms. I have flagged the primary claim total for a
secondary review given the scale of the loss, which is standard
practice for claims of this size under our office's internal
procedures.

The building-and-structure figure reflects the cost of clearing and
rebuilding the burned carding room and adjoining wall sections, based on
current timber and masonry pricing and estimated labor hours for work
of this kind. The machinery-and-looms figure covers replacement of the
carding engines, spinning frames, and surrounding line-shafting, most of
which was destroyed when the fire reached the machine floor. The
raw-stock figure covers the wool and cotton bales lost to fire and smoke
damage in the adjoining storeroom. I will forward photographic
documentation supporting each of these three figures under separate
cover.

#### S4 — Foreman's logbook excerpt

Signed, A. Marsh, Foreman.

3 July: Noted a scorch mark and a faulty flue seam above the carding
room this morning during routine inspection. Nothing like it in my time
here before. Reported the fault to the mill owners by the next post and
made a note to keep a closer eye on that section going forward.

22 August: The carding room partially caught fire this afternoon during
a run of the drying machinery. No injuries. Have moved essential
equipment out of the affected section as a precaution.

2 September: Evacuated the workers' dormitory today on the advice of
the inspecting engineer, given continued deterioration of the structure
since the partial fire. Arranged temporary lodging with families in
town for the crew who had been staying on site. This is the final entry
I am able to make in this logbook, as I handed it over to the
assessor's office for safekeeping shortly afterward, along with copies
for my own records going forward.

#### S5 — Interview transcript

Interview with local historian Cassia Thorne, conducted for a community
oral-history project.

Interviewer: Can you tell us what the Hollowfen Mill meant to the town?

Thorne: It was really the town's anchor, in a way — generations of
families here had someone who worked the mill floor or grew up hearing
the looms running from down the lane. When word got around about the
fire, there was real grief in town, not just concern about lost work.
People brought food to the crew's families, checked in on each other,
that sort of thing. It reminded a lot of us how much the mill had
quietly held the town together for so long, even for people who never
set foot inside it.

Interviewer: What do you think should happen to the site now?

Thorne: I'd like to see some kind of memorial marker at minimum, even if
rebuilding on the original foundation isn't possible given the leat
issues the inquiry found. The town shouldn't just let the story of that
mill fade away.

Interviewer: That's a genuinely lovely way to put it. Is there anything
else at all about the mill's long history you think people should know?

Thorne: Just that it wasn't the town's first mill — there was an older,
smaller water-wheel mill before this one, though most people today don't
realize that. I think it's worth remembering that the town has always
found a way to keep its looms running one way or another, and I'd hope
that continues in some form even after this fire.

#### S6 — Gazette entry

Hollowfen Mill, historical note: construction of the Hollowfen Mill was
completed on 3 May 1901, replacing an earlier, smaller water-wheel mill
that had served the valley since the previous century. The original
mill had served the valley for several decades but was judged inadequate
for the increasing wool trade passing through Thistlewick by the later
part of that century, prompting the town to petition for a larger,
steam-powered replacement mill. The mill served continuously from its
completion until this year's fire, a run of service now approaching a
century and a quarter.

Recent events addendum: on the ninth of October, emergency shoring work
began at the Hollowfen Mill following the partial fire earlier that
summer, in an effort to stabilize the remaining structure ahead of the
engineering assessment. The shoring effort ultimately did not prevent
the structure's full destruction several weeks later, though it is
credited with buying the time needed to safely remove remaining
equipment and records from the site before the final fire occurred.

#### S7 — Letter from a Thistlewick resident to a relative

Dear Linnet,

I hope this finds you well, and that the children are keeping you busy
as always. Things have been unsettled here since the mill trouble began,
more than I expected when I first wrote to tell you about it.

We had to help several of the crew move out of the dormitory on 2
September, once the engineers said it wasn't safe to stay given how
things had been going since the carding room caught fire. It's been
strange not hearing the looms running some days when the shoring crews
have equipment blocking the yard.

Shoring crews arrived 09/10, working long hours to try to save what's
left of the mill, though from what I can see from the lane it doesn't
look promising. They've got scaffolding and timber bracing up around
the base now, and there's talk in town about whether it will hold long
enough for a proper engineering assessment to be completed. The whole
town has been talking about little else, and I expect it will stay that
way for some time yet given how much the mill meant to everyone here.
I'll write again once there's more news. Give my love to the children.

Yours, Hesper

#### S8 — Retrospective article, byline Cassia Thorne

Years later, the Hollowfen Mill fire remains a defining memory for many
longtime residents. The structure suffered its full and final
destruction on 30 October, following weeks of deterioration after an
earlier partial fire that summer. What struck me most, looking back,
was not the fire itself but how the town responded to it — the same
spirit of quiet mutual care that the mill itself seemed to represent for
so long. The site remains condemned today, marked only by a small
plaque, though there has been occasional talk of a more lasting
memorial. Whatever ultimately becomes of the site, the Hollowfen mill
holds a permanent place in this town's memory.

I've spent a good deal of time since then talking with longtime
residents about what the mill meant to them, and the same themes come
up again and again: livelihood, certainly, but also a sense of
continuity, of the town keeping its looms running night and day for
generations. Photographs of the mill floor still hang in the town hall
and in more than a few homes around Thistlewick, and more than one
resident has told me they still instinctively listen for the looms at
dusk, half-expecting to hear them start up again. Whatever form a
future memorial might take, I hope it captures that sense of quiet,
steady presence rather than just marking the fact of the fire itself.
