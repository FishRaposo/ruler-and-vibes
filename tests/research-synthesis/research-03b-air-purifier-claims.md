## Task

Below are six sources addressing whether a fictional portable air
purifier, the "AeroClear Mini," reduces indoor PM2.5 (fine particulate)
levels, followed by four claims that are commonly circulated about the
product. The sources vary widely in rigor — pay attention to source
type, sample size, independence, and whether any finding has since been
withdrawn or corrected.

Produce `evidence-brief.md` (at most 600 words, whole file) containing:

1. An evidence table grading all six sources, with columns covering at
   least source type, sample size (or N/A), and key finding.
2. A "Bottom line" section containing a line labeled exactly `Best
   supported estimate:` followed by your conclusion, plus a confidence
   statement.
3. A "Claims no source supports" section, drawn from the four commonly
   circulated claims below.

### Sources

#### S1 — Manufacturer marketing page (Havenwell Home Goods)

Havenwell Home Goods is proud to introduce the AeroClear Mini, our
flagship portable air purifier for home use. In our own product trials,
the AeroClear Mini eliminates 90% of indoor PM2.5 particulates in under
an hour compared to an untreated room — see the difference for yourself
after just one evening. Homeowners across the country have made the
AeroClear Mini part of their nightly routine, running it on the
nightstand for a noticeable improvement in air freshness. We're so
confident in the results that we back every unit with our satisfaction
guarantee. Try it in your own bedroom and see why so many households
have made the switch this year.

#### S2 — Hobbyist blog testimonial

I've been running the AeroClear Mini in my bedroom this spring and I
have to say, the air does feel noticeably fresher compared to before I
got it. I just leave it on the low setting overnight and eyeball the
difference in the morning against how stuffy the room used to feel.
Unrelated side note, I also finally brought home a new cockatiel this
month after weeks of prepping the cage — that little guy has already
taken over the living room. Anyway, back to the purifier: I'd recommend
giving the AeroClear Mini a try if you're curious, though I'm just one
person and not running anything like a real controlled experiment here.

#### S3 — Pilot study, n=9, published in a regional indoor-air-quality bulletin

This pilot study examined the effect of the AeroClear Mini on indoor
PM2.5 levels under controlled conditions, using a sample of 9 test rooms
split between purifier-treated and untreated groups. Treated rooms
showed an 84% reduction in PM2.5 concentration compared to untreated
controls over a one-hour observation window. Particulate levels were
measured using a laser particle counter logging readings every minute.
Given the small sample size, we regard this as a preliminary finding
meriting larger-scale replication before any strong conclusions are
drawn about the purifier's effectiveness across broader household
conditions.

#### S4 — Controlled multi-site trial, n=150

This study evaluated the AeroClear Mini's effect on indoor PM2.5 levels
using a randomized, blinded, multi-site design across 150 households.
Treated rooms showed a PM2.5 reduction of 9% relative to untreated
controls (95% confidence interval: 4% to 14%), a modest but statistically
significant effect. This result is notably smaller than figures reported
in earlier, less rigorous testing, and we believe the larger sample
size, blinded measurement protocol, and multi-site design make this the
most reliable estimate of the purifier's true effect available to date.

#### S5 — Published correction notice

This notice formally corrects "Pilot Study of the AeroClear Mini on
Indoor PM2.5 Levels Under Controlled Conditions," previously published
by the same regional indoor-air-quality bulletin's authors. A
post-publication audit discovered that the study's laser particle
counter was miscalibrated throughout the observation period,
systematically overstating measured reduction. Accordingly, the study's
reported 84% reduction figure is withdrawn and should not be relied
upon. The original authors have been notified of this correction and
support its publication. Readers seeking a reliable estimate of the
AeroClear Mini's effect on indoor PM2.5 levels should consult more
recent, properly calibrated research instead.

#### S6 — Trade-magazine overview

The AeroClear Mini has generated considerable buzz among homeowners and
home-goods retailers alike over the past year. Early claims of dramatic
particulate elimination, some suggesting the unit could clear the vast
majority of indoor PM2.5 within an hour, have not held up well once more
rigorous, larger-scale controlled testing entered the picture. Retailers
report steady interest from customers regardless, and the unit remains a
popular seasonal seller. As with many home-goods products, the gap
between marketing claims and what controlled research ultimately
supports appears to be substantial in this case, though the unit does
appear to have some genuine, if modest, effect according to the more
careful studies available.

### Commonly circulated claims

1. The AeroClear Mini eliminates 90% of indoor PM2.5 particulates within
   an hour.
2. The AeroClear Mini has been shown to reduce indoor PM2.5 levels by a
   modest, single-digit-to-low-double-digit percentage in controlled
   testing.
3. The original clinical-sounding purification-rate claim from the early
   pilot study was later formally withdrawn due to a testing error.
4. The AeroClear Mini is safe for use in households with pet birds.
