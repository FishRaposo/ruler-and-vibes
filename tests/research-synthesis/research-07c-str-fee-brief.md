## Task

Below are six fictional sources about a proposed "short-term rental
registration fee" in the town of Haverbrook and a finished advocacy
brief that argues for the fee. The brief was written using only some
of the source material. Read the sources and the brief carefully, then
audit what the brief left out.

Produce `coverage-audit.md` with three clearly separated findings, each
carrying at least one `[S#]` citation:

1. **Omitted sources** — every source the brief never cites, and the
   specific fact each one contains that cuts against or qualifies the
   brief's argument.
2. **Silent scope-narrowing** — any place the brief restricts its claim
   to a narrower scope than the sources support, without saying so.
3. **Dropped caveat** — any place the brief quotes a source's headline
   figure while omitting a caveat that same source attaches to it.

### Sources

#### S1 — City housing department fact sheet

The proposed short-term-rental (STR) registration fee is projected to
reduce the number of long-term housing units converted to STR use in
the Old Mill District during peak summer season by approximately 16%,
based on modeling of comparable fee programs in similar tourist towns.
The department projects that reduced STR turnover would also produce
modest improvements in on-street parking availability for residents of
the district.

#### S2 — Housing economist op-ed

Based on modeling calibrated to similar registration-fee programs, the
number of long-term rental listings available in the Old Mill District
during peak summer season could increase by roughly 11 additional
units per month once the fee is in effect. This estimate assumes full
compliance with the registration requirement and excludes the
first-year grace period, during which enforcement and host
registration are typically incomplete; realized increases in year one
are likely to be smaller than the steady-state figure above.

#### S3 — Comparable-town case study

A comparable tourist town that implemented a similar STR registration
fee four years ago saw a 17% drop in tourism-related small-business
revenue (gift shops, walking-tour operators, and café patios) within
the first year of implementation, according to the local merchants'
guild's retrospective analysis. Business owners attributed the decline
primarily to fewer short-term visitors staying within walking distance
of their shops after a number of hosts exited the STR market rather
than pay the fee. Revenue partially recovered in year two as remaining
hosts and new visitors adjusted, but remained below pre-fee levels two
years out.

#### S4 — City council testimony summary

A representative of the regional hotel and inn owners' association
testified in support of the registration-fee proposal, citing reduced
unfair price competition from unregistered short-term rentals as a
benefit to member properties. The representative stated that steadier,
registered STR supply would offset the cost of increased compliance
monitoring for most member properties within the first two years.

#### S5 — Festival-weekend demand study

While the STR registration fee proposal is framed around peak summer
tourist season, this study finds that short-term-rental demand during
the Old Mill District's autumn harvest-festival weekends is comparable
to peak-summer levels for much of the fall. Any fee structure limited
to the summer season would leave this festival-driven autumn demand
largely unaddressed, and could shift some summer STR activity into
festival weekends instead.

#### S6 — Community equity advocacy report

Interviews with small-scale short-term-rental hosts in the Old Mill
District who rely on STR income to supplement earnings from part-time
or seasonal work (retirees, rideshare drivers, seasonal
event-staffing workers) indicate that many of these hosts operate a
single unit and have little ability to absorb a flat per-unit fee
compared to commercial operators managing multiple units. Because the
proposed fee structure applies the same flat rate regardless of
portfolio size, this report concludes that low-income single-unit
hosts would bear a disproportionate share of the fee's cost relative
to higher-income commercial multi-property operators.

### The brief under audit

> **Why the Old Mill District Registration Fee Deserves Support**
>
> The proposed short-term-rental registration fee is a well-modeled,
> hospitality-backed approach to easing one of the Old Mill District's
> most persistent problems: the loss of long-term housing to summer
> tourist rentals. The city housing department's own modeling projects
> a meaningful roughly 16% cut in long-term units converted to STR use
> during summer season once the fee takes effect [S1], and independent
> housing-economics analysis backs this up with real numbers renters
> will notice: an estimated 11 additional long-term rental listings
> available per month in the district [S2].
>
> Critics of registration fees often warn of harm to the hospitality
> economy, but the hospitality community itself has said otherwise. In
> testimony before the city council, a representative of the regional
> hotel and inn owners' association voiced support for the fee,
> pointing to reduced unfair price competition from unregistered
> short-term rentals as a direct benefit to member properties, with
> steadier, registered STR supply expected to offset the cost of
> compliance monitoring for most members within the first two years
> [S4].
>
> Taken together, the evidence here is straightforward: a modest fee
> during the summer tourist season meaningfully shifts housing back
> from short-term rentals, restores long-term rental supply renters can
> actually find, and earns support from the very hospitality businesses
> skeptics assume would oppose it. The city should move forward with
> implementation.

## Deliverables

- `coverage-audit.md`: three clearly separated findings — omitted
  sources, silent scope-narrowing, and a dropped caveat — each with at
  least one `[S#]` citation.

## Constraints

- Every source the brief never cites must be identified by ID, with the
  specific fact it contains that the brief's argument omits.
- Do not list a source the brief actually cites (S1, S2, S4) as
  "omitted."
- The scope-narrowing finding must identify the specific narrower scope
  (summer tourist season) versus the broader scope the sources support.
- The dropped-caveat finding must name the specific caveat, not just
  note that "some caveat exists."
