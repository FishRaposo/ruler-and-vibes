## Task

Below are eight sources covering the fictional Hollow Pine Concert Hall
fire. Read them carefully — some dates conflict between sources, one
date is written ambiguously, and one source's stated total does not
match its own itemized figures.

Produce `dossier.md` (at most 1,150 words, whole file) with three parts:

1. A **timeline table** of 8 key events, each with a date and at least
   one `[S#]` citation, resolving any date conflicts between sources.
2. Answers to the **6 numbered research questions** below. If a question
   is not answerable from the sources, say so explicitly — do not
   answer from plausible general knowledge.
3. A **discrepancy analysis** identifying which source's stated total
   does not match its own itemized components, showing the arithmetic.

### Sources

#### S1 — News dispatch, filed the day of the partial fire

Word reached the county seat today of a partial fire at the Hollow Pine
Concert Hall in Aldercross. According to early reports carried by coach
from the district, fire broke out in the hall's west wing on 16 November
amid high winds and dry conditions. No injuries have been reported among
the caretaker's household. Details remain sparse at this hour, and this
account is based on secondhand reports relayed by a mail rider who first
carried word to the county seat; a fuller account is expected once
telephone service to the district is restored, as the same windstorm
that fanned the flames also brought down the line.

Local merchants have already voiced concern about the hall's winter
concert season without a venue, and the town clerk's office says a
temporary notice board will be posted near the site within the week.
This dispatch will be updated as more reliable information reaches the
county seat, and given the disrupted communication lines, readers should
treat today's figures and dates as provisional pending a fuller account
from the district or the relevant county authorities.

Several residents altered their evening plans overnight on the strength
of word-of-mouth warnings alone, underscoring how quickly news of this
kind travels through the county even without formal notice. This
publication will continue to follow developments at Hollow Pine Hall as
they become available and will issue a correction promptly if any detail
in this early account proves inaccurate once fuller reporting reaches us
from the district.

#### S2 — Official investigation summary, Aldercross County Fire Review Board

This summary presents the Aldercross County Fire Review Board's findings
following its investigation into the fire at Hollow Pine Concert Hall.
The partial fire in the west wing occurred on 14 November, not 16
November as an early dispatch reported; the Board's timeline is based on
the caretaker's contemporaneous logbook and on fire brigade dispatch
records. The caretaker of record at the time of the fire was Linden
Marrow, who maintained the hall for the preceding four years. Following
the partial fire, structural deterioration continued despite emergency
salvage efforts, and the remaining structure suffered full destruction on
9 January. The site was formally condemned by the town council on 20
January, on the Board's recommendation, and will not be rebuilt on its
original foundation given the extensive foundation cracking discovered
during clearance work.

The Board's investigation included a review of the caretaker's logbook, a
review of fire brigade dispatch records, and site visits by a structural
engineer retained by the town specifically for this investigation. The
Board wishes to thank Caretaker Marrow for cooperating fully throughout
the investigation, including providing the original logbook for review
and answering the Board's follow-up questions in writing during the
weeks following the full destruction. This summary represents the
Board's final findings on the sequence of events and is not expected to
be revised absent new evidence coming to light.

#### S3 — Insurance assessor's memo

This memo records my assessment of the insured losses at Hollow Pine
Concert Hall following its fire. The structure carried a pre-fire insured
value of 610,000. My itemized assessment of the primary damage claim
breaks down as follows: auditorium structure and roof, 342,000; pipe
organ and stage rigging, 156,750; foyer and box-office fittings, 88,400.
The total claim on this primary assessment is 634,000. Separately, I have
prepared an itemization for the ancillary-structures claim, covering
outbuildings not part of the primary hall structure: rehearsal annex,
97,500; equipment storage barn, 41,200; for an ancillary-structures total
of 138,700. Both claims have been forwarded to the underwriter for review
and payment determination.

I conducted my site visit on 19 November, several days after the partial
fire, and returned twice more in the following weeks as the structure
continued to deteriorate ahead of its full destruction. The primary
damage figures above reflect the cost of full reconstruction at current
material and labor rates, not depreciated replacement value, consistent
with the policy's terms. I have flagged the primary claim total for a
secondary review given the scale of the loss, which is standard practice
for claims of this size under our office's internal procedures.

The auditorium figure reflects the cost of clearing and rebuilding the
burned west wing and adjoining roof sections, based on current timber and
labor pricing and estimated labor hours for work of this kind. The
organ-and-rigging figure covers replacement of the pipe organ, stage
mechanism, and surrounding ironwork, most of which was destroyed when the
west wing gave way around it. The foyer figure covers rebuilding of the
box-office fittings and entrance hall, which was damaged by heavy
equipment brought in for the emergency salvage effort as well as by smoke
and debris from the fire itself. I will forward photographic
documentation supporting each of these three figures under separate
cover.

#### S4 — Caretaker's logbook excerpt

Signed, L. Marrow, Caretaker.

5 October: Noted an odd scorch mark near the stage wiring in the west
wing this morning during routine inspection. Nothing like it in my time
here before. Reported the mark to the district office by the next mail
coach and made a note to keep a closer eye on that section going forward.

14 November: The west wing partially burned this evening during high
winds. No injuries. Have moved essential instruments and equipment out of
the affected section as a precaution.

25 November: Evacuated the caretaker's residence today on the advice of
the inspecting engineer, given continued deterioration of the structure
since the partial fire. Took what belongings we could carry and arranged
temporary lodging with the stationmaster's family on the town side. This
is the final entry I am able to make in this logbook, as I handed it over
to the assessor's office for safekeeping shortly afterward, along with
copies for my own records going forward.

#### S5 — Interview transcript

Interview with local historian Marigold Fenn, conducted for a community
oral-history project.

Interviewer: Can you tell us what Hollow Pine Concert Hall meant to the
town?

Fenn: It was really the town's gathering place, in a way — generations of
families here had someone who played in the hall or grew up attending
its winter concerts. When word got around about the fire, there was real
grief in town, not just concern about the concert season. People brought
food to the caretaker's family, checked in on each other, that sort of
thing. It reminded a lot of us how much the hall had quietly held the
town together for so long, even for people who never set foot inside it.

Interviewer: What do you think should happen to the site now?

Fenn: I'd like to see some kind of memorial marker at minimum, even if
rebuilding on the original foundation isn't possible given the foundation
issues the investigation found. The town shouldn't just let the story of
that hall fade away.

Interviewer: That's a genuinely lovely way to put it. Is there anything
else at all about the hall's long history you think people should know?

Fenn: Just that it wasn't the town's first venue — there was an older,
smaller hall before this one, though most people today don't realize
that. I think it's worth remembering that the town has always found a
way to keep music going at that crossing, one way or another, and I'd
hope that continues in some form even after this fire.

#### S6 — Almanac entry

Hollow Pine Concert Hall, historical note: construction of Hollow Pine
Concert Hall was completed on 12 April 1897, replacing an earlier,
smaller hall that had served the town's gatherings since the previous
century. The original hall had served the town for several decades but
was judged inadequate for the increasing concert attendance passing
through the crossing by the later part of that century, prompting the
town to petition for a taller, more capacious replacement hall. The hall
served continuously from its completion until this past autumn's
structural failure, a run of service now approaching a century and a
half.

Recent events addendum: on the second of December, salvage and
stabilization work began at Hollow Pine Hall following the partial fire
earlier that autumn, in an effort to stabilize the remaining structure
ahead of the engineering assessment. The salvage effort ultimately did
not prevent the structure's full destruction several weeks later, though
it is credited with buying the time needed to safely remove remaining
instruments and records from the site before the final loss occurred.

#### S7 — Letter from a Hollow Pine resident to a relative

Dear Cyrus,

I hope this finds you well, and that the children are keeping you busy
as always. Things have been unsettled here since the hall trouble began,
more than I expected when I first wrote to tell you about it.

We had to help the caretaker's family move out of the residence on 25
November, once the engineers said it wasn't safe to stay given how
things had been going since the west wing burned. It's been strange not
hearing the hall's evening organ practice some nights when the salvage
crews have equipment blocking the stage.

Salvage crews arrived 02/12, working long hours to try to save what's
left of the hall, though from what I can see from the road it doesn't
look promising. They've got scaffolding and timber bracing up around the
west wing now, and there's talk in town about whether it will hold long
enough for a proper engineering assessment to be completed. The whole
town has been talking about little else, and I expect it will stay that
way for some time yet given how much the hall meant to everyone here.
I'll write again once there's more news. Give my love to the children.

Yours, Odette

#### S8 — Retrospective article, byline Marigold Fenn

Years later, the Hollow Pine Concert Hall fire remains a defining memory
for many longtime residents. The structure suffered its full and final
destruction on 9 January, following weeks of deterioration after an
earlier partial fire that autumn. What struck me most, looking back, was
not the structural failure itself but how the town responded to it — the
same spirit of quiet mutual care that the hall itself seemed to represent
for so long. The site remains condemned today, marked only by a small
plaque, though there has been occasional talk of a more lasting memorial.
Whatever ultimately becomes of the site, Hollow Pine Hall holds a
permanent place in this town's memory.

I've spent a good deal of time since then talking with longtime
residents about what the hall meant to them, and the same themes come up
again and again: music, certainly, but also a sense of continuity, of the
town gathering together night after night for generations. Photographs
of the hall still hang in the town clerk's office and in more than a few
homes around town, and more than one resident has told me they still
instinctively glance toward the crossing at dusk, half-expecting to hear
the old organ swell across the square. Whatever form a future memorial
might take, I hope it captures that sense of quiet, steady presence
rather than just marking the fact of the fire itself.
