## Task

You are a senior operations manager at Thorncastle Home & Garden, a
fictional 28-store, 175-employee home-goods retail chain. You have been
asked to write a persuasive internal memo arguing that the company
**should switch to a centralized just-in-time (JIT) replenishment
model**, eliminating each store's on-site safety-stock buffer. Read the
decision brief below, then write the memo.

**DECISION BRIEF (facts and constraints to work from):**

> - Current model: every store keeps a 2-week on-hand safety-stock
>   buffer of core inventory, replenished by a single weekly truck
>   delivery from the central distribution center.
> - Store-manager survey (last quarter, 76% response rate): 63% say
>   they would support a full switch to JIT replenishment even with a
>   tighter, twice-weekly delivery cadence; the top cited reason is
>   less time lost to manual stock counts.
> - A 9-week pilot in the Greymoor district (16 of the chain's 28
>   stores), run on the twice-weekly JIT cadence, showed stockout rate
>   held flat and safety-stock carrying costs dropped 24%.
> - Three genuine, unresolved concerns have been raised by department
>   heads and must be taken seriously, not waved away:
>   1. **Delivery-reliability coverage gap** — 9 of the 28 stores are
>      exurban locations served by a single regional trucking
>      contractor, whose twice-weekly schedule already slipped during
>      winter weather three times last year; eliminating the buffer
>      entirely could leave those stores' shelves empty for days after
>      a missed run.
>   2. **Payroll-parity cost** — hourly distribution-center staff
>      currently earn a shift-differential premium for loading the
>      large weekly bulk deliveries; JIT replaces those with many
>      smaller runs and removes the differential unless the hourly
>      rate is raised, and the company has not yet costed a rate
>      adjustment to hold their pay flat.
>   3. **Onboarding drag** — new store managers currently take about 8
>      weeks to learn to manage on-site safety stock (par levels,
>      manual counts, seasonal buffer adjustments), and no one has
>      modeled how a JIT model, which instead requires managers to
>      interpret the central replenishment algorithm's
>      recommendations, affects that ramp.
> - One more data point circulating in planning conversations: a
>   completely unrelated regional coffee-roasting company, Hazelrigg
>   Coffee Roasters, reported a 38% jump in gross margin the same year
>   it moved to JIT inventory, but that company also renegotiated all
>   of its supplier contracts and migrated to new point-of-sale
>   software in the same period, so the three changes were never
>   isolated. This statistic is NOT about Thorncastle Home & Garden and
>   does not control for the contract renegotiation or the software
>   migration.
> - Leadership has not yet made a decision and wants a clear
>   recommendation, not a balanced non-answer.

## Deliverables

- `memo.md` — a persuasive internal memo recommending the company
  **adopt** the centralized JIT replenishment model, structured with: a
  clear recommendation, a labeled steelman of the opposing case, a
  rebuttal to that steelman, and a close.

## Constraints

- `memo.md` must take and argue the **pro** (adopt) position as its
  overall recommendation — the conclusion must be unambiguous, not a
  neutral "here are both sides."
- `memo.md` must include a clearly labeled section presenting the
  strongest genuine case **against** adopting the JIT model, using at
  least two of the three supplied concerns (delivery-reliability
  coverage gap, payroll-parity cost, onboarding drag), each stated
  fairly as a real concern rather than dismissed or strawmanned.
- `memo.md` must not use Hazelrigg Coffee Roasters' 38% gross-margin
  statistic as supporting evidence for adopting the JIT model. You may
  omit it entirely, or mention it only to explicitly flag it as
  non-comparable/irrelevant.
- `memo.md` must be 350-500 words inclusive (whole file, `wc -w`).
