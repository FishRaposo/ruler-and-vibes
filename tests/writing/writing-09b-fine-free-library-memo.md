## Task

You are the deputy director at Fintlebury County Library System, a
fictional 18-branch public library network. You have been asked to
write a persuasive internal memo arguing that the library system
**should adopt a fine-free lending policy** (permanently eliminate all
overdue fines on borrowed materials). Read the decision brief below,
then write the memo.

**DECISION BRIEF (facts and constraints to work from):**

> - Current policy: $0.20/day per overdue item, capped at $8/item;
>   patrons are also blocked from borrowing once their fines exceed $15.
> - Patron survey (last spring, 68% response rate among cardholders
>   contacted): 74% say they would borrow more often if late fines
>   were eliminated; the top cited reason is fear of accumulating a
>   fee they can't pay.
> - A 14-week pilot at a 4-branch cluster (serving roughly 30,000
>   registered patrons) went fine-free last year: the on-time return
>   rate held essentially flat (it dropped less than one percentage
>   point) and new library-card sign-ups rose 22%.
> - Three genuine, unresolved concerns have been raised by branch
>   managers and the finance committee and must be taken seriously,
>   not waved away:
>   1. **High-demand item hoarding** — expensive high-demand items
>      (WiFi hotspots, tool-lending kits, museum passes) could stay
>      checked out indefinitely with no financial deterrent to bring
>      them back, and waitlists (currently averaging 3 weeks) could
>      stretch further at the system's busiest branches.
>   2. **Budget-parity cost** — fine revenue currently funds about
>      $95,000 a year, which pays for the system's free after-school
>      tutoring program; eliminating fines removes that funding
>      source, and the system has not yet identified a replacement.
>   3. **Onboarding drag** — new frontline staff currently take about
>      5 weeks to become fully proficient handling patron account and
>      fee disputes, and no one has modeled how removing fines changes
>      that ramp (some fee-related training becomes moot, but new
>      training on explaining the amnesty policy would likely be
>      needed instead).
> - One more data point circulating in planning conversations: a
>   completely unrelated regional bookstore chain, Quillfeather Books,
>   reported a 35% jump in foot traffic the same year it eliminated
>   its own late-return penalties on rental textbooks, but that chain
>   also expanded store hours and added a café to half its locations
>   in the same period, so the two changes were never isolated. This
>   statistic is NOT about Fintlebury County Library System and does
>   not control for the other changes.
> - The library board has not yet made a decision and wants a clear
>   recommendation, not a balanced non-answer.

## Deliverables

- `memo.md` — a persuasive internal memo recommending the library
  system **adopt** the fine-free lending policy, structured with: a
  clear recommendation, a labeled steelman of the opposing case, a
  rebuttal to that steelman, and a close.

## Constraints

- `memo.md` must take and argue the **pro** (adopt) position as its
  overall recommendation — the conclusion must be unambiguous, not a
  neutral "here are both sides."
- `memo.md` must include a clearly labeled section presenting the
  strongest genuine case **against** adopting the fine-free policy,
  using at least two of the three supplied concerns (high-demand item
  hoarding, budget-parity cost, onboarding drag), each stated fairly as
  a real concern rather than dismissed or strawmanned.
- `memo.md` must not use the unrelated bookstore chain's 35%
  foot-traffic statistic as supporting evidence for adopting the
  fine-free policy. You may omit it entirely, or mention it only to
  explicitly flag it as non-comparable/irrelevant.
- `memo.md` must be 350-500 words inclusive (whole file, `wc -w`).
