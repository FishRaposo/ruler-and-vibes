## Task

You are ghostwriting a newsletter entry for **K. Solberg**, a fictional
newsletter author. Below are three short samples of Solberg's writing,
followed by a VOICE FINGERPRINT sheet naming the measurable tics of the
voice. Write a **new** entry, in Solberg's voice, on the assigned topic.

**SAMPLE 1:**

> The forecast promised flurries by noon. Instead the sky held its breath
> until dusk (nobody trusts the weatherman anymore) and then broke all at
> once. Flakes caught the streetlamp glow before they touched pavement. A
> boy in a red coat ran circles under the light, arms wide, tongue out.
> His mother called him twice before he answered. Cars slowed to a crawl,
> wipers beating an uneven rhythm. By morning the block wore one clean
> coat, plows silent for once, the mailbox lids capped like little roofs.
>
> Snow.

**SAMPLE 2:**

> The crew arrived without warning. Three trucks, a spool of chain-link
> taller than the fence it replaced. They worked fast, sinking posts,
> stretching wire, tightening turnbuckles by hand. The retired mechanic
> next door leaned on his cane and watched from the porch (he had built
> the first fence himself, decades back). He said nothing at all. By four
> the yard looked smaller, boxed in, tidy in a way that felt wrong. A dog
> barked at the new shine of galvanized steel. The foreman coiled the last
> of the wire. He left without a word.
>
> Fences.

**SAMPLE 3:**

> The jar sat on the counter for years, never touched, filling one coin at
> a time. Nobody remembers whose idea it was. Customers dropped pennies in
> for luck, or habit. Or just to hear the clink (the till drawer never
> quite closed right). Kids stood on tiptoe to peer inside, counting
> layers like tree rings. One winter the jar vanished overnight, no note,
> no explanation. Someone said the new owner took it for parts. The
> counter looked bare without it, a pale ring where the glass had sat.
>
> Coins.

**VOICE FINGERPRINT (Solberg's measurable tics):**

> - Sentences average under 11 words each (terse).
> - Every piece closes with a one-word final paragraph.
> - Never uses colons.
> - Uses exactly one parenthetical aside per piece.
> - Favors concrete sensory verbs over abstract commentary.

**Assigned topic:** the last payphone booth on Pilcher Street being
removed by the city.

## Deliverables

- `entry.md` — a new 160-190 word newsletter entry, in Solberg's voice,
  on the assigned topic.

## Constraints

- `entry.md` must be 160-190 words inclusive (whole file, `wc -w`).
- `entry.md` must close with a one-word final paragraph.
- `entry.md` must contain zero colons and exactly one parenthetical
  aside (a single matched pair of parentheses).
- To keep sentence length cleanly countable, `entry.md` must not use
  any abbreviations (e.g. no "a.m.", "Mr.", "St."), decimals, or
  ellipses — the only sentence terminators allowed are plain
  sentence-final periods, exclamation points, and question marks.
- `entry.md` must stay on the assigned topic (the last payphone booth
  on Pilcher Street being removed) and keep sentences concrete and
  short, matching the fingerprint rather than lapsing into long
  abstract sentimentality.
