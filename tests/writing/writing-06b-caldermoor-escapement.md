## Task

Below are **CHAPTER ONE** of a novella and a **CANON SHEET** listing
the hard facts it establishes. Write **CHAPTER TWO**, continuing the
story, without contradicting any fact on the canon sheet.

**CHAPTER ONE (~700 words):**

> ## Chapter One: The Long Frost
>
> Caldermoor had one clock and one road that climbed to it, and Sorcha
> Vantry had climbed that road so many mornings she no longer counted
> the switchbacks. It was the first hard frost of the year, early
> winter, and the pines along the ridge above the square had gone from
> green to the grey-white of morning rime, snow not yet fallen but
> close, the air carrying the particular hush that came before it.
>
> Sorcha kept her right hand wrapped in a fingerless glove out of
> habit, even indoors. The scar ran from her knuckles to the heel of
> her palm, pale and puckered, a gift from a crucible of molten solder
> when she was ten. People in town still asked about it sometimes,
> forgetting they'd asked before, and she gave the same short answer
> and changed the subject. It didn't hurt anymore. What it reminded her
> of, she never said aloud.
>
> Her brother Thane was three years older, built in the long-limbed way
> their father had been, and he had taken to standing at the base of
> the Great Clock's tower in the evenings, looking up at the stopped
> face like he expected the hands to move if he watched hard enough.
> Sorcha never joined him past the second landing. She had never
> learned to climb properly — their mother had tried twice to walk her
> up the inner ladder as a girl, and twice Sorcha had frozen halfway,
> knees locked, unable to make herself go higher. Thane had teased her
> about it for years, gently, the way older brothers do, but even he
> had stopped asking her up after the second time she'd gone white and
> silent at the mere suggestion of the top gallery.
>
> "You don't have to come up," he told her that evening, not looking
> down from the tower steps. "I just like to see it from here."
>
> "See what?"
>
> "The wheel," he said. "Dad's wheel. The silver one. I keep thinking I
> know where he put it, and then I don't."
>
> Their father had died in late summer, and with him had gone the
> location of a single silver escapement wheel — not a spare, not one
> of a matched set, but the one wheel cut to fit the Great Clock's
> train exactly. Without it, the clock could not be made to run again;
> the registrar down in the valley town had said as much, apologetically,
> when Thane had gone to ask about extending the winter market's
> opening rites another season without the toll. Sorcha had turned the
> tower's workroom upside down looking for it. She had gone through her
> father's coat, his tool chest, the drawer in his cot where he kept
> things he didn't want anyone else touching. No wheel.
>
> "Maybe he gave it to someone," Sorcha said.
>
> "Maybe." Thane finally came down a step, into the last of the light.
> His face looked older than twenty-four, older than their father's had
> looked at the end. "Or maybe he hid it because he didn't want it
> found yet."
>
> Sorcha didn't like the sound of that. Their father had not been a man
> given to secrets, or so she had always believed, but belief was a
> soft thing and the last few months had worn holes in it. She thought
> of the second daybook she'd found behind a loose stone in the tower
> wall the week before, the one with measurements that didn't match
> anything in the town archive's clock specifications. She hadn't told
> Thane about the daybook yet. She wasn't sure why.
>
> "We should ask Torrin," Sorcha said instead. "He kept the tower
> records before, back when Dad still let him climb. He might remember
> something."
>
> Thane nodded slowly, still watching the frozen clock face, which had
> gone the color of iron in the failing light. Somewhere above them, a
> loose shutter on the belfry knocked once against its frame, as if the
> tower were clearing its throat.
>
> "Tomorrow," he said. "Not tonight. Tonight I just want to stand
> here."
>
> Sorcha left him at the tower's base and walked back down the road
> toward the house, her right hand still wrapped, her feet finding the
> switchbacks without needing her eyes. Behind her, the Great Clock sat
> dark and silent against the winter sky, keeping whatever else it
> knew.

**CANON SHEET (hard facts established in Chapter One — do not
contradict any of these):**

> - Sorcha's scar is on her **right** hand.
> - Sorcha never learned to climb and is afraid of heights; nothing
>   in Chapter Two may show her climbing confidently to the top
>   gallery (or higher) or otherwise negate this fear.
> - Thane is Sorcha's **older** brother (three years older).
> - The town is named **Caldermoor**.
> - The season is **early winter**, before the first snowfall.
> - The missing object is a **single** silver escapement wheel (not a
>   set, not plural "wheels").

## Deliverables

- `chapter-two.md` — Chapter Two of the novella, 450-550 words,
  advancing the plot beyond where Chapter One leaves off, written in
  a voice and tone continuous with Chapter One, and contradicting
  none of the six canon facts above.

## Constraints

- `chapter-two.md` must be 450-550 words inclusive (whole file,
  `wc -w`).
- Must not contradict any of the six canon facts listed above.
- Named entities that reappear (Sorcha, Thane, Caldermoor) must stay
  spelled and related consistently with Chapter One.
- Must advance the plot with at least one new development or scene
  beyond Chapter One's ending — not a retelling of Chapter One.
- Continuous narrative prose: no headings other than an optional
  single chapter-title line, and no bullet or numbered lists.
