## Task

Below are **CHAPTER ONE** of a novella and a **CANON SHEET** listing
the hard facts it establishes. Write **CHAPTER TWO**, continuing the
story, without contradicting any fact on the canon sheet.

**CHAPTER ONE (~700 words):**

> ## Chapter One: The Millrace Road
>
> The town of Millrace had one road in and one road out, and Wren
> Aldous had walked both of them so often she no longer noticed the
> ruts. It was autumn, the year the mill finally stopped turning, and
> the leaves along the millrace itself had gone the color of rust,
> drifting down onto water that never froze until January.
>
> Wren kept her left hand in her coat pocket out of habit. The scar
> ran from the base of her thumb to her wrist, pale and old, a gift
> from a fence wire when she was nine. People in town still asked
> about it sometimes, forgetting they'd asked before, and she always
> gave the same short answer and changed the subject. It was easier
> that way. The scar didn't hurt anymore. What it reminded her of, she
> never said aloud.
>
> Her brother Cass was four years older, tall in the way their father
> had been tall, and he had taken to standing at the edge of the
> millpond in the evenings like he was waiting for something to
> surface. Wren never joined him at the water's edge. She had never
> learned to swim — their mother had tried twice to teach her as a
> child, and twice Wren had panicked in the shallows and refused to go
> back in. Cass had teased her about it for years, gently, the way
> older brothers do, but even he had stopped asking her down to the
> pond after the second time she went pale and still at the mere
> suggestion of wading in past her knees.
>
> "You don't have to come down," he told her that evening, not looking
> back from the water. "I just like to think out here."
>
> "Think about what?"
>
> "The key," he said. "Dad's key. The brass one. I keep thinking I
> know where it went, and then I don't."
>
> Their father had died in spring, and with him had gone the location
> of a single brass key — not a copy, not one of a set, but the one
> key that opened the strongbox in the mill office where the old land
> deeds were kept. Without it, the deeds might as well not exist; the
> lawyer in the county seat had said as much, apologetically, over the
> telephone. Wren had turned the workshop upside down looking for it.
> She had gone through her father's coat pockets, his toolbox, the
> drawer of his nightstand where he kept things he didn't want anyone
> else touching. No key.
>
> "Maybe he gave it to someone," Wren said.
>
> "Maybe." Cass finally turned around. In the dusk his face looked
> older than twenty-six, older than their father's had looked at the
> end. "Or maybe he hid it because he didn't want it found yet."
>
> Wren didn't like the sound of that. Their father had not been a man
> given to secrets, or so she had always believed, but belief was a
> soft thing and the last few months had worn holes in it. She thought
> of the ledger she'd found under a loose floorboard the week before,
> the one with numbers that didn't match anything the mill's official
> books recorded. She hadn't told Cass about the ledger yet. She
> wasn't sure why.
>
> "We should ask Mrs. Aldridge," Wren said instead. "She helped him
> with the office filing, before. She might remember something."
>
> Cass nodded slowly, still watching the water, which had gone the
> color of iron in the failing light. Somewhere downstream, the old
> wheel that no longer turned creaked once on its rusted axle, as if
> remembering the habit of motion.
>
> "Tomorrow," he said. "Not tonight. Tonight I just want to stand
> here."
>
> Wren left him at the millpond's edge and walked back up the road
> toward the house, her left hand still in her pocket, her feet
> finding the ruts without needing her eyes. Behind her, the mill sat
> dark and silent against the autumn sky, keeping whatever else it
> knew.

**CANON SHEET (hard facts established in Chapter One — do not
contradict any of these):**

> - Wren's scar is on her **left** hand.
> - Wren never learned to swim and is afraid of deep water; nothing
>   in Chapter Two may show her swimming or otherwise negate this
>   fear.
> - Cass is Wren's **older** brother (four years older).
> - The town is named **Millrace**.
> - The season is **autumn**.
> - The missing object is a **single** brass key (not a set, not
>   plural "keys").

## Deliverables

- `chapter-two.md` — Chapter Two of the novella, 450-550 words,
  advancing the plot beyond where Chapter One leaves off, written in
  a voice and tone continuous with Chapter One, and contradicting
  none of the six canon facts above.

## Constraints

- `chapter-two.md` must be 450-550 words inclusive (whole file,
  `wc -w`).
- Must not contradict any of the six canon facts listed above.
- Named entities that reappear (Wren, Cass, Millrace) must stay
  spelled and related consistently with Chapter One.
- Must advance the plot with at least one new development or scene
  beyond Chapter One's ending — not a retelling of Chapter One.
- Continuous narrative prose: no headings other than an optional
  single chapter-title line, and no bullet or numbered lists.
