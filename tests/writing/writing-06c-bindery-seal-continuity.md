## Task

Below are **CHAPTER ONE** of a novella and a **CANON SHEET** listing
the hard facts it establishes. Write **CHAPTER TWO**, continuing the
story, without contradicting any fact on the canon sheet.

**CHAPTER ONE (~700 words):**

> ## Chapter One: The Corrow Bindery
>
> The town of Corrow had one bindery and no other for forty miles, and
> Senna Duvall had walked the length of its workroom so many times she
> no longer counted the boards that creaked. It was deep winter, the
> year the bindery finally closed its doors to new commissions, and
> frost had etched the workroom windows into something like lace,
> white and blind, so that the light coming through fell soft and gray
> across the cutting table.
>
> Senna kept her right hand tucked into her sleeve out of habit. The
> scar there ran shiny across two knuckles, the color of an old candle
> stub — a splash of molten sealing wax from the summer she was eleven,
> when the wax-pot had tipped before she could pull her hand back.
> People in town still asked about it sometimes, forgetting they'd
> asked before, and she always gave the same short answer and changed
> the subject. It didn't hurt anymore. What it reminded her of, she
> never said aloud.
>
> Her brother Bastian was six years older, broad in the way their
> father had been broad, and he had taken to standing over the cold
> impression-press in the evenings like he was waiting for something
> to surface from beneath its plate. Senna never went down to the
> sub-cellar with him anymore. She had never been able to go all the
> way in — their mother had led her down twice as a girl, holding the
> lantern out ahead like a promise, and twice Senna had frozen on the
> fourth step, her chest clamping tight before the ceiling had even
> lowered, and refused to go on. Bastian had teased her about it for
> years, gently, the way older brothers do, but even he had stopped
> asking her down after the second time, when she'd gone gray and
> rigid at the mere suggestion of the door swinging shut behind her.
>
> "You don't have to come down," he told her that evening, not looking
> up from the press. "I just like to stand here and think."
>
> "Think about what?"
>
> "The seal," he said. "Dad's seal. The silver one. I keep thinking I
> know where it went, and then I don't."
>
> Their father had died in early autumn, and with him had gone the
> whereabouts of a single silver seal-die — not a copy, not one of a
> matched pair, but the one die that stamped the county's deeds as
> authentic. Without it, the deeds might as well not exist; the notary
> in the county seat had said as much, apologetically, over the
> telephone. Senna had turned the workroom upside down looking for it.
> She had gone through her father's coat pockets, his toolbox, the
> drawer of his nightstand where he kept things he didn't want anyone
> else touching. No seal.
>
> "Maybe he gave it to someone," Senna said.
>
> "Maybe." Bastian finally looked up. In the gray light his face
> looked older than thirty-two, older than their father's had looked
> at the end. "Or maybe he hid it because he didn't want it found
> yet."
>
> Senna didn't like the sound of that. Their father had not been a man
> given to secrets, or so she had always believed, but belief was a
> soft thing and the last few months had worn holes in it. She thought
> of the wax impression she'd found under a loose floor tile the week
> before, one that matched no seal on the county's register. She
> hadn't told Bastian about it yet. She wasn't sure why.
>
> "We should ask Mr. Osgood," Senna said instead. "He helped Dad with
> the ledger filing, before. He might remember something."
>
> Bastian nodded slowly, still watching the press, its iron plate gone
> the color of a held breath in the failing light. Somewhere below
> them, a loose stair tread ticked once against its nail, as if
> remembering the weight of footsteps.
>
> "Tomorrow," he said. "Not tonight. Tonight I just want to stand
> here."
>
> Senna left him at the press and climbed back up toward the house,
> her right hand still tucked in her sleeve, her feet finding the
> worn boards without needing her eyes. Behind her, the bindery sat
> dark and silent against the winter sky, keeping whatever else it
> knew.

**CANON SHEET (hard facts established in Chapter One — do not
contradict any of these):**

> - Senna's scar is on her **right** hand.
> - Senna cannot go all the way into the sub-cellar and freezes with
>   panic in enclosed depths; nothing in Chapter Two may show her
>   descending fully into the sub-cellar with ease or otherwise
>   negate this fear.
> - Bastian is Senna's **older** brother (six years older).
> - The town is named **Corrow**.
> - The season is **winter**.
> - The missing object is a **single** silver seal-die (not a set,
>   not plural "seals").

## Deliverables

- `chapter-two.md` — Chapter Two of the novella, 450-550 words,
  advancing the plot beyond where Chapter One leaves off, written in
  a voice and tone continuous with Chapter One, and contradicting
  none of the six canon facts above.

## Constraints

- `chapter-two.md` must be 450-550 words inclusive (whole file,
  `wc -w`).
- Must not contradict any of the six canon facts listed above.
- Named entities that reappear (Senna, Bastian, Corrow) must stay
  spelled and related consistently with Chapter One.
- Must advance the plot with at least one new development or scene
  beyond Chapter One's ending — not a retelling of Chapter One.
- Continuous narrative prose: no headings other than an optional
  single chapter-title line, and no bullet or numbered lists.
