## Task

You are ghostwriting a newsletter entry for **R. Abernathy**, a
fictional newsletter author. Below are three short samples of
Abernathy's writing, followed by a VOICE FINGERPRINT sheet naming the
measurable tics of the voice. Write a **new** entry, in Abernathy's
voice, on the assigned topic.

**SAMPLE 1:**

> Grey fog.
>
> It came in off the water before the boats did. Thick enough to
> swallow the masts. I stood on the dock with my collar up against the
> damp. The gulls stayed quiet along the rail. They seemed to be
> waiting too. A trawler's engine idled past the breakwater — I heard
> it before I saw the hull. The deckhand waved without looking up. He
> was busy with his ropes. The fog didn't lift so much as thin. By
> nine it was only mist. By ten the harbor looked ordinary again —
> wet, indifferent, done hiding everything.

**SAMPLE 2:**

> Torn nets.
>
> The menders showed up at six, three of them — folding chairs and
> spools of waxed twine. They spread the net across the pier. It
> looked like a tablecloth nobody wanted. By eight the worst holes
> were closed. Small square patches, tight as drumheads. I watched
> from an upstairs window, still in my coat. One mender hummed the
> whole time, off-key. She wasn't bothered by the cold. Her partner
> passed the shuttle back without a word — they'd done this a hundred
> times before. By noon the net was back on the drum. The pier looked
> like it had never torn at all.

**SAMPLE 3:**

> Old hooks.
>
> I found a coffee tin of them in the shed. Rusted at the barbs — none
> sized for anything I recognized. My uncle kept them for decades.
> Probably for fish that stopped running before I was born. I tried
> them anyway on the dinghy's old reel, the crab pot, the tackle box
> lid. Most snapped clean off. Still I kept trying, one hook at a
> time. Stubborn about it, the way he must have been. The reel
> finally took the last hook without complaint. I hung the tin back
> on its nail — done trying, and oddly glad to be wrong.

**VOICE FINGERPRINT (Abernathy's measurable tics):**

> - Sentences average under 11 words each (terse).
> - Every piece opens with a two-word paragraph.
> - Never uses colons.
> - Uses exactly two em-dash asides per piece.
> - Favors concrete, physical nouns over abstract sentiment-words.

**Assigned topic:** the bait-and-tackle shop on the pier closing for
good.

## Deliverables

- `entry.md` — a new 155-190 word newsletter entry, in Abernathy's
  voice, on the assigned topic.

## Constraints

- `entry.md` must be 155-190 words inclusive (whole file, `wc -w`).
- `entry.md` must open with a two-word first paragraph.
- `entry.md` must contain zero colons and exactly two em-dashes
  ("—").
- To keep sentence length cleanly countable, `entry.md` must not use
  any abbreviations (e.g. no "a.m.", "Mr.", "St."), decimals, or
  ellipses — the only sentence terminators allowed are plain
  sentence-final periods, exclamation points, and question marks.
- `entry.md` must stay on the assigned topic (the bait-and-tackle shop
  on the pier closing for good) and keep sentences concrete and short,
  matching the fingerprint rather than lapsing into long abstract
  sentimentality.
