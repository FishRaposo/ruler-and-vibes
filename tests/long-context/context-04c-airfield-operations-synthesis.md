### Fenmoor Aeromodellers Association — Model Aircraft and Drone Field Operations Code

Adopted by the Fenmoor Aeromodellers Association general meeting as
Standing Code No. 37, this code governs the authorization, certification,
and operation of member-flown model aircraft and drones at the
association's Skylark flying field, and establishes restrictions on flight
during declared field-condition levels. This document consolidates the
original code with Amendment 1 and Amendment 2, both adopted by subsequent
membership vote, and includes the fee schedule currently in effect at
Appendix A. Where a later amendment conflicts with an earlier provision,
the later amendment controls, as each amendment below states explicitly
which prior clauses it supersedes.

Fenmoor adopted this code after several near-miss incidents between
heavier airframes and other field users during busy weekends. The
association's stated goal in adopting the original code was to encourage
responsible flying of member aircraft at the Skylark field while
preserving the association's ability to impose stricter limits during
periods of hazardous conditions. The certification and rebate provisions
added by Amendment 1 and later revised by Amendment 2 were intended to
offset some of the upfront cost of the safety-certification course, in the
hope that a lower net field fee would encourage broader voluntary
certification across the association's flying membership.

This document is organized as follows: Section 1 defines key terms;
Section 2 sets out the authorization requirement; Section 3 establishes
the baseline flight restrictions tied to declared field-condition levels;
Section 4 lists the exceptions to those restrictions; Sections 5 and 6
present Amendment 1 and Amendment 2 respectively, each amendment stating
which earlier provisions it changes or supersedes; Appendix A sets out the
current fee schedule; and Sections 7 through 10 cover enforcement,
administration, the relationship of this code to other association rules,
and effective dates.

#### Section 1: Definitions

For purposes of this code, "model aircraft" means any member-flown fixed-
wing or rotary aircraft flown for sport or recreation at the Skylark
field, and "drone" means any member-flown multirotor or autonomous-capable
aircraft flown at the field; both are referred to collectively as an
"airframe" in this code. "Airframe" does not include free-flight balloons,
kites, or tethered aerostats, which are governed by separate association
recreation rules not addressed in this code, nor does it include any
full-scale crewed aircraft. "Field operation" means the launch, flight,
and recovery of an airframe at the Skylark field, and does not include
bench testing with the aircraft restrained on the ground, static display,
or simulator use, none of which are addressed by this code. "Certified
pilot" means a member who has completed the safety-certification course
described in Section 2 and holds a current certification card issued by
the association's safety officer.

This code applies to member-flown recreational operations at the Skylark
field. It does not apply to commercial, contract, or paid aerial work,
whose operations, if any, are governed by separate agreements not part of
this code.

"Field-condition level" means one of three escalating hazard
classifications declared by the safety officer under the association's
field-safety plan: green (mild hazard, advisory caution plus posted flight
windows), amber (moderate hazard, mandatory limits with narrow
exceptions), and red (severe hazard, mandatory limits with no exceptions).
"Takeoff weight" means the total ready-to-fly mass of an airframe
including battery and payload, expressed in kilograms for purposes of the
fee schedule in Appendix A. "Base fee" and "weight surcharge" have the
meanings given in Appendix A.

#### Section 2: Authorization Requirement

Every member flying an airframe at the Skylark field must obtain a
field-use authorization from the safety officer before the airframe
operates. The authorization application must include the airframe's
takeoff weight class, the intended flight area, and an attestation that
operations will comply with the restrictions in Section 3. No exception in
Section 4 waives this authorization requirement; the exceptions in Section
4 apply only to the flight-timing restrictions described in Section 3, not
to the requirement to obtain an authorization before an airframe operates.
A member operating an airframe without first obtaining an authorization
under this section is in violation of this code regardless of whether the
flight itself would otherwise have been permitted.

Authorization applications are reviewed within ten days of submission.
Once an authorization is issued, the member may separately apply for
certified-pilot status, described further in Amendment 1 and Amendment 2
below, which affects the applicable fee under Appendix A but is not a
substitute for the authorization itself.

The authorization review generally involves a paper review of the
submitted weight class and flight-area description; a field check is only
required if the safety officer identifies a potential conflict with
Section 3's restrictions or if the airframe is unusually heavy. Most
applications for a standard single airframe are approved on paper review
alone. An authorization, once issued, does not expire and does not need to
be renewed, though the member must notify the safety officer of any
material change to the airframe, such as an increase in takeoff weight
beyond the class originally declared in the application.

#### Section 3: Flight Restrictions

During any period in which the association has declared a field-condition
level under its field-safety plan, flight of airframes is restricted
according to the declared level. During condition green, flight is
permitted on the windows posted by the safety officer, typically limited
to designated hours of the day. During condition amber, flight is
prohibited entirely, except as specifically permitted under the exceptions
listed in Section 4 below. During condition red, all flight is prohibited
without exception, and Section 4's exceptions do not apply during a
declared red condition.

Outside of any declared field-condition level, flight of an airframe under
a valid authorization is allowed without time-of-day restriction, subject
to the general courtesy and noise provisions of the association's site
agreement, which are outside the scope of this document.

The safety officer publishes the current field-condition level, if any, on
the association's field-status board, updated whenever the level changes.
Members are responsible for checking the current level before flying;
ignorance of a declared level is not a defense to an enforcement action
under Section 7. Field-condition levels are declared based on wind
readings, visibility, and the crowding of the field, reviewed each
flying-day morning by the safety officer, and a level may be escalated or
de-escalated at any morning review or, in an emergency, on shorter notice
at the safety officer's discretion.

#### Section 4: Exceptions

The following exceptions apply only during a declared condition green or
amber, and only to the flight-timing restrictions in Section 3; none of
these exceptions waives the authorization requirement in Section 2.

(a) Line-of-sight hand-flown trainer flights. A member may fly a trainer
airframe during a declared condition green or amber, provided the flight
is flown continuously within unaided visual line of sight and hand-
controlled throughout by a person at the controls. This exception does not
extend to any flight flown under autonomous waypoint control, first-
person-view goggles without a visual observer, or any beyond-line-of-sight
method, even if that method is used only on a trainer airframe.

(b) Certified-instructor introductory sessions. A newly enrolled trainee
flying under a certified instructor may operate during its first sixty
days of enrollment, regardless of declared condition green or amber,
provided the instructor files an introductory-session notice with the
safety officer before flying begins under this exception.

(c) Emergency spotting use. An airframe may be flown without restriction
for emergency search-and-spot purposes at the direction of the site
warden, regardless of declared field-condition level.

No other exceptions to the Section 3 flight-timing restrictions exist
under this code as currently amended. In particular, no exception exists
for demonstration flights, air displays, or any use not specifically
described in subsections (a) through (c) above, and no exception exists
based on a member's travel or convenience alone; the exceptions in this
section are defined by the nature of the flight and, where applicable, the
method of control, not by the member's circumstances.

The safety officer may publish additional interpretive guidance clarifying
how these exceptions apply to specific control methods not explicitly named
above, such as return-to-home failsafes or stabilization aids, but any such
guidance must be consistent with the plain text of subsections (a) through
(c) and cannot itself create a new exception beyond what the membership has
adopted by code or amendment.

#### Section 5: Amendment 1

Amendment 1, adopted by the membership to encourage safety certification
of pilots, established the following provisions, effective as of its
adoption date:

(a) Certified-pilot rebate. A certified pilot, as defined in Section 1,
receives a 20% rebate off the combined base fee and weight surcharge
described in Appendix A.

(b) Scope clarification. Amendment 1 also clarified that the definition of
"airframe" in Section 1 includes fixed-wing gliders launched by hand or
bungee in addition to powered aircraft, resolving an ambiguity in the
original code text about whether unpowered gliders were covered.

Amendment 1's scope clarification in subsection (b) remains in effect and
has not been altered by any subsequent amendment. Its rebate provision in
subsection (a), however, is addressed by Amendment 2 below.

At the time of its adoption, Amendment 1's certification program was
projected to certify roughly one hundred fifty additional pilots over its
first two seasons, based on a poll of member interest taken by the safety
officer. Amendment 1 also directed the safety officer to report back to
the membership after one season on certification uptake and program cost,
which led directly to the budget review referenced in Amendment 2 below.

#### Section 6: Amendment 2

Amendment 2, adopted by the membership after a budget review of the safety
officer's certification-program costs, makes the following changes:

(a) Replacement of Amendment 1's rebate clause. Amendment 2 explicitly
replaces Amendment 1's certified-pilot rebate clause (Section 5,
subsection (a)) in its entirety. Effective as of Amendment 2's adoption
date, a certified pilot receives a 35% rebate off the combined base fee
and weight surcharge described in Appendix A, superseding the 20% figure
previously established by Amendment 1. This revised rebate applies to all
certified pilots as of Amendment 2's effective date, including pilots who
certified under Amendment 1's original terms before Amendment 2 was
adopted; Amendment 2 does not grandfather any pilot into the superseded
20% rate, and the 20% figure in Section 5(a) should be read as historical
only and is no longer in effect for any pilot as of this amendment.

(b) Scope clarification on shared airframes. Amendment 2 further clarifies
that a single airframe flown by multiple members of one club team (such as
a school team or a build group sharing one aircraft) is treated as one
airframe for purposes of the fee schedule in Appendix A; the applicable
fee depends on takeoff weight and certification status as described in
Appendix A, and does not vary based on the number of members who take
turns flying the airframe.

Amendment 2 does not alter the authorization requirement in Section 2, the
flight restrictions in Section 3, or the exceptions in Section 4.

The membership's stated rationale for increasing the rebate from 20% to
35% under Amendment 2, despite the budget review that prompted the
amendment, was that certification uptake following Amendment 1 had been
lower than the safety officer's original projection, and the membership
concluded that a larger rebate was a more effective incentive than the
smaller one originally set. The budget review's cost analysis showed that
the administrative cost of processing a certification was largely fixed
regardless of the rebate percentage applied, so increasing the rebate was
not expected to materially increase the office's per-certification
processing cost.

#### Appendix A: Fee Schedule

The field fee for operating an airframe is calculated as follows.

Every airframe pays a base fee of 48 currency units.

A weight surcharge of 22 currency units applies to any airframe with a
takeoff weight over 5 kilograms. Airframes at or under 5 kilograms pay no
weight surcharge.

For a certified pilot, as defined in Section 1, the combined total of the
base fee and any applicable weight surcharge is rebated at the rate
currently in effect under Amendment 2, Section 6(a) — a 35% rebate —
rather than the superseded 20% rate originally set by Amendment 1.

For example, a certified pilot flying an airframe over 5 kilograms pays a
base fee of 48 plus a weight surcharge of 22, for a combined subtotal of
70, rebated by 35% under the currently effective rate, yielding a final
fee. Fee amounts should always be calculated using the currently effective
rebate rate under Amendment 2 rather than any earlier rate that appears
elsewhere in this document for historical reference only.

Fees are payable in full at the time of authorization issuance and are not
prorated or refunded if a pilot later lets certification lapse or an
airframe is retired. The membership may adjust the base fee, surcharge, or
rebate rate by future amendment; any such change would be reflected in a
subsequent revision of this appendix and would supersede the figures above
in the same manner that Amendment 2 superseded Amendment 1's rebate rate.

The fee schedule applies uniformly across all airframe types within the
scope of this code, including single-pilot aircraft and shared team
aircraft flown by more than one member, per the clarification in Section
6(b) above. The safety officer accepts payment by cash, transfer, or the
association's online portal; partial payments are not accepted, and an
authorization application is not considered complete until the applicable
fee is paid in full. Fee amounts are reviewed by the safety officer each
season as part of the budget cycle, though no change to the base fee or
surcharge has been made since the original code's adoption; only the
certified-pilot rebate rate has changed, first by Amendment 1 and then by
Amendment 2 as described above.

#### Section 7: Enforcement and Penalties

A member found to be operating an airframe without an authorization, or
violating the flight restrictions of Section 3 without a qualifying
exception under Section 4, is subject to a warning for a first violation
and a suspension of field privileges for any subsequent violation within a
twelve-month period. Enforcement is carried out by the safety officer, who
may refer repeated or willful violations to the association's disciplinary
committee. This code does not authorize enforcement action against
activities not addressed by this code, such as balloon or kite flying,
static display, or commercial aerial work, none of which fall within the
scope of this code as defined in Section 1.

Enforcement actions under this section follow a standard process:
observation or complaint intake, a documented warning with a stated
corrective period for a first violation, and a privilege-suspension hearing
before the disciplinary committee for any subsequent violation within the
twelve-month look-back period described above. A member may appeal a
suspension to the disciplinary committee within thirty days of the
suspension notice. Nothing in this section limits the safety officer's
discretion to work informally with a member to bring an operation into
compliance before escalating to a formal enforcement action, and in
practice most first violations are resolved through the warning process
alone without proceeding to a suspension.

#### Section 8: Administration

The safety officer is responsible for administering the authorization and
certification process described in Sections 2, 5, and 6, for publishing the
currently effective fee schedule under Appendix A, and for declaring
field-condition levels under the association's field-safety plan referenced
in Section 3. The officer publishes an annual report summarizing
authorizations issued, pilots certified, and enforcement actions taken
under this code, which is presented to the membership each winter alongside
the association's budget request.

The safety officer also maintains a roster of certified pilots, searchable
by membership number, which team leaders may consult to confirm whether a
pilot's certification will apply to a shared airframe. Certified-pilot
status is tied to the individual member rather than to any airframe, so a
certified pilot who switches to a different airframe continues to receive
the rebate under Appendix A without needing to re-certify, provided the
certification card itself remains current.

Questions about whether a specific activity falls within the scope of this
code, including activities this code does not address, should be directed
to the safety officer for an administrative determination; this document is
not exhaustive of every possible field activity a member might undertake;
and where the code is silent, the safety officer may issue guidance, though
such guidance does not itself amend this code and any actual expansion of
scope requires a future amendment adopted by the membership in the same
manner as Amendment 1 and Amendment 2 above.

#### Section 9: Relationship to Other Association Rules

This code addresses only member-flown model aircraft and drone field
operations as defined in Section 1. Free-flight balloons, kites, and
tethered aerostats, including any lighter-than-air device flown for any
purpose at or near the field, are governed by a separate association
recreation rulebook and are not addressed by any provision of this
document. Commercial and contract aerial work, including any paid mapping,
inspection, or filming flight, is likewise governed by separate agreements
not addressed by this code. Members with questions about activities outside
the scope defined in Section 1 should consult the relevant separate rule or
contact the safety officer directly, since this document does not speak to
those situations one way or the other. This code does not attempt to
enumerate every field activity that falls outside its scope; the
definitions in Section 1 and the description of covered operations above
are the governing test for whether a given scenario falls within or outside
this code, not any illustrative list.

#### Section 10: Effective Dates

The original code took effect on the day of its adoption. Amendment 1 took
effect on its own adoption date, several seasons after the original code.
Amendment 2 took effect on its own adoption date, approximately one year
after Amendment 1. All provisions in this consolidated document reflect the
code as currently in effect, with historical rates and clauses that have
since been superseded retained in the text only for reference and
explicitly marked as superseded where applicable, as with Amendment 1's
rebate rate in Section 5(a).

This consolidated document is republished by the safety officer whenever a
new amendment is adopted, so that members, team leaders, and officers can
consult a single current text rather than needing to cross-reference the
original code against a separate list of amendments. Prior consolidated
versions, including the version published after Amendment 1 but before
Amendment 2, remain available in the association's records archive for
historical reference, but the version currently in effect is always the
most recently published consolidated text, which is this document.

## Scenarios

- **Scenario 1**: A member wants to fly a 3-kilogram trainer airframe at
  the Skylark field and does not hold safety certification. Is this
  permitted, and what fee applies?
- **Scenario 2**: A member flies a 6.5-kilogram airframe and holds current
  safety certification under the currently effective certification program.
  Is this permitted, and what fee applies?
- **Scenario 3**: During a declared condition amber, a member wants to fly
  a trainer airframe under autonomous waypoint control, relying on the
  line-of-sight hand-flown exception. Is this permitted?
- **Scenario 4**: A commercial aerial-survey operator wants to know whether
  this code permits paid mapping flights flown for a client at the field.
  Is this permitted?
- **Scenario 5**: A member wants to launch a free-flight helium weather
  balloon from the field. Is this permitted under this code?
- **Scenario 6**: A member certified under Amendment 1's original terms,
  before Amendment 2 was adopted, flies a 5.5-kilogram airframe. What
  rebate rate applies to them now that Amendment 2 is in effect?
- **Scenario 7**: A member operated an airframe without obtaining an
  authorization under Section 2, and now claims the situation is covered by
  a "temporary conditions hardship," pointing to the Section 4 exceptions.
  Is this permitted?
- **Scenario 8**: A school club team of 4 members shares a single
  certified-pilot-flown airframe with a 7-kilogram takeoff weight. Is this
  permitted, and what fee applies?
