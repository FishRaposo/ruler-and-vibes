### Greywater Mill — Location Shoot Diary

#### Shooting Day 1

Principal photography on *Greywater Mill* opened at Fairholme under a flat
grey sky that the director of photography, a meticulous, unhurried man
named Dashiell Okoro who had shot two prior period features on the same
estate over the past decade, declared "exactly the light the whole picture
wants." The first camera setup of the entire shoot, slated as the crew
called the first take of the morning, was logged on the continuity report
as slate SL-207A, marking the official start of principal photography on
this production. The morning ran cool and still, and the gaffer reported
the manor's east frontage holding its overcast even light beautifully for
the first several hours, a welcome change from the harsh, shifting sun that
had plagued the same location during the previous summer's reshoots.

At the first proper break, the production coordinator, Marlowe Ashby,
carried out the formal count of the wardrobe department's stock, a task she
performed methodically at the outset of every production regardless of how
many times the costume house's own paperwork had already listed the same
figures. Her count confirmed 246 wardrobe pieces logged across the two
costume trucks, matching the costume house's manifest exactly and giving
the production a clean baseline before the real work of the shoot began.
Marlowe noted in her own log that this baseline count would matter later,
since garment attrition against shooting days was one of the figures she
was responsible for tracking through the whole schedule, and any
discrepancy at the mid-shoot restock would need to be traced back to this
exact starting number.

The rest of Day 1 passed uneventfully by the standards of a multi-week
location shoot, though not without its small dramas. The unit completed a
modest number of setups by early afternoon, well short of the day's planned
target, after the B-camera developed an intermittent flicker on playback
that required a swap of the camera body under a sky that had by then
brightened just enough to worry the gaffer about continuity. Dashiell used
the delay to walk the manor's east lawn and study a stone doorway that had
caught his eye through the viewfinder, noting in his lighting log that the
texture looked consistent with a frontage he'd photographed on an earlier
production at a different estate entirely, though he was careful to flag
this as a passing impression rather than a firm match until he could line
the two up properly against his reference stills under better conditions
than a hurried camera swap allowed.

The unit wrapped a little later than planned, but in good spirits, with
Marlowe noting that the camera-body swap had cost them perhaps forty
minutes against the day's schedule, nothing serious this early in a
multi-week shoot. The first-night meal from the catering truck was the
traditional "first-slate stew," a recipe the unit's caterer, a wiry,
cheerful man named Halloran Steed, insisted on serving on the opening night
of every production he'd ever cooked for, regardless of how many times the
crew teased him about the superstition behind it. Halloran claimed the
tradition brought good luck to a shoot's opening days, and while nobody on
the crew seriously believed this, nobody was willing to be the one who
broke the tradition and blamed the camera flicker on it either.

#### Shooting Day 2

The second day began with a full mechanical check of both camera bodies,
standard practice after any playback issue the previous day, and the camera
department found nothing else of concern beyond the body that had already
been swapped out. The unit made good progress through a sequence of
interior setups that gradually shifted from the manor's bright morning room
to its darker, more cramped servants' passage, requiring far more careful
lighting than Day 1's relatively straightforward frontage work. Dashiell
spent much of the day walking the set with his light meter, building a
running log of exposure settings that he expected would form the backbone
of the production's eventual look-book, a document he was already mentally
drafting well before the shoot's midpoint, a habit he'd developed over many
years of feature work as a way of not losing the thread of a visual
argument he might not write up formally for months.

By the end of Day 2, the unit had completed a combined number of setups
that Marlowe recorded against the day's stock consumption figures, part of
her ongoing effort to build a reliable setups-per-day figure for this
specific crew under these specific location conditions, a number that would
help scheduling for future productions at similar estates. She noted that
the figure was still too early to be reliable, since a single day of
awkward interiors could skew it significantly compared to a longer averaged
figure, and she planned to revisit the calculation once more days of data
had accumulated across a wider variety of setup types.

The evening brought the crew's first proper equipment inventory since the
load-in, a routine Marlowe ran every second evening regardless of how
uneventful the intervening days had been, cross-checking each department's
gear against the master equipment list compiled back at the rental house
before the trucks ever rolled. She found everything accounted for, a small
relief given how easily kit could walk off or go missing during a busy
location shoot, and closed her log for the day with a note that the
production remained fully on schedule despite the previous day's camera
delay, the forty minutes lost having been more than recovered by Day 2's
smoother interior work.

#### Shooting Day 3

Day 3 brought the shoot's first major exterior sequence, a planned move
across the estate to film at a walled kitchen garden Dashiell had flagged
as visually significant during the production's prep phase, based on scout
photographs reviewed months earlier back at the production office. The move
added several hours to the day's schedule but was considered essential to
the picture's look, and Dashiell spent the bulk of the day lighting and
shooting across the garden's high walls, working methodically from one end
of the space to the other with a patience the rest of the crew had come to
expect from him on previous productions.

The rest of the unit used the extended setup to perform routine gear
maintenance and to reorganize the grip truck, whose contents had shifted
during the previous two days' constant repositioning, a task that took
longer than expected once the crew realized several sandbags and stands had
been loaded in an order that made the most frequently used ones the hardest
to reach. Marlowe used part of the day to cross-check the wardrobe count
against the garments actually in rotation, confirming the department was
tracking close to the planned attrition rate for this stage of the shoot,
with no significant deviation worth flagging in her log.

The evening wrap came earlier than the previous two nights, giving the crew
a rare chance for a proper sit-down meal from the catering truck rather
than the quick plates typical of a long shooting day. Halloran took the
opportunity to try out a spice combination he'd been wanting to attempt
since before the shoot, a small gamble that paid off well enough that two
of the junior crew asked him to write the recipe down for them before the
shoot ended, a request he promised to honor once he'd refined the
proportions a little further on a future attempt.

#### Shooting Day 4

The fourth day returned the unit to interior work in the manor's main hall
after the kitchen-garden move, and shooting resumed at a steadier pace
through a run of setups Dashiell described as broadly similar in lighting
to what the crew had built on Day 2. No major incidents were recorded,
though the unit did pause briefly when the dolly developed a faint squeal
on a slow tracking move, prompting a short stop to grease the track before
continuing; the noise cleared within minutes and no further issue was noted
for the remainder of the shoot with that dolly, much to the relief of the
key grip, who had spent the rest of the setup listening more closely to the
track than watching the frame.

Late in the day, the unit reached the point in the schedule set aside for
an extended stationary block at the manor's chapel wing, where they planned
to remain for two shooting days to allow a wider range of coverage around a
single fixed set rather than continuing to move the unit daily. Dashiell
was visibly pleased with the wing's range of usable angles within easy
reach, describing it in his log as "about as good a standing set as this
estate was ever going to offer," a judgment the rest of the crew took as
high praise given how rarely Dashiell allowed himself unqualified
enthusiasm about anything before a sequence had actually been shot in full.

Striking and re-rigging for the chapel-wing block took longer than a normal
company move, since the crew brought in the larger lighting rig used for
stationary blocks of more than one day, along with a proper covered cart to
keep sensitive camera gear out of the weather during the two days of fixed
work ahead. Marlowe oversaw the rig personally, having learned on a
previous shoot that an improperly weighted overhead rig could become a
serious hazard if the wind picked up through the estate's open cloister, an
experience she was in no hurry to repeat on this production or any future
one.

#### Shooting Day 5

With the unit stationary at the chapel wing, the crew split into two
smaller teams for the day, each covering a different set of angles around
the standing set. Dashiell led the first team on the wing's north-facing
coverage that he'd been eager to shoot since scouting it the previous
evening, while a second, smaller team under one of the junior crew,
Emberlyn Rooke, handled a wider but less flattering set of reverse angles
along the south wall, mostly to bank safety coverage for the edit rather
than in expectation of hero shots, a task Emberlyn approached with good
humor despite knowing it was unlikely to produce anything the director
would feature prominently in the cut.

Power and equipment management continued as a background task throughout
the stationary day, running alongside the coverage work rather than pausing
for it. The production's battery cart, used to keep the camera and monitor
batteries charged and to power the standing set's practical lighting
overnight, was checked and topped off during the afternoon by Marlowe
personally, with the charge meter reading recorded at 84 amp-hours
remaining on the cart's main bank after the top-off, a figure she logged
carefully since the battery cart's supply was separate from the manor's
mains power and needed independent tracking against the days remaining
before the production's mid-shoot equipment resupply. She noted that this
reading, taken here at the chapel wing, would be her reference point for
calculating the cart's drain rate over the following stretch of the shoot,
a calculation she planned to revisit once the unit had moved on and the
cart had run for several more consistent days without a top-off to distort
the figures.

The two coverage teams reconvened at the unit base well after dark, both
reporting a productive if unremarkable day. Emberlyn's south-wall team had,
as expected, banked mostly safety coverage, though one of her crew had
spotted an old, weathered set-dressing crate of unknown origin tucked in
the chapel wing's undercroft that nobody on the production could
immediately place against any of the art department's own paperwork, a
small mystery Emberlyn noted in her log mostly out of curiosity rather than
any expectation it would matter to the shoot.

#### Shooting Day 6

The second day at the chapel wing continued the coverage work from Day 5,
with the two teams largely swapping walls so that Emberlyn's team could
shoot the north-facing angles Dashiell's team had covered the previous day,
while Dashiell himself pushed into a tighter set of the undercroft angles
than the previous day's south team had attempted, curious about the
weathered crate Emberlyn had mentioned the night before. The exchange
proved useful: Emberlyn's team caught a small but striking shaft of window
light on the north wall that Dashiell's team had apparently shot straight
past without exploiting the previous day, a detail Dashiell was
good-humored about when it was pointed out to him back at base that
evening, admitting that a second eye on the same set was exactly why the
crew rotated coverage between stationary days in the first place.

The production's sound recordist, a quiet man named Thackeray who had
joined the unit specifically for this feature after two previous pictures
with a different production company, maintained the scheduled radio
check-in with the production office as he did every evening, reporting the
unit's status and general progress without incident. He noted in his own
log that the estate's stone walls had made the wireless comms unusually
clean that evening, a small detail he recorded mostly out of habit, since
signal conditions varied enough from set to set that tracking them
occasionally proved useful for planning future shoots' wireless-mic setups,
particularly for productions in stone buildings with similarly awkward
radio-frequency shadowing.

Marlowe also logged a second battery-cart check this same evening, a
follow-up to the previous day's top-off now that the cart had run for a
full day powering the standing set's practicals and charging batteries
overnight. The meter read 85 amp-hours, a small increase Marlowe attributed
to a slightly imprecise meter reading rather than any real change, since the
cart had not been recharged between the two checks; she noted the apparent
rise as an example of the meter's normal drift rather than a meaningful
discrepancy worth investigating further, and used the previous day's figure
as her authoritative reference point for the drain-rate calculation going
forward.

Dashiell, for his part, spent a good portion of the evening studying the
weathered undercroft crate the south team had found, cautiously optimistic
that its aged timber might be worth dressing into a background of the
following day's final chapel setup before the unit struck the wing, a plan
he ran past the rest of the crew over dinner and which was met with general
enthusiasm, since it meant one more shot at a standing set everyone had, by
this point, grown rather fond of.

#### Shooting Day 7

The unit struck the chapel wing on the morning of Day 7, resuming its move
through the shooting schedule after two productive stationary days, though
not before Dashiell spent a final hour at first light shooting the extra
undercroft-crate insert he had discussed the previous evening, a decision
that delayed the company move only slightly and which nobody seriously
objected to given how well the aged timber read on camera in the morning
light. Before the move, the production's assistant-director structure was
formally set down for the first time in this diary, though the role had of
course been filled since Day 1: the first assistant director on this
production, tasked with running the set floor and calling the shooting
order each day, is Solveig Hartmann, working alongside the second assistant
director's duties in a combined AD structure the production's staffing plan
had assigned to the two of them jointly. Solveig's specific
responsibility, as distinct from the second AD Corwin Ellery's
paperwork-focused work on call sheets and cast wrangling, covered floor
management, shooting-order calls, and liaison with the director and the
camera department, a division of labor the two of them had worked out
between themselves during the prep days before the shoot ever began.

Shooting on Day 7 was uneventful, with the unit making solid progress
through a run of setups that Dashiell noted matched the scout photographs
closely, a small reassurance given how often scout stills and the real set
had diverged on previous productions at similarly rambling estates,
sometimes badly enough to force a last-minute relight when a photographed
angle turned out to have changed entirely since the scout was shot. The
evening wrap was a simple one, called quickly to make the most of the
remaining daylight for a final gear check before what the crew expected to
be a more demanding stretch of shooting over the following two days, based
on the cramped interiors flagged in the schedule for the section
immediately ahead.

Halloran, meanwhile, had begun quietly rationing the more interesting
ingredients in the catering truck's supply, having realized partway through
the shoot that his earlier experimenting on Day 3 had used more of one
particular spice than he'd budgeted for the whole production, a minor
logistical slip he was determined to manage discreetly rather than admit to
the crew and risk becoming a running joke for the remainder of the shoot.

#### Shooting Day 8

Day 8 lived up to the previous evening's expectation, with the unit shooting
a sequence in the manor's cramped cellar passages that required careful,
slow rigging and, on two occasions, brief manual clearing of stored estate
junk that had to be shifted out of frame before each setup. Progress was
slower than any previous day of the shoot, and Marlowe's running
setups-per-day figures showed a noticeable dip for the day, consistent with
the additional rigging the cramped space demanded, a figure she noted
without particular alarm since the production's scheduling had built in a
margin for exactly this kind of demanding stretch.

Dashiell, operating the camera himself for this stretch rather than handing
it to the operator as he often did on easier setups, used the slower pace to
record detailed lighting notes on the cellar's awkward stone surfaces,
building out his look-book even without the chance to light each corner as
fully as he'd have liked. He remarked that the day's slow going at least
gave him an unusually thorough study of a set he might otherwise have lit
too quickly to document well, a small silver lining he was quick to point
out to the rest of the crew once it became clear the day's schedule was
slipping regardless of anyone's efforts to keep pace with the original plan.

By evening, the unit had shot perhaps two-thirds of the cellar sequence that
the schedule had flagged as a likely trouble spot, and Marlowe's log for the
day noted the crew's intention to push through the remainder early the
following morning while the camera team was fresh, rather than risk the same
slow, high-attention rigging late in the day when fatigue might turn a
manageable setup into something more serious.

The evening wrap that night was a subdued one, the whole crew noticeably
more tired than on any previous evening of the shoot, and conversation over
the catering plates ran shorter than usual before people began drifting off
to their trailers. Halloran kept the meal simple and quick to serve, sensing
correctly that nobody had the energy for anything more ambitious after a day
spent alternately rigging in a cramped cellar and hauling estate junk out of
frame in the close air. Thackeray's evening check-in with the production
office was likewise brief, limited to the essential status update rather
than the more detailed exchange that had characterized some of the smoother
earlier evenings, and he noted in his own log that the office coordinator on
duty that night seemed to sense the crew's fatigue and kept the exchange
efficient without any unnecessary chatter.

Emberlyn, despite the long day, used a few minutes before turning in to
check the camera gear stored from the Day 3 and Day 5-6 blocks, making sure
nothing had been knocked loose or damaged during the day's constant
rigging, a precaution she'd taken to heart after hearing about a previous
production that had lost a day to a cracked lens element nobody caught until
it showed up in the rushes. She found everything secure and turned in
herself shortly after, glad the following day's remaining cellar work was
expected to be shorter than the stretch just completed.

#### Shooting Day 9

The cellar sequence continued into the morning of Day 9 before finally
giving way to easier main-hall work by midday, much to the relief of the
camera team who had spent two full days rigging carefully around stored junk
and low stone ceilings. The unit made up some of the lost time once the
setups eased, though Marlowe noted in her log that the two slow days had
eaten meaningfully into the schedule buffer the production had built in
during prep, a buffer she'd been quietly tracking since Day 1 for exactly
this kind of contingency, and one she was now watching more closely than she
had at any earlier point in the shoot.

It was on this day, the 14th of Hearthfall by the production's own in-story
calendar convention (a fictional calendar the property department used
consistently on the picture's dressed set pieces and dated props throughout
this shoot), that the crew recovered an item of note while clearing the
cellar's last corner: a piece of camera equipment, evidently left behind by
an earlier production that had shot at the estate, its equipment tag still
legible despite years of dust. The recovered unit, an old prime lens, was
logged under its engraved serial, LN-53109-C, and Dashiell noted with
interest that the model matched glass used by a rental house that had, as
far as he knew, not supplied this region for a considerable number of years,
making the find something of a small mystery the crew did not attempt to
resolve further, beyond noting it for the production's wrap report as a
curiosity rather than a usable piece of kit.

The rest of Day 9 passed without further incident, the unit settling back
into the steady rhythm of shooting that had characterized the earlier,
easier stretches of the schedule before the cellar sequence disrupted it.
Thackeray's evening check-in with the production office reported the lens
recovery as a minor item of interest alongside the routine status update,
more out of thoroughness than any expectation the office would have
follow-up questions about a decades-old piece of abandoned glass from an
unrelated production.

The afternoon's easier work also gave the key grip a chance to swap dolly
track between two of the manor's main sets, a routine maintenance task that
had been on Marlowe's schedule since before the load-in but which the
cellar's cramped conditions had made impractical to carry out safely
earlier. The swap went smoothly, and the grip reported all the track in good
working order afterward, with no wear severe enough to cause concern before
the production's next scheduled gear service back at the rental house
following the wrap. Halloran used the same easier stretch to restock the
catering truck's water supply from one of the estate's larger tanks, topping
off the smaller containers used for daily service so that the following
morning's breakfast would not be delayed by a supply run first thing.

Dashiell spent a good part of the evening turning the recovered lens over in
his hands by lamplight, comparing its barrel markings and mount against a
reference catalog of older cinema glass he happened to carry in his personal
kit, a habit some of the younger crew found endearing and others found
faintly excessive given how unlikely it was that the exercise would produce
any conclusion beyond what he'd already noted in the log. He eventually
satisfied himself that the lens was, as he'd first suspected, a model
retired from production well before the shoot's youngest crew member had even
been born, and packed it carefully away for the trip back to the rental
house, where he thought a colleague who collected vintage cinema optics
might find it a mildly interesting addition to a small shelf of similar
curiosities gathered over the years.

Emberlyn, meanwhile, used the quiet evening to get a head start on
organizing the camera report for the stretch of shooting just completed,
cross-referencing setup numbers against the continuity log so that the
eventual edit would be able to place every take precisely against the
shooting script rather than relying on rougher day-by-day notes. It was
tedious work, and she admitted in her own log that she found it considerably
less exciting than the shooting itself, but necessary all the same for a
report that the post-production team would expect to hold up to scrutiny
from an editor who might want to revisit specific takes weeks later.

Marlowe closed the day's log with a brief note on morale, observing that the
crew seemed to have shaken off the fatigue of the cellar sequence more
quickly than she'd expected, helped along by the small novelty of the lens
recovery and the general relief of easier setups ahead. She also used the
evening to recalculate her running setups-per-day figures now that a full
stretch of both difficult and easy work had been logged, arriving at a
blended figure she considered far more reliable than the early, single-day
estimates from Day 2, and which she planned to use for the remainder of the
production's scheduling rather than continuing to refine it further unless
conditions changed significantly.

Thackeray, before turning in, ran a final check on the wireless-mic gear
ahead of the following day's shooting, a habit he kept up regardless of how
routine the previous several check-ins had been, reasoning that the cost of
a quick nightly check was trivial compared to the trouble an undetected
fault could cause if it went unnoticed until it ruined a take. He found
nothing amiss, logged the check as routine, and noted that the production was
now more than halfway through its planned shooting schedule by his own rough
count of days shot against days remaining, a small milestone he mentioned to
Solveig before they both turned in for the night.

#### Shooting Day 10

With the cellar sequence fully behind them, Day 10 saw the unit make strong
progress through a run of wide, well-lit main-hall setups, the best shooting
conditions of the shoot so far according to the camera operator, who had
grown visibly more relaxed after two grinding days rigging in the cramped
cellar. Dashiell used the smoother going to catch up on lighting notes he'd
deferred during the slower, more demanding days, cross-referencing his
cellar exposure readings from Day 8 against fresh readings taken during a
brief relight midday, satisfying himself that the rushed cellar notes had
held up well against direct comparison once he finally had the chance to
check them properly.

The evening check-in, handled jointly by Thackeray and the office as had
become routine since Day 7, reported the unit's status without incident, and
Thackeray noted in his own log that this was the cleanest wireless check-in
of the shoot so far, with no repeated transmissions needed to get a clear
signal through, unlike several earlier evenings when the estate's stone
walls or the valley's terrain had required a second attempt before the
message went through cleanly.

Halloran marked the smoother day with a slightly more ambitious dinner than
the shoot's usual working-day fare, drawing on the spice supply he'd been
quietly rationing since Day 7 a little more freely than his self-imposed
budget technically allowed, reasoning that morale mattered enough after the
previous two demanding days to justify the small indulgence, a decision the
rest of the crew appreciated without ever learning about the rationing that
had preceded it.

Emberlyn spent part of the smoother afternoon compiling her own running
notes from the two stationary days at the chapel wing, cross-referencing her
angle sketches of the undercroft against Dashiell's more detailed lighting
notes to make sure the two records agreed on the set's usable coverage. She
found a minor discrepancy in her own pacing measurements of the undercroft,
off by what she estimated was no more than a few feet, and corrected her
sketch accordingly rather than let the small error propagate into the
production's continuity records. Dashiell, when shown the correction, agreed
it was a sensible fix and thanked Emberlyn for catching it before the notes
were finalized, remarking that this kind of quiet cross-checking between crew
members was exactly why the production's records tended to hold up well under
later scrutiny in the edit.

The camera department's mechanical condition remained a running concern for
the operator despite the smoother setups, and he used part of the
afternoon's easier going to listen carefully for any return of the faint
dolly squeal that had cropped up briefly back on Day 4. He reported nothing
of concern by evening, noting that the dolly had performed without complaint
through both the Day 8-9 cellar sequence and the smoother work since, and
that whatever had caused the brief squeal on Day 4 appeared to have been a
one-time issue with a dry track rather than a developing fault worth flagging
for the rental house's service crew ahead of the next production.

Thackeray used part of the evening, after the joint check-in with the office,
to perform routine maintenance on the wireless-mic gear itself, cleaning a
connector that had been giving him a faint intermittent crackle on receive
for the past day or two. The fix seemed to resolve the issue entirely, and he
noted with satisfaction that the sound gear had given the production no
serious trouble at all across the ten days since the load-in, a record he
attributed partly to careful maintenance and partly to simple good fortune,
since location sound gear could be unpredictable even with the best care.

Marlowe closed out Day 10's log with a broader review of the production's
progress against the original schedule, noting that while the Day 8-9 cellar
sequence had cost real time, the smoother shooting since had recovered a
meaningful portion of it, and that the buffer she'd been protecting since Day
1 remained intact enough that she was no longer worried about the shoot's
ability to reach the last scheduled setup and still make the planned
picture-wrap date without requesting extra days from the studio.

#### Shooting Day 11

Day 11 brought the shoot's second major exterior move, this time to a
disused watermill on the estate's southern boundary that Dashiell considered
second in importance only to the Day 3 kitchen garden for the picture's
overall look. The move required careful company logistics across estate
ground with no proper vehicle access at all, relying entirely on the grip
department's careful planning and a good deal of hand-carrying of gear, a slow
and exacting process that tested the whole crew's patience more than any
single interior sequence at the manor had managed to.

The unit reached the watermill by early afternoon and spent the remainder of
daylight on setups and coverage, with Dashiell visibly energized by what he
called "the best exterior I've lit on this whole picture" in his log entry for
the day, a claim that prompted some good-natured skepticism from Emberlyn
given how many times Dashiell had said something similar about earlier
locations, though even Emberlyn admitted after seeing the mill in the
afternoon light that this one seemed to genuinely deserve the praise.

Marlowe, meanwhile, continued her routine tracking of wardrobe and gear,
noting no significant deviations from the production's planned attrition curve
despite the demanding off-vehicle move, a testament, she wrote, to the buffer
built into the original planning figures even after the schedule pressure
introduced by the Day 8-9 cellar sequence. She also used the evening to plan
the following day's return to the main unit base, working out the most
efficient route back given how far off the estate's proper roads the mill
location had taken the crew.

#### Shooting Day 12

Returning to the main unit base after the Day 11 watermill move, the unit
reached a staging point the shooting schedule had flagged well in advance as
a key reference for the production's back half: the company-move checkpoint
logged as 63-K12-N, recorded as the unit passed it in the early afternoon.
This checkpoint marked, per the original schedule reviewed by the whole crew
before the load-in, the approximate three-quarter point of the shoot's
planned setup count, with a final push toward the last scheduled setup still
to come over the following days.

The crew also marked, from this checkpoint, a measured distance back to the
kitchen garden shot on Day 3, using the production's location odometer
cross-referenced against the transport logs from both days: the distance from
the Day 3 kitchen garden to this checkpoint was recorded as 37.9 km along the
traveled estate route, a figure Marlowe noted primarily for the production's
final location-summary map rather than for any operational purpose during the
remaining schedule. She double-checked the figure against a second odometer
reading before logging it, a habit she'd developed after a discrepancy on a
previous production had cost the transport team an afternoon of confused
back-and-forth.

The evening at this checkpoint was a relatively relaxed one, with the crew
taking stock of how many setups remained and Dashiell reviewing his full
coverage so far against his original shot list, satisfied that the shoot was
on track to meet its creative goals even after the schedule pressure of the
cellar sequence earlier in the shoot.

#### Shooting Day 13

Day 13 continued the push toward the last scheduled setup, with shooting
conditions remaining favorable and no incidents of note beyond a brief stop to
help a location van from a different, unrelated production the unit happened to
pass on the estate's shared access road, exchanging brief pleasantries and a
rough condition report for the ground each crew had already covered, a small
and pleasant surprise given how rarely the estate saw any other production
traffic at all during this season. Dashiell used the day's otherwise
uneventful shooting to finalize his notes from the Day 11 watermill move,
cross-referencing setup numbers against his lighting sketches before the
details faded from memory, a task he preferred to complete within a few days
of shooting whenever the schedule allowed it.

Elsewhere in the day's records, the crew logged a separate distance
measurement unrelated to the Day 12 figure: the distance from a different
reference point, an old estate gatehouse noted on the original location maps,
