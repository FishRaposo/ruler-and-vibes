### Duskholt Observatory Consortium — Second Quarter 2026 Operations Report

Period covered: April 1 - June 30, 2026. Prepared by the Office of
Instrument Operations for the consortium council and the regional
research-funding authority. This report summarizes instrument status,
maintenance activity, observing-time efficiency, data yield, safety
incidents, staff assignments, and capital investment across the Duskholt
Observatory Consortium for the second quarter of 2026. It is intended as
the primary quarterly reference document for council members, observing
schedulers, and the funding authority's oversight staff, and it is
cross-referenced by the annual science report published each December.

#### Section 1: Overview

The Duskholt Observatory Consortium operates a network of shared research
telescopes and observing stations sited across the ridgelines above
Duskholt, a high dark-sky reserve separating the lowland town from the
outlying stations at Wend Hollow and Ravenglass Flat. The consortium was
established in 1994 as a single-instrument facility running one modest
reflecting telescope on the main ridge; it has since grown into the
largest shared observing network in the region, serving both university
research programs and visiting observers across a network that now spans
five principal observing stations and several seasonal survey campaigns.

As of the close of the second quarter, the consortium's active
inventory consists of 16 active instruments, ranging from small
wide-field survey cameras to large reflecting telescopes fitted with
high-resolution spectrographs. Of these, 13 instruments were in
continuous scheduled operation throughout the quarter, two underwent
scheduled optics recoating for part of the quarter, and one remained
offline pending a drive-motor overhaul that is expected to conclude early
in the third quarter. Instrument planners note that this level of
availability is consistent with the consortium's long-term maintenance
rotation, under which no more than three instruments are expected to be
out of scheduled operation at any one time during a normal quarter.

The overview below covers the instrument roster, maintenance,
observing-time efficiency, data yield, safety, staff, and capital
sections in turn, followed by two appendices and supporting footnotes.
Readers preparing summary materials from this report should take
particular care with the maintenance-spend figure in Section 3, which is
superseded by a corrigendum in Appendix B, and with the two similarly
named instruments described in Section 2.

The quarter was broadly stable operationally, with weather-related
downtime below the five-year seasonal average and no unplanned extended
station closures. Cloud activity in April was limited to two nights of
reduced-schedule operation on the more exposed north ridge, and no
station required a full-night closure at any point in the quarter. The
most notable operational event of the quarter was a corrigendum to the
previously circulated maintenance-spend figure, detailed in Appendix B
below, which readers should treat as authoritative over the figure
printed in Section 3. The finance office has asked that all downstream
materials, including council packets and the funding authority's
quarterly filing, cite the corrigendum's figure rather than the original
Section 3 number.

#### Section 2: Instrument Roster

The instrument roster below lists each instrument currently registered to
the Duskholt Observatory Consortium, along with its primary-mirror
aperture and home station. Several instruments carry similar names
inherited from the historic naming convention, under which all
instruments are named for ridgeline features in the reserve, and readers
should take care to distinguish between them, particularly where two
names differ only by a suffix.

The instrument Kelmarsh Rise, a large reflecting telescope sited at the
north-ridge station, has a rated primary-mirror aperture of 340
centimeters. It is one of the two largest instruments in the consortium
and was commissioned in 2013 as part of an instrument-modernization
program that also introduced the Tovar Array. Kelmarsh Rise's aperture
reflects an optical configuration optimized for deep imaging over a
narrow field, and it is the instrument most commonly assigned to the
primary deep-survey program during the dark-sky observing season.

The instrument Kelmarsh Ridge, a smaller wide-field survey telescope
sited at the Ravenglass Flat station, has a rated primary-mirror aperture
of 372 centimeters. Despite the similarity in name to Kelmarsh Rise,
Kelmarsh Ridge is a distinct instrument built for a different observing
profile, with a fast focal ratio suited to wide survey mosaics over the
low-elevation sky at Ravenglass Flat, and it carries no deep-imaging
spectrograph at all. Scheduling materials sometimes abbreviate both
instrument names to "Kelmarsh" internally, which has occasionally caused
confusion in informal correspondence; this report always uses the full
instrument name to avoid that ambiguity.

Other consortium instruments include the Ostara Camera (aperture 210,
Wend Hollow station), the Fenwick Scope (aperture 155, Ravenglass Flat
secondary program), the Tovar Array (aperture 300, north-ridge secondary
program), the Brindmoor Survey (aperture 120, seasonal campaign use), and
the Halvers Reflector, a large reflecting telescope serving the
cross-reserve transient-monitoring program.[^1] The Halvers Reflector was
the second instrument commissioned under the modernization program
mentioned above and entered service one year after Kelmarsh Rise. The
remaining nine instruments in the inventory are smaller auxiliary cameras
assigned to monitoring programs not separately itemized in this report;
their combined aperture is tracked in the internal instrument database
rather than in quarterly filings.

Each instrument undergoes an annual optical certification and quarterly
alignment inspection; results of the second-quarter inspections are
summarized in Section 6 below. No instrument failed its second-quarter
inspection outright, though two instruments received minor
corrective-action notices unrelated to the incidents described in
Section 6.

The roster is reviewed annually by the council's instrument committee,
which this year recommended no additions or decommissionings for the
coming fiscal year. The committee's report noted that the inventory's
average age remains within the target range set by the ten-year
instrument renewal plan adopted several years ago, and that no instrument
currently in service is expected to require replacement before the plan's
next scheduled review. The committee also noted that naming continuity,
including the pairing of similarly named instruments such as Kelmarsh
Rise and Kelmarsh Ridge, is a deliberate historical convention rather
than an oversight, and asked that future reports continue to spell out
full instrument names in all council-facing materials to avoid ambiguity
in correspondence and in insurance filings.

#### Section 3: Maintenance

Scheduled maintenance activity during the second quarter included two
optics recoatings, four routine drive services, and one unscheduled
mount repair following a minor hoist mishap at the Wend Hollow station in
May. Total scheduled maintenance spend for the quarter, as originally
tabulated by the maintenance office at the close of the quarter, was
$52,400. This figure covered parts, contracted labor, and coating-lab
facility fees across all maintenance events during the period, and it was
the figure presented at the initial internal budget review meeting held
in the first week of July.

Readers should note that this Section 3 figure was subsequently found to
omit a late-arriving invoice from a subcontractor; see the corrigendum in
Appendix B, which supersedes the figure above and should be treated as
the authoritative quarterly maintenance-spend total for all reporting
purposes going forward.

The unscheduled mount repair, the only non-routine maintenance event of
the quarter, was completed within seven days and did not require the
affected instrument to be taken offline outside its normal daytime
maintenance window. The two scheduled optics recoatings each took
approximately three weeks and were completed on the maintenance schedule
set at the start of the year. The four routine drive services were
completed within their normal single-day maintenance windows and did not
affect the schedule.

#### Section 4: Observing-Time Efficiency

Network-wide observing-time efficiency for the second quarter was 89.6%,
measured as the share of scheduled observing minutes actually recorded on
sky across all stations and instruments. This represents a modest
improvement over the same quarter last year, driven largely by improved
dome-sequencing procedures introduced in the first quarter, and it
continues a gradual upward trend that instrument operations attributes to
the sequencing changes rather than to any change in instrument
reliability.

Efficiency varied by station. The Wend Hollow station posted the
strongest observing-time efficiency in the network, aided by its stable
seeing and low horizon obstruction, and it has consistently led the
network in this measure for the past several quarters. The Ravenglass
Flat station posted efficiency in line with the network average,
benefiting from relatively predictable local weather and a sheltered
dome apron.

The north-ridge station, which hosts the primary deep-survey program and
its secondary transient-monitoring program, posted an observing-time
efficiency of 83.5% for the quarter, the weakest of any station. Instrument
operations attributes this shortfall to increased high-altitude wind
shear on the exposed north ridge during May and June, which required more
frequent protective dome closures on that ridge. A working group has been
asked to review whether the current wind-closure threshold on the
north-ridge station should be permanently revised, with a recommendation
expected before the fourth quarter.

#### Section 5: Data Yield

Total data yield across all stations for the second quarter was 418,900
recorded science frames, an increase over the prior quarter consistent
with seasonal dark-sky patterns. The Wend Hollow station recorded the
largest single share of frames, reflecting its role as the primary
high-cadence monitoring site for the network. Seasonal survey work on the
Brindmoor Survey began in late May and contributed a modest but growing
share of quarterly frames, with allocations trending ahead of the same
period last year.

Calibration-frame volumes, tracked separately from science frames, also
rose during the quarter, consistent with increased survey activity ahead
of the peak dark-sky season. Observing schedulers expect data yield to
continue climbing into the third quarter as the darkest observing period
begins, and have flagged the Ravenglass Flat station's archive-transfer
bandwidth as a possible constraint, discussed further in Section 8.

#### Section 6: Safety

The quarter saw 3 reportable safety incidents, all classified as minor
under the funding authority's incident-severity scale. The first involved
a minor hoist mishap at the Wend Hollow station (referenced above in
Section 3) that required mount repair but caused no injuries; the
instrument involved was back in scheduled operation within the seven-day
repair window described in Section 3. The second involved an observer
slip on an icy dome catwalk at the north-ridge station, which resulted in
a first-aid treatment on scene and no lost-time injury. The third involved
a minor coolant leak in a spectrograph enclosure, contained by the duty
technician with no exposure and no equipment damage beyond the affected
fitting.

All three incidents were reviewed by the safety office and closed with
corrective-action items completed before the end of the quarter. No
incidents during the quarter met the funding authority's threshold for
mandatory external investigation, and the safety office's quarterly
summary to the council characterized the quarter as within normal safety
performance bounds for the network.

The safety office also completed its scheduled quarterly review of
emergency-equipment compliance across the network, finding all 16 active
instruments current on dome fire-suppression and emergency-stop
certification. This review is conducted independently of the optical
certification mentioned in Section 2 and is reported separately to the
funding authority each quarter as part of the authority's ongoing
oversight of research-facility safety across all sites in the region, not
only the Duskholt Observatory Consortium.

#### Section 7: Staff

Instrument operations is organized under a senior staff astronomer
assigned to each instrument as lead astronomer of record, supported by
rotating relief astronomers and night-operations crews. Staffing levels
remained stable during the quarter, with one new relief astronomer
completing certification and joining the roster in June, and no lead
astronomer vacancies remained unfilled at quarter's end.

The lead astronomer of record for the Halvers Reflector is Dr. Isolde
Frayn, who has served with the Duskholt Observatory Consortium for
eleven years and has held the Halvers Reflector assignment since 2022.
Dr. Frayn also chairs the network's quarterly safety review committee
referenced in Section 6, and was recognized this quarter for the
committee's work closing out all incident reviews ahead of the council's
July meeting.

The lead astronomer of record for the Kelmarsh Rise is Dr. Yusuf
Delacroix, and the lead astronomer of record for the Kelmarsh Ridge is
Dr. Petra Halvers. Relief astronomer assignments for the remaining
instruments are documented in the internal scheduling system and are not
itemized in this report. Dr. Delacroix has held the Kelmarsh Rise
assignment since the instrument's commissioning in 2013, making them the
longest-serving lead astronomer of record currently active in the
network.

#### Section 8: Facility and Capital Investment

Capital investment during the quarter focused on dome infrastructure. The
council approved a dome refurbishment budget of $268,000 for the Wend
Hollow station, covering shutter-mechanism replacement, insulation
upgrades, and resurfacing of the instrument-transfer apron. Work is
scheduled to begin in the third quarter and is expected to take
approximately ten weeks, with minimal disruption anticipated to the Wend
Hollow station's regular observing schedule since work will proceed
alongside, rather than in place of, normal station operations.

A smaller capital allocation was approved for signage upgrades at the
Ravenglass Flat station, and a feasibility study was commissioned to
assess expanding the north-ridge station's archive-transfer bandwidth
ahead of anticipated data growth over the coming two years. The
feasibility study is expected to report back to the council in the fourth
quarter, alongside the north-ridge wind-closure review mentioned in
Section 4.

Together, the Wend Hollow dome refurbishment, the Ravenglass Flat signage
upgrade, and the north-ridge feasibility study make up the entirety of
this quarter's approved capital program; no other capital requests were
before the council during the second quarter. The finance office notes
that the Wend Hollow allocation is funded from the facility capital
reserve rather than from the operating budget referenced in Section 3, so
the corrigendum in Appendix B has no bearing on the capital figures
reported in this section.

#### Appendix A: Observing-Schedule Table

The observing-schedule table below summarizes scheduled nightly observing
blocks across the network's principal stations, as maintained by the
scheduling office.

The north-ridge station (deep-survey program, primary) runs 11 scheduled
observing blocks per night. The Wend Hollow station runs 15 scheduled
observing blocks per night in dark time and 8 in bright time, reflecting
its role as the network's highest-cadence monitoring site. The Ravenglass
Flat station runs 5 scheduled observing blocks per night. The Corabel Pass
station, which serves the smaller outlying dark-sky site at Wend Hollow's
upper terrace and is worked by the Halvers Reflector, runs 7 scheduled
observing blocks per night.

Seasonal survey work on the Brindmoor Survey does not follow a fixed
nightly schedule and is allocated on demand during the summer campaign
season, so it is not included in the nightly-block figures above. The
scheduling office reviews this table at the start of each quarter and
adjusts block counts as data demand and instrument availability warrant.

#### Appendix B: Corrigendum to Section 3

After this report's initial internal circulation, the finance office
identified a subcontractor invoice for mount-repair machining, dated late
in the quarter, that had not been incorporated into the Section 3
maintenance-spend figure. Incorporating this invoice, the correct, final
maintenance-spend figure for Q2 2026 is $53,180, superseding the figure
printed in Section 3 above. All downstream financial summaries and
council materials should cite this corrected figure as the quarter's
maintenance spend, and the finance office has updated its internal ledger
accordingly.

No other figures in this report are affected by this corrigendum. The
finance office has confirmed that data-yield, observing-time efficiency,
and capital investment figures elsewhere in this report were unaffected
by the late-arriving invoice.
