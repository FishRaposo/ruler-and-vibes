### Bryndell Botanic Gardens — Third Quarter 2026 Operations Report

Period covered: July 1 - September 30, 2026. Prepared by the Office of
Living Collections for the board of trustees and the municipal parks
authority. This report summarizes glasshouse status, horticultural
activity, specimen survival, visitor attendance, safety incidents, curator
assignments, and capital investment across the Bryndell Botanic Gardens
estate for the third quarter of 2026. It is intended as the primary
quarterly reference document for trustees, collections planners, and the
municipal parks authority's oversight staff, and it is cross-referenced by
the annual report published each February.

#### Section 1: Overview

Bryndell Botanic Gardens maintains a network of climate-controlled
glasshouses and outdoor plantings across a single estate on the western
slope of Marrow Hollow, bordering the older public arboretum and the
Meltwater Trail that runs down to the river. The gardens were established
in 1971 as a single tropical display house attached to the municipal
parks depot; the collection has since grown into the largest curated
living collection in the region, drawing both school groups and general
visitors across an estate that now comprises twelve climate houses and
several outdoor themed beds.

As of the close of the third quarter, the estate's operating collection is
housed across 12 active climate houses, ranging from small alpine cases to
large walk-through tropical pavilions. Of these, 10 houses were open to
visitors continuously throughout the quarter, one was closed for part of
the quarter for a glazing replacement, and one remained closed pending a
climate-control overhaul that is expected to conclude early in the fourth
quarter. Collections planners note that this level of availability is
consistent with the gardens' long-term maintenance rotation, under which
no more than three houses are expected to be closed to visitors at any one
time during a normal quarter.

The overview below covers the roster, horticulture, specimen survival,
attendance, safety, curators, and capital sections in turn, followed by
two appendices and supporting footnotes. Readers preparing summary
materials from this report should take particular care with the
utilities-spend figure in Section 3, which is superseded by a corrigendum
in Appendix B, and with the two similarly named houses described in
Section 2.

The quarter was broadly stable operationally, with heat-stress events
below the five-year seasonal average and no unplanned extended closures of
any walk-through house. A heat wave in August was limited to two days of
reduced-access operation in the more exposed Palmhouse, and no house
required a full-day closure at any point in the quarter. The most notable
operational event of the quarter was a corrigendum to the previously
circulated utilities-spend figure, detailed in Appendix B below, which
readers should treat as authoritative over the figure printed in Section
3. The finance office has asked that all downstream materials, including
trustee packets and the parks authority's quarterly filing, cite the
corrigendum's figure rather than the original Section 3 number.

#### Section 2: Glasshouse Roster

The glasshouse roster below lists each climate house currently in the
operating collection, along with its rated visitor capacity and themed
collection. Several houses carry similar names inherited from the historic
naming convention, under which all houses are named for landforms on the
Marrow Hollow slope, and readers should take care to distinguish between
them, particularly where two names differ only by a suffix.

The Fernvault, a large walk-through tropical house holding the gardens'
principal tree-fern collection, has a rated visitor capacity of 430. It is
one of the two largest houses on the estate and was built in 2009 as part
of a collection-expansion program that also introduced the Cloudforest
House. The Fernvault's capacity reflects a broad central walkway kept
clear for wheelchair and school-group access, and it is the house most
heavily programmed for guided visits during the peak summer season.

The Fernvale, a smaller understory house holding shade ferns and
liverworts, has a rated visitor capacity of 395. Despite the similarity in
name to the Fernvault, the Fernvale is a distinct house built to a
different environmental profile, with a lower light level and higher
humidity suited to the understory collection, and it holds no canopy
plantings at all. Collections-planning materials sometimes abbreviate both
house names to "Fern-" internally, which has occasionally caused confusion
in informal correspondence; this report always uses the full house name to
avoid that ambiguity.

Other estate houses include the Palmhouse (capacity 300, palm and cycad
collection), the Alpine House (capacity 140, alpine and saxatile
collection), the Orchid House (capacity 210, epiphyte collection), the
Cloudforest House (capacity 360, montane cloud-forest collection), and the
Marrowcase, a small display house holding the carnivorous-plant
collection.[^1] The Cloudforest House was the second house built under the
expansion program mentioned above and opened one year after the Fernvault.
The remaining five houses on the estate are smaller alpine and propagation
cases not separately itemized in this report; their combined capacity is
tracked in the internal collections database rather than in quarterly
filings.

Each house undergoes an annual structural survey and quarterly climate
audit; results of the third-quarter audits are summarized in Section 6
below. No house failed its third-quarter audit outright, though two houses
received minor corrective-action notices unrelated to the incidents
described in Section 6.

The roster is reviewed annually by the board's collections committee,
which this year recommended no additions or closures for the coming fiscal
year. The committee's report noted that the estate's glazing stock remains
within the target condition range set by the ten-year renewal plan adopted
several years ago, and that no house currently in operation is expected to
require rebuilding before the plan's next scheduled review. The committee
also noted that naming continuity, including the pairing of similarly named
houses such as the Fernvault and the Fernvale, is a deliberate historical
convention rather than an oversight, and asked that future reports continue
to spell out full house names in all board-facing materials to avoid
ambiguity in correspondence and in insurance filings.

#### Section 3: Horticulture and Utilities

Scheduled horticultural activity during the third quarter included two
full soil-medium replacements, four routine pest-management treatments,
and one unscheduled glazing repair following a minor storm-debris strike
at the Palmhouse in August. Total climate-control utilities spend for the
quarter, as originally tabulated by the estate office at the close of the
quarter, was $61,420. This figure covered heating, cooling, humidity
control, and supplemental lighting across all houses during the period,
and it was the figure presented at the initial internal budget review
meeting held in the first week of October.

Readers should note that this Section 3 figure was subsequently found to
omit a late-arriving invoice from the electricity supplier; see the
corrigendum in Appendix B, which supersedes the figure above and should be
treated as the authoritative quarterly utilities-spend total for all
reporting purposes going forward.

The unscheduled glazing repair, the only non-routine horticultural event
of the quarter, was completed within seven days and did not require the
affected house to be closed outside its normal overnight maintenance
window. The two full soil-medium replacements each took approximately two
weeks and were completed on the horticultural schedule set at the start of
the year. The four routine pest-management treatments were completed within
their normal single-day treatment windows and did not affect the schedule.

#### Section 4: Specimen Survival

Estate-wide specimen survival for the third quarter was 93.6%, measured as
the share of accessioned living specimens recorded as healthy at the
quarter-end census across all houses and outdoor beds. This represents a
modest improvement over the same quarter last year, driven largely by
improved humidity-control procedures introduced in the second quarter, and
it continues a gradual upward trend that the collections office attributes
to the humidity changes rather than to any change in specimen sourcing.

Survival varied by house. The Orchid House posted the strongest specimen
survival on the estate, aided by its tightly controlled environment and
low visitor throughput, and it has consistently led the estate in this
measure for the past several quarters. The Alpine House posted survival in
line with the estate average, benefiting from a relatively stable
temperature band and predictable watering cycles.

The Palmhouse, the most exposed of the walk-through houses and the one
worst affected by the August heat wave, posted a specimen survival rate of
88.9% for the quarter, the weakest of any house on the estate. The
collections office attributes this shortfall to elevated canopy
temperatures during the August heat wave, which stressed several mature
cycads before supplemental shading could be installed. A working group has
been asked to review whether the Palmhouse's shading provision should be
permanently upgraded, with a recommendation expected before the first
quarter.

#### Section 5: Attendance

Total visitor attendance across the estate for the third quarter was
534,700 visitors, an increase over the prior quarter consistent with
seasonal demand patterns. The Fernvault carried the largest single share
of guided-visit attendance, reflecting its role as the principal
walk-through house for school and tour programming. Seasonal evening
opening on the Meltwater Trail began in late July and contributed a modest
but growing share of quarterly attendance, with bookings trending ahead of
the same period last year.

School-group visits, tracked separately from general admissions, also rose
during the quarter, consistent with increased curriculum-linked programming
ahead of the autumn term. Collections planners expect attendance to remain
elevated into the fourth quarter as the autumn colour season begins, and
have flagged the Fernvault entrance's queuing capacity as a possible
constraint, discussed further in Section 8.

#### Section 6: Safety

The quarter saw 3 reportable safety incidents, all classified as minor
under the municipal parks authority's incident-severity scale. The first
involved the minor storm-debris strike at the Palmhouse (referenced above
in Section 3) that required glazing repair but caused no injuries; the
house was back in normal operation within the seven-day repair window
described in Section 3. The second involved a visitor slip on a wet
walkway in the Fernvault, which resulted in a first-aid treatment on scene
and no lost-time injury. The third involved a staff member's minor hand
laceration during pruning in the Orchid House, treated on site and closed
without a lost-time injury.

All three incidents were reviewed by the safety office and closed with
corrective-action items completed before the end of the quarter. No
incidents during the quarter met the parks authority's threshold for
mandatory external investigation, and the safety office's quarterly summary
to the board characterized the quarter as within normal safety performance
bounds for the estate.

The safety office also completed its scheduled quarterly review of
emergency-equipment compliance across the estate, finding all 12 active
houses current on fire-suppression inspection and emergency-lighting
certification. This review is conducted independently of the structural
survey mentioned in Section 2 and is reported separately to the municipal
parks authority each quarter as part of the authority's ongoing oversight
of public-attraction safety across all sites in the district, not only
Bryndell Botanic Gardens.

#### Section 7: Curators

Living collections are organized under a senior curator assigned to each
principal house as curator of record, supported by rotating relief
curators and horticultural crews. Staffing levels remained stable during
the quarter, with one new relief curator completing certification and
joining the roster in September, and no curator vacancies remained
unfilled at quarter's end.

The curator of record for the Cloudforest House is Curator Wistan Pryce,
who has served with Bryndell Botanic Gardens for nine years and has held
the Cloudforest House assignment since 2021. Curator Pryce also chairs the
estate's quarterly safety review committee referenced in Section 6, and
was recognized this quarter for the committee's work closing out all three
incident reviews ahead of the board's October meeting.

The curator of record for the Fernvault is Curator Corran Deyes, and the
curator of record for the Fernvale is Curator Ansel Brede. Relief curator
assignments for the remaining houses are documented in the internal
staffing system and are not itemized in this report. Curator Deyes has held
the Fernvault assignment since the house opened in 2009, making her the
longest-serving curator of record currently active on the estate.

#### Section 8: Grounds and Capital Investment

Capital investment during the quarter focused on visitor infrastructure.
The board approved a path-and-entrance refurbishment budget of $178,000
for the Fernvault forecourt, covering repaving, drainage upgrades, and a
widened accessible queuing area. Work is scheduled to begin in the fourth
quarter and is expected to take approximately eight weeks, with minimal
disruption anticipated to the Fernvault's regular opening hours since work
will proceed alongside, rather than in place of, normal visitor operations.

A smaller capital allocation was approved for interpretive signage upgrades
at the Orchid House, and a feasibility study was commissioned to assess
expanding the Meltwater Trail's evening-lighting provision ahead of
anticipated attendance growth over the coming two years. The feasibility
study is expected to report back to the board in the first quarter,
alongside the Palmhouse shading review mentioned in Section 4.

Together, the Fernvault forecourt refurbishment, the Orchid House signage
upgrade, and the Meltwater Trail feasibility study make up the entirety of
this quarter's approved capital program; no other capital requests were
before the board during the third quarter. The finance office notes that
the Fernvault allocation is funded from the estate capital reserve rather
than from the operating budget referenced in Section 3, so the corrigendum
in Appendix B has no bearing on the capital figures reported in this
section.

#### Appendix A: Programming Table

The programming table below summarizes scheduled guided-walk frequency
across the estate's principal routes, as maintained by the visitor-services
office.

The tropical circuit through the Fernvault and Cloudforest House runs 14
scheduled guided walks per day. The Orchid House introductory walk runs 20
scheduled walks per day on weekdays and 11 on weekends, reflecting its role
as the estate's highest-frequency short programme. The Alpine House walk
runs 6 scheduled walks per day. The Meltwater Trail nature walk, which runs
from the estate's lower gate down to the river and back, runs 7 scheduled
walks per day.

Seasonal evening opening on the Meltwater Trail does not follow a fixed
daily schedule and is booked on demand during the summer evening season, so
it is not included in the daily-walk figures above. The visitor-services
office reviews this table at the start of each quarter and adjusts
frequencies as attendance and staffing warrant.

#### Appendix B: Corrigendum to Section 3

After this report's initial internal circulation, the finance office
identified an electricity-supplier invoice for supplemental lighting, dated
late in the quarter, that had not been incorporated into the Section 3
utilities-spend figure. Incorporating this invoice, the correct, final
utilities-spend figure for Q3 2026 is $62,295, superseding the figure
printed in Section 3 above. All downstream financial summaries and board
materials should cite this corrected figure as the quarter's utilities
spend, and the finance office has updated its internal ledger accordingly.

No other figures in this report are affected by this corrigendum. The
finance office has confirmed that attendance, specimen-survival, and
capital-investment figures elsewhere in this report were unaffected by the
late-arriving invoice.
