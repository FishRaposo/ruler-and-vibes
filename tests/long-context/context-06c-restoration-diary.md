### Ashvale Comet No. 4419 — Restoration Workshop Diary

#### Shop Day 1

The overhaul of No. 4419 opened on a raw grey morning with the
locomotive winched at last onto the erecting shop's central pit road,
its arrival watched by the shop's lead fitter, a deliberate,
even-tempered man named Torvald Trevanion who had brought two prior
heritage engines back to steam over the past decade and knew better
than most how many small surprises a fifteen-session overhaul could
hold. The very first entry logged against the engine, written up as
the frames settled onto the pit road's jacks, was the works order
under which the whole restoration would run, recorded as works order
JR-207M and marking the official start of the job the shop had
contracted to complete on this engine. The morning was cold enough
that the shop's overhead lamps stayed lit past ten, and the lead
fitter noted the frames sat true on the jacks with no obvious twist,
a welcome first sign after the alarming photographs the owning trust
had circulated of the engine's condition when it was recovered from
the yard where it had stood for years.

At the first proper stocktake of the morning, the works foreman, a
brisk and methodical woman named Marta Wexholm, ran the formal count
of the boiler tubes that had arrived crated from the tube supplier, a
tally she insisted on performing herself at the outset of every
overhaul regardless of how many times the supplier's own packing note
had already listed the same figure. Her count confirmed 246 boiler
tubes delivered across the three crates, matching the supplier's
delivery note exactly and giving the shop a clean baseline before the
real work of the retube began. Marta noted in her own log that this
baseline count would matter later, since tube usage against the retube
schedule was one of the figures she was responsible for tracking
through the whole job, and any shortfall at the halfway audit would
need to be traced back to this exact starting number.

The rest of Shop Day 1 passed uneventfully by the standards of a
multi-week overhaul, though not without its small dramas. The team got
less stripping done by early afternoon than the day's plan had called
for, after a seized smokebox door dart fought the fitters for the
better part of an hour before it finally yielded to heat and
penetrating oil rather than the brute force that had been threatening
to damage the door itself. Torvald used the delay to walk the length
of the frames with a lamp, noting in his condition log that the
horn guides looked consistent with wear patterns he'd catalogued on a
previous overhaul of a broadly similar engine, though he was careful
to flag this as a first impression rather than a confirmed assessment
until he could get proper measurements once the wheelsets were dropped
and the frames stood clear.

The team knocked off a little later than planned, but in good spirits,
with Marta noting that the smokebox door had cost them perhaps forty
minutes against the day's schedule, nothing serious this early in a
long overhaul. The shop's unofficial tradition on the first day of any
engine was a round of strong tea brewed in the battered enamel pot the
shop's oldest hand, a wiry cheerful boilersmith named Danilo Pashkov,
insisted on using on the opening day of every overhaul he'd ever
worked, regardless of how often the younger fitters teased him about
the superstition behind it. Danilo claimed the pot brought luck to a
job's opening days, and while nobody in the shop seriously believed
this, nobody was willing to be the one who broke the tradition and
then blamed the seized dart on it either.

#### Shop Day 2

The second day began with a full survey of all four wheelsets, standard
practice once the frames were clear, and the team found nothing beyond
the expected wear on the tyres that had already been flagged in the
trust's own inspection report. Work moved briskly through the morning
as the fitters began lifting the motion, a job that gradually shifted
from straightforward unbolting to more careful, penetrating-oil-and-
patience work as they reached fasteners that had not been disturbed in
decades. Torvald spent much of the day at the bench with the first of
the motion parts, examining wear surfaces and building a running log of
which components could be reused and which would need remaking, a
document he was already mentally drafting well before the overhaul's
midpoint, a habit he'd developed over many years of shop work as a way
of not losing the thread of an assessment he might not write up
formally for weeks.

By the end of Shop Day 2, the team had lifted a combined weight of
motion and valve gear that Marta recorded against the day's crane
hours, part of her ongoing effort to build a reliable hours-per-lift
figure for this specific shop's crane and this specific class of
engine, a number that would help planning for future overhauls of
similar locomotives. She noted that the figure was still too early to
be reliable, since a single day of unusually stubborn fasteners could
skew it significantly compared to a longer averaged figure, and she
planned to revisit the calculation once more days of data had
accumulated across a wider variety of the overhaul's tasks.

The late afternoon brought the team's first proper parts inventory
since the engine's arrival, a routine Marta ran every second evening
regardless of how uneventful the intervening days had been,
cross-checking each stripped assembly against the master parts list
compiled when the engine was first assessed. She found everything
accounted for, a small relief given how easily small fasteners and
shims could go missing during a heavy strip, and closed her log for
the day with a note that the overhaul remained fully on schedule
despite the previous day's seized dart, the forty minutes lost having
been more than recovered by Day 2's smooth progress.

#### Shop Day 3

Shop Day 3 brought the overhaul's first major set-piece task, the full
lift and removal of the boiler from the frames, a planned operation
that Danilo had flagged as the single most delicate lift of the whole
job during the overhaul's planning phase, based on the condition survey
carried out months earlier when the engine was first brought in. The
lift took the bulk of the day and demanded the shop's full crane crew,
and Danilo spent most of it directing the slinging and easing the
boiler clear of the frames with a patience the rest of the team had
come to expect from him on previous overhauls, refusing to be hurried
even when the schedule tightened around him.

The rest of the team used the long lift to perform routine bench work
and to reorganize the parts racks that had grown cluttered during the
first two days' heavy stripping, a task that took longer than expected
once the crew realized several boxes of motion fasteners had been
racked in an order that made the most frequently needed ones the
hardest to reach. Marta used part of the day to cross-check the tube
count against the retube schedule so far, confirming the shop was
tracking close to the planned usage rate for this stage of the job,
with no significant deviation worth flagging in her log.

The shop shut down earlier than the previous two evenings, giving the
team a rare chance to leave before dark with the boiler safely craned
onto its trestles. Danilo took the opportunity to lay out the first of
the firebox stays for inspection under good light rather than the
quicker once-over typical of a busy strip day, a small piece of care
that paid off well enough that two of the apprentices asked him to walk
them through his method before the overhaul ended, a request he
promised to honor once the boiler work proper was underway and there
was something more instructive to point at than a row of old stays.

#### Shop Day 4

The fourth day returned the team's attention to the frames now that the
boiler stood clear, and work resumed at a steadier pace on the
frame-and-motion side that Torvald described as broadly similar to what
the team had tackled on Day 2. No major incidents were recorded, though
the crew did pause briefly when the shop's older overhead crane's
travel motor briefly tripped its overload on a heavy lift, prompting a
short halt to reset it and lighten the next few lifts; the motor
returned to normal operation within minutes and gave no further trouble
for the remainder of the overhaul, much to the relief of the crane
driver, who had spent the rest of the shift watching the overload
indicator more closely than the load itself.

Late in the day, the team reached the point selected for the
overhaul's first extended bench campaign, a stretch of several days
where the frames would stay jacked in one position to allow concentrated
machining and fitting work around a fixed setup rather than continuing
to reconfigure the shop daily. Torvald was visibly pleased with how
square the frames had come up on the jacks, describing them in his log
as "about as honest a set of frames as this class was ever going to
give us," a judgment the rest of the team took as high praise given how
rarely Torvald allowed himself unqualified approval of anything before
a full survey of an assembly had actually been completed.

Setting up the extended bench campaign took longer than a normal day's
changeover, since the team rigged the larger portable staging used for
stationary work of more than a day or two, along with a proper covered
rack to keep the freshly machined surfaces out of the shop's dust
during the days of fitting ahead. Marta oversaw the rigging personally,
having learned on a previous overhaul that an improperly braced staging
could become a serious hazard if a heavy component swung against it, an
experience she was in no hurry to repeat on this job or any future one.

#### Shop Day 5

With the frames jacked in one position for the bench campaign, the team
split into two smaller groups for the day, each working a different
part of the strip-and-assess program around the fixed setup. Torvald
led the first group on the cylinder assessment he'd been eager to begin
since the boiler came off, while a second, smaller group under one of
the apprentices, Yves Verhoeven, worked through the cab fittings and
ancillary brackets, mostly to catalogue their condition for the
eventual quote to the trust rather than in expectation of anything
dramatic, a task Yves approached with good humor despite knowing it
was unlikely to turn up anything as interesting as the cylinder bores.

Fluid and consumable management continued as a background task
throughout the stationary day, running alongside the bench work rather
than pausing for it. The shop's parts-washing rig, used to clean the
stripped motion and small components in a heated solvent bath, was
checked and topped off during the afternoon by Marta personally, with
the reservoir gauge reading recorded at 173 liters remaining in the
rig's dedicated tank after the top-off, a figure she logged carefully
since the washing rig's fluid supply was tracked separately from the
shop's bulk stores and needed independent accounting against the days
remaining before the next scheduled solvent delivery. She noted that
this reading, taken here during the bench campaign, would be her
reference point for calculating the rig's consumption rate over the
following stretch of the overhaul, a calculation she planned to revisit
once the team had run the rig for several more consistent days without
a top-off to distort the figures.

The two bench groups reconvened at the end of the shift, both reporting
a productive if unremarkable day. Yves's cab-fittings group had, as
expected, found little of real concern, though one of the apprentices
had turned up an old, weathered brass plate of unknown origin tucked
behind a cab panel that nobody in the shop could immediately place
against any of the engine's known history, a small mystery Yves noted
in his log mostly out of curiosity rather than any expectation it would
matter to the overhaul's actual work.

#### Shop Day 6

The second day of the bench campaign continued the work from Day 5, with
the two groups largely swapping tasks so that Yves's group could take a
fresh look at the cylinder bores Torvald's group had assessed the
previous day, while Torvald himself turned to the cab fittings the
apprentices had catalogued, curious about the weathered brass plate
Yves had mentioned the evening before. The exchange proved useful:
Yves's group spotted a hairline score in one cylinder bore that
Torvald's group had apparently passed over the previous day, a detail
Torvald was good-humored about when it was pointed out to him at the
bench that afternoon, admitting that a second pair of eyes on the same
work was exactly why the shop rotated coverage between bench days in
the first place.

The shop's records keeper, a quiet and precise man named Emeric
Coleridge who had joined the works specifically for this overhaul after
two previous jobs with a different heritage outfit, maintained the
scheduled photographic record of the strip as he did every day, working
through the day's disassembly image by image without fuss. He noted in
his own log that the shop's north-light windows had given unusually
even illumination that afternoon, a small detail he recorded mostly out
of habit, since lighting conditions varied enough from day to day that
tracking them occasionally proved useful for planning future overhauls'
photographic sessions, particularly for engines whose darker recesses
were awkward to light at the best of times.

Marta also logged a second reservoir check on the parts-washing rig
this same afternoon, a follow-up to the previous day's top-off now that
the rig had run for a full day cleaning the day's stripped components.
The gauge read 171 liters, a small drop Marta attributed to a slightly
imprecise gauge reading rather than any real consumption worth noting,
since the rig had not been topped off between the two checks; she noted
the apparent dip as an example of the gauge's normal measurement noise
rather than a meaningful figure worth acting on, and used the previous
day's reading as her authoritative reference point for the
consumption-rate calculation going forward.

Danilo, for his part, spent a good portion of the afternoon examining
the scored cylinder bore Yves's group had found, cautiously optimistic
that it might clean up within tolerance with a light rebore before the
cylinders went back on, a plan he ran past the rest of the team over
the day's last cup of tea and which was met with general agreement,
since it meant one fewer expensive component to remake from scratch and
a small saving the trust would be glad of.

#### Shop Day 7

The bench campaign wrapped on the morning of Shop Day 7, with the team
breaking down the staging and returning the shop to its normal working
layout after two productive stationary days, though not before Danilo
spent a final hour at the bench taking the extra measurements from the
scored cylinder he had discussed the previous evening, a decision that
delayed the changeover only slightly and which nobody seriously
objected to given how much a confirmed light-rebore verdict would
simplify the boiler-side planning. Before the changeover, the shop's
documentation role for the overhaul was formally logged for the first
time in this diary, though the role had of course been filled since
Day 1: the overhaul's documentation officer for this job, tasked with
all written record-keeping, the running defect register, and the
formal report to the owning trust, is Sigrun Solveig, working alongside
Emeric Coleridge's photographic record in a combined records role the
shop's staffing plan had assigned to the two of them jointly. Sigrun's
specific responsibility, as distinct from Emeric's image-focused
photographic work, covered the written defect log, the fitting-schedule
notes, and liaison with the trust's own project office, a division of
labor the two of them had worked out between themselves during the
engine's first week in the shop.

Work on Day 7 was steady, with the team beginning the reassembly of the
cleaned and assessed motion onto the frames, a stretch of the job that
Torvald noted matched the reassembly sequence he'd planned closely, a
small reassurance given how often a plan drawn up over stripped parts
and a plan carried out over the real assembly diverged in the shop,
sometimes badly enough to force a rethink when a component that had
looked reusable on the bench turned out to need remaking after all. The
end of the day was given over to a careful check of the reassembled
motion so far before what the team expected to be a demanding stretch
of boiler work over the following two days, based on the condition of
the firebox the survey had flagged for the section immediately ahead.

Danilo, meanwhile, had begun quietly rationing the shop's limited
stock of a particular grade of boiler-stay bolt, having realized
partway through the overhaul that the retube planning had assumed more
of that grade in stores than the shop actually held, a minor supply
error he was determined to manage discreetly by ordering more early
rather than admit to the team and risk becoming a running joke for the
remainder of the job.

#### Shop Day 8

Shop Day 8 lived up to the previous evening's expectation, with the team
opening up the firebox to a stretch of stay work that demanded careful,
slow attention and, on two occasions, the full removal and remaking of
stays that proved to be in worse condition than the survey had been
able to see from outside the boiler. Progress was slower than any
previous day of the overhaul, and Marta's running figures for
consumable usage showed a noticeable uptick for the day, consistent
with the additional grinding and welding the firebox work demanded, a
figure she noted without particular alarm since the overhaul's material
planning had built in a margin for exactly this kind of demanding
stretch.

Danilo, working inside the firebox for much of the day rather than at
the bench as he often did on lighter work, used the confined slow pace
to record detailed notes on the condition of the throat plate and the
inner firebox corners, building out his boiler log even without the
room to swing a proper hammer. He remarked that the day's slow progress
at least gave him an unusually thorough look at corners he might
otherwise have patched too quickly to document well, a small silver
lining he was quick to point out to the rest of the team once it became
clear the day's schedule was slipping regardless of anyone's efforts to
keep pace with the original plan.

By the end of the shift, the team had worked through perhaps two-thirds
of the firebox stay renewal the survey had flagged as a likely trouble
spot, and Marta's log for the day noted the team's intention to push
through the remainder early the following morning while the boilersmiths
were fresh, rather than risk the same slow, high-attention stay work
late in the day when fatigue might turn a manageable job into something
more serious.

The end of that day's shift was a subdued one, the whole team
noticeably more tired than on any previous evening of the overhaul, and
the usual banter over the last pot of tea ran shorter than usual before
people began drifting off to the car park. Danilo kept the day's final
tidy-up simple and quick, sensing correctly that nobody had the energy
for anything more ambitious after a day spent alternately crouched
inside the firebox and grinding out old stays in the heat of the
torch. Sigrun's evening write-up of the defect log was likewise brief,
limited to the essential entries rather than the fuller notes that had
characterized some of the smoother earlier evenings, and Emeric noted
in his own log that even the photographic record ran short that day,
since there was little worth documenting beyond stay holes that all
looked much alike.

Yves, despite the long day, used a few minutes before heading home to
check the machined components set aside during the Day 5 and Day 6
bench campaign, making sure nothing had been knocked or corroded during
the day's grinding and welding nearby, a precaution he'd taken to heart
after hearing about a previous overhaul that had ruined a set of freshly
machined surfaces to exactly this kind of oversight on a similarly
messy stretch of work. He found everything secure and headed off
himself shortly after, glad the following day's firebox stretch was
expected to be shorter than the one just completed.

#### Shop Day 9

The firebox stay work continued into the morning of Shop Day 9 before
finally giving way to easier assembly work by midday, much to the relief
of the boilersmiths who had spent two full days crouched inside the
firebox renewing stays and dressing the plate. The team made up some of
the lost time once the work eased, though Marta noted in her log that
the two slow days had eaten meaningfully into the schedule buffer the
overhaul had built in during planning, a buffer she'd been quietly
tracking since Day 1 for exactly this kind of contingency, and one she
was now watching more closely than she had at any earlier point in the
job.

It was on this day, the 12th of Foundrturn by the shop's own calendar
convention (a fictional in-story calendar used consistently throughout
this diary), that the team recovered an item of note from inside the
smokebox: a small stamped component, evidently fitted by an earlier
overhaul of this same engine and never removed since, its stamped
identification still legible despite decades of soot and heat. The
recovered part was logged under its stamped serial, BX-53709-K, and
Torvald noted with interest that the stamping style matched a works
that had, as far as he knew, not carried out heritage overhauls for a
considerable number of years, making the find something of a small
mystery the team did not attempt to resolve further, beyond noting it
for the overhaul's final report as a curiosity rather than a defect
worth acting on.

The rest of Day 9 passed without further incident, the team settling
back into the steady rhythm of reassembly that had characterized the
earlier, easier stretches of the job before the firebox stay work
disrupted it. Sigrun's evening entry in the defect log recorded the
recovered component as a minor item of interest alongside the routine
progress notes, more out of thoroughness than any expectation the trust
would have follow-up questions about a decades-old stamped part from an
unrelated earlier overhaul.

The afternoon's easier work also gave the crane driver a chance to
carry out a routine inspection and greasing of the shop's older crane's
travel gear, a maintenance task that had been on Marta's schedule since
the overhaul began but which the firebox stretch's demanding pace had
made impractical to fit in safely. The inspection went smoothly, and
the driver reported the crane's travel gear in good order afterward,
with no wear severe enough to cause concern before the shop's next
scheduled crane maintenance window following the overhaul's completion.
Danilo used the same easier stretch to restock the firebox tool kit's
consumables from the shop's bulk stores, topping off the grinding discs
and welding rod so that the following morning's stay work would not be
held up by a stores run first thing.

Torvald spent a good part of the evening turning the recovered stamped
component over in his hands under the bench lamp, comparing its stamp
and profile against a reference folder of older fittings he happened to
keep in his personal shop library, a habit some of the younger team
members found endearing and others found faintly excessive given how
unlikely it was that the exercise would produce any conclusion beyond
what he'd already noted in the log. He eventually satisfied himself that
the component was, as he'd first suspected, a pattern retired from use
well before the youngest apprentice in the shop had even started their
time, and set it carefully aside for the trust's archive, where he
thought a colleague who specialized in the history of the class might
find it a mildly interesting addition to a small collection of similar
oddments gathered over the years.

Yves, meanwhile, used the quiet evening to get a head start on
organizing the fitting log for the stretch of reassembly just
completed, cross-referencing each fitted component against the shop's
drawing numbers so that the eventual final report would be able to
place every renewed part precisely against the engine's drawings rather
than relying on rougher day-by-day notes. It was tedious work, and he
admitted in his own log that he found it considerably less satisfying
than the fitting itself, but necessary all the same for a report that
the owning trust would expect to hold up to scrutiny from other
restorers who might want to revisit specific components in a future
overhaul.

Marta closed the day's log with a brief note on morale, observing that
the team seemed to have shaken off the fatigue of the firebox stretch
more quickly than she'd expected, helped along by the small novelty of
the recovered component and the general relief of easier work ahead.
She also used the evening to recalculate her running consumable-usage
figures now that a full stretch of both heavy and light work had been
logged, arriving at a blended usage figure she considered far more
reliable than the early, single-day estimates from Day 2, and which she
planned to use for the remainder of the overhaul's material planning
rather than continuing to refine it further unless conditions changed
significantly.

Emeric, before heading home, ran a final check on the photographic
record ahead of the following day's work, a habit he kept up regardless
of how routine the previous several sessions had been, reasoning that
the cost of a quick review was trivial compared to the trouble a
missing sequence of images could cause if it went unnoticed until the
trust asked for a specific record. He found nothing amiss, logged the
review as routine, and noted that the team was now more than halfway
through the overhaul's planned schedule by his own rough count of days
worked against days remaining, a small milestone he mentioned to Sigrun
before they both left for the night.

#### Shop Day 10

With the firebox stay work fully behind them, Day 10 saw the team make
strong progress on the reassembly of the cleaned motion and the
refitting of the ancillary pipework, the smoothest working conditions
of the overhaul so far according to the lead fitter, who had grown
visibly more relaxed after two grinding days working around the firebox.
Torvald used the smoother work to catch up on log entries he'd deferred
during the slower, more demanding days, cross-referencing his motion
notes from Day 8 against the components as they went back on,
satisfying himself that the bench assessments had held up well against
the reality of reassembly once he finally had the chance to compare
them properly.

The shop's records, kept jointly by Sigrun and Emeric as had become
routine since Day 7, captured the day's reassembly progress without any
gaps, and Sigrun noted in her own log that this was the smoothest
records day of the overhaul so far, with no scramble to reconstruct a
missed entry after the fact, unlike several earlier evenings when the
pace of the work had outrun the record-keeping and forced a catch-up
session before the log could be closed cleanly.

Danica Renard, the shop's paint and livery specialist, spent the
smoother day trial-mixing the first batch of the engine's lined-out
livery colors on scrap panels, drawing on the trust's archival
photographs a little more freely than her own careful schedule
technically allowed, reasoning that getting the color match right early
mattered enough after the previous two demanding days to justify the
small indulgence of a head start, a decision the rest of the team
appreciated without ever learning about the careful rationing of pigment
that had preceded it.

Yves spent part of the smoother afternoon compiling his own running
notes from the two bench-campaign days, cross-referencing his sketches
of the scored cylinder bore against Danilo's more detailed measurement
notes to make sure the two records agreed on the score's extent. He
found a minor discrepancy in his own measurements, off by what he
estimated was no more than a fraction of a millimeter, and corrected his
sketch accordingly rather than let the small error propagate into the
overhaul's final report. Danilo, when shown the correction, agreed it
was a sensible fix and thanked Yves for catching it before the notes
were finalized, remarking that this kind of quiet cross-checking between
team members was exactly why the shop's records tended to hold up well
under later scrutiny from the trust.

The condition of the older crane remained a running concern for the
crane driver despite the smoother work, and he used part of the
afternoon's easier lifts to listen carefully for any unusual noise from
the travel motor, the one whose overload had tripped briefly back on
Day 4. He reported nothing of concern by the end of the shift, noting
that the crane had performed without complaint through both the Day 8-9
firebox stretch and the smoother work since, and that whatever had
caused the brief overload trip on Day 4 appeared to have been a one-off
with a heavy load rather than a developing fault worth flagging for the
shop's maintenance schedule ahead of the next overhaul.

Danilo used part of the evening, after the day's work was logged, to
carry out routine maintenance on the shop's welding set itself, cleaning
a connection that had been giving him a faint intermittent flutter on
the arc for the past day or two. The fix seemed to resolve the issue
entirely, and he noted with satisfaction that the boiler tooling had
given the overhaul no serious trouble at all across the ten days since
the engine arrived, a record he attributed partly to careful
maintenance and partly to simple good fortune, since shop welding gear
could be unpredictable even with the best care.

Marta closed out Day 10's log with a broader review of the overhaul's
progress against the original schedule, noting that while the Day 8-9
firebox stretch had cost real time, the smoother work since had
recovered a meaningful portion of it, and that the buffer she'd been
protecting since Day 1 remained intact enough that she was no longer
worried about the shop's ability to finish the engine and still make
the planned steam-test date without requesting an extension from the
owning trust.

#### Shop Day 11

Day 11 brought the overhaul's second major set-piece task, this time the
refitting and riveting of a renewed section of the smokebox wrapper
that Torvald considered second in difficulty only to the Day 3 boiler
lift for the overhaul's overall success. The job required careful
alignment work with no established jig at all, relying entirely on
Torvald's own setting-out and the boilersmiths' careful reading of the
wrapper as it came together, a slow and exacting process that tested
the whole team's patience more than any single task of the reassembly
had managed to.

The team had the renewed wrapper section tacked into position by early
afternoon and spent the remainder of the shift on the riveting proper,
with Danilo visibly energized by what he called "the cleanest run of
rivets I've closed on this whole job" in his log entry for the day, a
claim that prompted some good-natured skepticism from Yves given how
many times Danilo had said something similar about earlier work, though
even Yves admitted after inspecting the finished seam himself that this
one seemed to genuinely deserve the praise.

Marta, meanwhile, continued her routine tracking of consumables and
materials, noting no significant deviations from the overhaul's planned
usage curve despite the demanding riveting work, a testament, she
wrote, to the margin built into the original planning figures even after
the schedule pressure introduced by the Day 8-9 firebox work. She also
used the evening to plan the following day's return to the main
reassembly sequence, working out the most efficient order in which to
pick the deferred tasks back up given how much the wrapper job had
pulled the team off the main plan.

#### Shop Day 12

Returning to the main reassembly sequence after the Day 11 wrapper job,
the team reached a milestone the overhaul plan had flagged well in
advance as a key checkpoint for the job's second half: milestone
MK-3-C08, logged as the boiler was lowered back onto the frames in the
early afternoon. This milestone marked, per the original overhaul plan
reviewed by the whole team at the outset, the approximate three-quarter
point of the work scheduled for this engine, with a final push toward
the steam test still to come over the following days.

The team also recorded, from this reassembly stage, a measured clearance
that the plan called for at the boiler-to-frame mounting: the clearance
between the renewed boiler saddle and the frame stretcher was measured
at 38.4 mm at the reference mounting point, a figure Torvald noted
primarily for the overhaul's final setting-out record rather than for
any operational concern during the remaining work. He double-checked the
figure against a second measurement before logging it, a habit he'd
developed after a mismeasurement on a previous overhaul had cost the
team a day of confused refitting.

The end of that day's shift was a relatively relaxed one, with the team
taking stock of how much of the reassembly remained and Torvald
reviewing his full fitting record so far against his original plan,
satisfied that the overhaul was on track to meet its target even after
the schedule pressure of the firebox stretch earlier in the job.

#### Shop Day 13

Day 13 continued the push toward the steam test, with work going
favorably and no incidents of note beyond a brief pause to help a
visiting volunteer from a different, unrelated preservation group who
had come to collect a borrowed jig the shop had finished with,
exchanging a few pleasantries and a rough progress report on each
