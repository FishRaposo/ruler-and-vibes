## Task

Below is a consolidated municipal ordinance, including two amendments.
Read it carefully, paying attention to which amendment currently governs
each provision — Amendment 2 explicitly supersedes part of Amendment 1.
Then:

1. Answer the 8 scenario questions below in `ANSWERS.md`. For each
   scenario, write a block with a `Verdict:` line containing exactly one
   of `Permitted`, `Not permitted`, or `Not specified in the document`; a
   `Cite:` line naming the section(s) of the ordinance that support your

2. Write `BRIEF.md`, a synthesis in 200 words or fewer of the ordinance's

### Town of Bellhollow — Greywater Reuse and Outdoor Irrigation Code

Adopted by the Bellhollow Town Council as Ordinance No. 214, this code
governs the installation, registration, and operation of residential
greywater reuse systems within town limits, and establishes restrictions
on outdoor irrigation during declared drought stages. This document
consolidates the original ordinance with Amendment 1 and Amendment 2,
both adopted by subsequent council action, and includes the fee schedule
currently in effect at Appendix A. Where a later amendment conflicts with
an earlier provision, the later amendment controls, as each amendment
below states explicitly which prior clauses it supersedes.

Bellhollow adopted this code in response to a multi-year regional dry
spell that placed increasing strain on the town's municipal water supply.
The Town Council's stated goal in adopting the original ordinance was to
encourage responsible reuse of household greywater for outdoor irrigation
while preserving the town's ability to impose stricter restrictions
during periods of declared drought. The registration and discount
provisions added by Amendment 1 and later revised by Amendment 2 were
intended to offset some of the upfront cost of installing a compliant
greywater reuse system, in the hope that a lower net cost would encourage
broader voluntary adoption across the town's residential properties.

This document is organized as follows: Section 1 defines key terms;
Section 2 sets out the permit requirement; Section 3 establishes the
baseline irrigation restrictions tied to declared drought stages; Section
4 lists the exceptions to those restrictions; Sections 5 and 6 present
Amendment 1 and Amendment 2 respectively, each amendment stating which
earlier provisions it changes or supersedes; Appendix A sets out the
current fee schedule; and Sections 7 through 10 cover enforcement,
administration, the relationship of this code to other town codes, and
effective dates.

#### Section 1: Definitions

For purposes of this code, "greywater" means household wastewater from
bathroom sinks, showers, bathtubs, and clothes washing machines, and does
not include wastewater from kitchen sinks, dishwashers, or toilets
("blackwater"), nor does it include rainwater collected from roofs or
other surfaces, which is governed by separate town regulations not
addressed in this code. "Greywater reuse system" means any plumbing
configuration that captures greywater and redirects it for outdoor
irrigation use rather than discharging it to the sanitary sewer.
"Outdoor irrigation" means the application of water to lawns, gardens,
landscaping, or other outdoor vegetation, and does not include water used
for indoor purposes, vehicle washing, or industrial or commercial
processes, none of which are addressed by this code. "Registered system"
means a greywater reuse system for which the owner has completed the
registration process described in Section 2 and has been issued a
registration certificate by the town's water resources office.

This code applies to residential properties within Bellhollow town
limits. It does not apply to commercial, industrial, or municipal
properties, whose greywater and water-reuse practices, if any, are
governed by separate provisions not part of this code.

"Drought stage" means one of three escalating water-shortage
classifications declared by the water resources office under the town's
emergency water management plan: stage 1 (mild shortage, voluntary
conservation plus scheduled outdoor-irrigation limits), stage 2
(moderate shortage, mandatory restrictions with narrow exceptions), and
stage 3 (severe shortage, mandatory restrictions with no exceptions).
"Lot size" means the total area of a residential parcel as recorded in
the town assessor's records, expressed in square meters for purposes of
the fee schedule in Appendix A. "Base fee" and "lot-size surcharge" have
the meanings given in Appendix A.

#### Section 2: Permit Requirement

Every property owner installing a greywater reuse system within town
limits must obtain a permit from the water resources office before the
system begins operation. The permit application must include a diagram
of the proposed plumbing configuration, the intended irrigation area, and
an attestation that the system will comply with the restrictions in
Section 3. No exception in Section 4 waives this permit requirement; the
exceptions in Section 4 apply only to the irrigation-timing restrictions
described in Section 3, not to the requirement to obtain a permit before
a system begins operation. A property owner operating a greywater reuse
system without first obtaining a permit under this section is in
violation of this code regardless of whether the water use itself would
otherwise have been permitted.

Permit applications are reviewed within fifteen business days of
submission. Once a permit is issued, the owner may separately apply for
registered-system status, described further in Amendment 1 and Amendment
2 below, which affects the applicable fee under Appendix A but is not a
substitute for the permit itself.

The permit review generally involves a desk review of the submitted
diagram and irrigation-area description; a site visit is only required
if the water resources office identifies a potential conflict with
Section 3's restrictions or if the proposed system serves an unusually
large irrigation area. Most residential applications for a single-family
property are approved on desk review alone. A permit, once issued, does
not expire and does not need to be renewed, though the owner must notify
the water resources office of any material change to the system's
configuration, such as an expansion of the irrigation area beyond what
was originally described in the application.

#### Section 3: Outdoor Irrigation Restrictions

During any period in which the town has declared a drought stage under
its emergency water management plan, outdoor irrigation using greywater
is restricted according to the declared stage. During drought stage 1,
outdoor irrigation using greywater is permitted on the schedule set by
the water resources office, typically limited to designated days of the
week. During drought stage 2, outdoor irrigation using greywater is
prohibited entirely, except as specifically permitted under the
exceptions listed in Section 4 below. During drought stage 3, all
outdoor irrigation, greywater or otherwise, is prohibited without
exception, and Section 4's exceptions do not apply during a declared
stage 3 emergency.

Outside of any declared drought stage, outdoor irrigation using greywater
from a permitted system is allowed without time-of-day or day-of-week
restriction, subject to the general nuisance and runoff provisions of the
town's water code, which are outside the scope of this document.

The water resources office publishes the current drought stage, if any,
on the town's public water-status page, updated whenever the stage
changes. Property owners are responsible for checking the current stage
before irrigating; ignorance of a declared stage is not a defense to an
enforcement action under Section 7. Drought stages are declared based on
reservoir levels, groundwater monitoring data, and seasonal precipitation
forecasts reviewed monthly by the water resources office, and a stage
may be escalated or de-escalated at any monthly review or, in an
emergency, on shorter notice at the director's discretion.

#### Section 4: Exceptions

The following exceptions apply only during a declared drought stage 1 or
stage 2, and only to the irrigation-timing restrictions in Section 3;
none of these exceptions waives the permit requirement in Section 2.

(a) Hand-watering of edible gardens. A property owner may hand-water an
edible garden using greywater during a declared drought stage 1 or stage
2, provided the watering is done by hand using a watering can, hose
nozzle held and operated continuously by a person, or similar
hand-operated method. This exception does not extend to irrigation
delivered by an automated drip system, sprinkler, or any unattended
irrigation method, even if that method is used exclusively on an edible
garden.

(b) Licensed landscaper establishment period. A newly installed
landscape maintained by a licensed landscaping contractor may receive
greywater irrigation during its first ninety days after installation,
regardless of declared drought stage 1 or stage 2, provided the
contractor files an establishment-period notice with the water resources
office before irrigation begins under this exception.

(c) Emergency firefighting use. Greywater may be used without
restriction for emergency firefighting purposes at the direction of the
town fire department, regardless of declared drought stage.

No other exceptions to the Section 3 irrigation-timing restrictions
exist under this code as currently amended. In particular, no exception
exists for ornamental water features, decorative fountains, or any use
not specifically described in subsections (a) through (c) above, and no
exception exists based on financial hardship alone; the exceptions in
this section are defined by the nature of the water use and, where
applicable, the method of application, not by the property owner's
circumstances.

The water resources office may publish additional interpretive guidance
clarifying how these exceptions apply to specific irrigation methods not
explicitly named above, such as soaker hoses or moisture-sensing
controllers, but any such guidance must be consistent with the plain
text of subsections (a) through (c) and cannot itself create a new
exception beyond what the Town Council has adopted by ordinance or
amendment.

#### Section 5: Amendment 1

Amendment 1, adopted by the Town Council to encourage registration of
greywater reuse systems, established the following provisions, effective
as of its adoption date:

(a) Registered-system discount. A registered system, as defined in
Section 1, receives a 15% discount off the combined base fee and
lot-size surcharge described in Appendix A.

(b) Scope clarification. Amendment 1 also clarified that the definition
of "outdoor irrigation" in Section 1 includes irrigation of ornamental
landscaping in addition to lawns and gardens, resolving an ambiguity in
the original ordinance text about whether purely ornamental plantings
were covered.

Amendment 1's scope clarification in subsection (b) remains in effect
and has not been altered by any subsequent amendment. Its discount
provision in subsection (a), however, is addressed by Amendment 2 below.

At the time of its adoption, Amendment 1's registration program was
projected to encourage roughly two hundred additional households to
register their systems over its first two years, based on a survey of
resident interest conducted by the water resources office. Amendment 1
also directed the water resources office to report back to the Town
Council after eighteen months on registration uptake and program cost,
which led directly to the budget review referenced in Amendment 2 below.

#### Section 6: Amendment 2

Amendment 2, adopted by the Town Council after a budget review of the
water resources office's registration-program costs, makes the following
changes:

(a) Replacement of Amendment 1's discount clause. Amendment 2 explicitly
replaces Amendment 1's registered-system discount clause (Section 5,
subsection (a)) in its entirety. Effective as of Amendment 2's adoption
date, a registered system receives a 30% discount off the combined base
fee and lot-size surcharge described in Appendix A, superseding the 15%
figure previously established by Amendment 1. This revised discount
applies to all registered systems as of Amendment 2's effective date,
including systems that registered under Amendment 1's original terms
before Amendment 2 was adopted; Amendment 2 does not grandfather any
system into the superseded 15% rate, and the 15% figure in Section 5(a)
should be read as historical only and is no longer in effect for any
system as of this amendment.

(b) Scope clarification on shared systems. Amendment 2 further clarifies
that a single greywater reuse system serving multiple dwelling units on
one lot (such as a duplex, triplex, or small apartment building) is
treated as one system for purposes of the fee schedule in Appendix A; the
applicable fee depends on lot size and registration status as described
in Appendix A, and does not vary based on the number of dwelling units
served by the system.

Amendment 2 does not alter the permit requirement in Section 2, the
irrigation restrictions in Section 3, or the exceptions in Section 4.

The Town Council's stated rationale for increasing the discount from 15%
to 30% under Amendment 2, despite the budget review that prompted the
amendment, was that registration uptake following Amendment 1 had been
lower than the water resources office's original projection, and the
Council concluded that a larger discount was a more effective incentive
than the smaller one originally set. The budget review's cost analysis
showed that the administrative cost of processing a registration was
largely fixed regardless of the discount percentage applied, so
increasing the discount was not expected to materially increase the
office's per-registration processing cost.

#### Appendix A: Fee Schedule

The permit fee for a greywater reuse system is calculated as follows.

Every system pays a base fee of 60 currency units.

A lot-size surcharge of 35 currency units applies to any property with a
lot size over 800 square meters. Properties at or under 800 square
meters pay no lot-size surcharge.

For a registered system, as defined in Section 1, the combined total of
the base fee and any applicable lot-size surcharge is discounted at the
rate currently in effect under Amendment 2, Section 6(a) — a 30% discount
— rather than the superseded 15% rate originally set by Amendment 1.

For example, a registered system on a lot over 800 square meters pays a
base fee of 60 plus a lot-size surcharge of 35, for a combined subtotal
of 95, discounted by 30% under the currently effective rate, yielding a
final fee. Fee amounts should always be calculated using the currently
effective discount rate under Amendment 2 rather than any earlier rate
that appears elsewhere in this document for historical reference only.

Fees are payable in full at the time of permit issuance and are not
prorated or refunded if a system is later deregistered or removed. The
water resources office may adjust the base fee, surcharge, or discount
rate by future amendment; any such change would be reflected in a
subsequent revision of this appendix and would supersede the figures
above in the same manner that Amendment 2 superseded Amendment 1's
discount rate.

The fee schedule applies uniformly across all residential property
types within the scope of this code, including single-family homes and
multi-unit residential buildings served by a single shared system, per
the clarification in Section 6(b) above. The water resources office
accepts payment by check, money order, or the town's online payment
portal; partial payments are not accepted, and a permit application is
not considered complete until the applicable fee is paid in full. Fee
amounts are reviewed by the water resources office every two years as
part of its budget cycle, though no change to the base fee or surcharge
has been made since the original ordinance's adoption; only the
registered-system discount rate has changed, first by Amendment 1 and
then by Amendment 2 as described above.

#### Section 7: Enforcement and Penalties

A property owner found to be operating a greywater reuse system without
a permit, or violating the irrigation restrictions of Section 3 without
a qualifying exception under Section 4, is subject to a warning for a
first violation and a civil penalty for any subsequent violation within a
twelve-month period. Enforcement is carried out by the water resources
office, which may refer repeated or willful violations to the town code
enforcement division. This code does not authorize enforcement action
against uses not addressed by this code, such as indoor water use,
rainwater harvesting systems, or commercial and industrial water
practices, none of which fall within the scope of this ordinance as
defined in Section 1.

Enforcement actions under this section follow a standard process:
inspection or complaint intake, a documented warning with a stated
corrective period for a first violation, and a civil penalty hearing
before the code enforcement division for any subsequent violation within
the twelve-month look-back period described above. A property owner may
appeal a civil penalty to the code enforcement division within thirty
days of the penalty notice. Nothing in this section limits the water
resources office's discretion to work informally with a property owner
to bring a system into compliance before escalating to a formal
enforcement action, and in practice most first violations are resolved
through the warning process alone without proceeding to a civil penalty.

#### Section 8: Administration

The water resources office is responsible for administering the permit
and registration process described in Sections 2, 5, and 6, for
publishing the currently effective fee schedule under Appendix A, and for
declaring drought stages under the town's emergency water management
plan referenced in Section 3. The office publishes an annual report
summarizing permits issued, systems registered, and enforcement actions
taken under this code, which is presented to the Town Council each
spring alongside the office's budget request.

The water resources office also maintains a public registry of registered
systems, searchable by address, which property buyers and sellers may
consult to confirm whether a system's registered status will transfer
with the property. Registered-system status is tied to the property
rather than to the individual owner, so a new owner of a property with an
existing registered system continues to receive the discount under
Appendix A without needing to re-register, provided the system itself is
not materially altered in a way that would require a new permit
application under Section 2.

Questions about whether a specific use case falls within the scope of
this code, including uses this code does not address, should be directed
to the water resources office for an administrative determination; this
document is not exhaustive of every possible water-use scenario a
property owner might encounter; and where the ordinance is silent, the
water resources office may issue guidance, though such guidance does not
itself amend this code and any actual expansion of scope requires a
future amendment adopted by the Town Council in the same manner as
Amendment 1 and Amendment 2 above.

#### Section 9: Relationship to Other Town Codes

This code addresses only greywater reuse systems and outdoor irrigation
restrictions as defined in Section 1. Rainwater harvesting systems,
including cisterns or rain barrels used to collect roof runoff for any
purpose, indoor or outdoor, are governed by a separate town rainwater
management code and are not addressed by any provision of this document.
Commercial water-reuse practices, including any use of reclaimed water in
vehicle washing, industrial processing, or other commercial operations,
are likewise governed by separate commercial water-use provisions not
addressed by this code. Property owners with questions about water uses
outside the scope defined in Section 1 should consult the relevant
separate code or contact the water resources office directly, since this
document does not speak to those situations one way or the other. This
code does not attempt to enumerate every water-use scenario that falls
outside its scope; the definitions in Section 1 and the description of
covered uses above are the governing test for whether a given scenario
falls within or outside this code, not any illustrative list.

#### Section 10: Effective Dates

The original ordinance took effect thirty days after adoption. Amendment
1 took effect on its own adoption date, several years after the original
ordinance. Amendment 2 took effect on its own adoption date, approximately
eighteen months after Amendment 1. All provisions in this consolidated
document reflect the code as currently in effect, with historical rates
and clauses that have since been superseded retained in the text only for
reference and explicitly marked as superseded where applicable, as with
Amendment 1's discount rate in Section 5(a).

This consolidated document is republished by the water resources office
whenever a new amendment is adopted, so that residents, contractors, and
town staff can consult a single current text rather than needing to
cross-reference the original ordinance against a separate list of
amendments. Prior consolidated versions, including the version published
after Amendment 1 but before Amendment 2, remain available in the town
clerk's public records archive for historical reference, but the version
currently in effect is always the most recently published consolidated
text, which is this document.

## Scenarios

- **Scenario 1**: A homeowner wants to install a residential greywater
  reuse system on a 600 square meter lot and does not plan to register
  the system. Is this permitted, and what fee applies?
- **Scenario 2**: A homeowner installs a residential greywater reuse
  system on a 950 square meter lot and registers it under the currently
  effective registration program. Is this permitted, and what fee
  applies?
- **Scenario 3**: During a declared drought stage 2, a homeowner wants to
  irrigate an edible garden using greywater delivered through an
  automated drip-irrigation system, relying on the hand-watering
  exception. Is this permitted?
- **Scenario 4**: A commercial car-wash operator wants to know whether
  this code permits reusing reclaimed water for vehicle rinsing at their
  business. Is this permitted?
- **Scenario 5**: A homeowner wants to install a rainwater-harvesting
  cistern to supply an indoor toilet-flushing system. Is this permitted
  under this code?
- **Scenario 6**: A homeowner registered their greywater reuse system
  under Amendment 1's original terms, before Amendment 2 was adopted.
  Their lot is 820 square meters. What discount rate applies to them now
  that Amendment 2 is in effect?
- **Scenario 7**: A homeowner installed a greywater reuse system without
  obtaining a permit under Section 2, and now claims the situation is
  covered by a "temporary drought hardship," pointing to the Section 4
  exceptions. Is this permitted?
- **Scenario 8**: A small apartment building with 6 units shares a single
  registered greywater reuse system. The combined lot is 1,200 square
  meters. Is this permitted, and what fee applies?
