### Kestrel Line Overland Survey — Expedition Logbook

#### Day 1

The Kestrel Line survey convoy departed the forward staging depot at
first light, four vehicles in a loose column with the lead vehicle
carrying the survey team's chief geologist, a careful, soft-spoken man
named Aurelio Bosch who had mapped two prior corridors in the same
region over the past decade. The convoy's first waypoint of the entire
survey, logged as the column cleared the depot's perimeter fence, was
recorded as waypoint WP-114C, marking the official start of the
corridor being surveyed on this expedition. The morning was cool and
overcast, and the lead driver reported the gravel track in good
condition for the first several kilometers, a welcome change from the
washboard surface that had slowed the previous year's expedition to a
crawl in the same stretch.

At the first scheduled halt, the expedition's logistics officer,
Marguerite Aoun, conducted the formal count of the convoy's starting
provisions, a task she performed methodically at the outset of every
expedition regardless of how many times the depot's own paperwork had
already listed the same figures. Her count confirmed 318 ration packs
loaded across the four vehicles, matching the depot's manifest exactly
and giving the team a clean baseline before the real work of the
survey began. Marguerite noted in her own log that this baseline count
would matter later, since ration consumption against elapsed days was
one of the figures she was responsible for tracking through the whole
expedition, and any discrepancy at resupply would need to be traced
back to this exact starting number.

The rest of Day 1 passed uneventfully by the standards of a
multi-week overland survey, though not without its small dramas. The
convoy covered a modest distance by early afternoon, well short of the
day's planned target, after the second vehicle developed a slow leak
in a rear tire that required a roadside change under a sun that had by
then burned off the morning's cloud cover entirely. Aurelio used the
delay to walk a short distance off the track and examine an outcrop
that had caught his eye from the vehicle window, noting in his
geological log that the rock looked consistent with formations he'd
catalogued on a previous expedition through a different corridor
entirely, though he was careful to flag this as a preliminary
impression rather than a confirmed correlation until he could examine
a proper sample under better conditions than a roadside tire change
afforded.

The team made camp a little later than planned, but in good spirits,
with Marguerite noting that the tire delay had cost them perhaps forty
minutes against the day's schedule, nothing serious this early in a
multi-week survey. Dinner that first night was the traditional
"departure stew," a recipe the expedition's cook, a wiry, cheerful man
named Osei Danquah, insisted on preparing on the first night of every
expedition he'd ever cooked for, regardless of how many times his
colleagues teased him about the superstition behind it. Osei claimed
the tradition brought good luck to a survey's opening days, and while
nobody on the team seriously believed this, nobody was willing to be
the one who broke the tradition and blamed the tire delay on it
either.

#### Day 2

The second day began with a full mechanical check of all four
vehicles, standard practice after any tire issue the previous day, and
the team found nothing else of concern beyond the tire that had
already been swapped. The convoy made good progress through terrain
that gradually shifted from open scrubland to a more broken, rocky
landscape, requiring more careful route-finding than Day 1's
relatively straightforward track. Aurelio spent much of the day on
foot alongside the slow-moving vehicles, examining exposures along the
route and building a running log of formation types that he expected
would form the backbone of the survey's eventual geological summary
report, a document he was already mentally drafting well before the
expedition's midpoint, a habit he'd developed over many years of field
work as a way of not losing the thread of an argument he might not
write up formally for months.

By the end of Day 2, the convoy had covered a combined distance from
the depot that Marguerite recorded against the day's fuel consumption
figures, part of her ongoing effort to build a reliable
distance-per-liter figure for this specific vehicle loadout under
these specific terrain conditions, a number that would help planning
for future expeditions through similar corridors. She noted that the
figure was still too early to be reliable, since a single day's rough
terrain could skew it significantly compared to a longer averaged
figure, and she planned to revisit the calculation once more days of
data had accumulated across a wider variety of terrain types.

The evening brought the team's first proper equipment inventory since
departure, a routine Marguerite ran every second evening regardless of
how uneventful the intervening days had been, cross-checking each
vehicle's cargo against the master manifest compiled back at the
depot before the convoy ever left. She found everything accounted for,
a small relief given how easily gear could shift or go missing during
rough travel, and closed her log for the day with a note that the
expedition remained fully on schedule despite the previous day's tire
delay, the forty minutes lost having been more than recovered by Day
2's smoother travel.

#### Day 3

Day 3 brought the survey's first extended geological transect, a
planned detour off the main corridor track to examine a ridge system
Aurelio had flagged as significant during the expedition's planning
phase, based on satellite imagery reviewed months earlier back at the
home institute. The detour added several hours to the day's travel but
was considered essential to the survey's scientific goals, and Aurelio
spent the bulk of the day collecting samples and recording
strike-and-dip measurements across the ridge's exposed face, working
methodically from one end of the exposure to the other with a
patience the rest of the team had come to expect from him on previous
expeditions.

The rest of the team used the extended stop to perform routine vehicle
maintenance and to reorganize cargo that had shifted during the
previous two days' rough travel, a task that took longer than expected
once the crew realized several fuel containers had been packed in an
order that made the most frequently accessed ones the hardest to
reach. Marguerite used part of the day to cross-check the ration count
against consumption so far, confirming the team was tracking close to
the planned consumption rate for this stage of the expedition, with no
significant deviation worth flagging in her log.

The evening camp was set up earlier than the previous two nights,
giving the team a rare opportunity for a proper hot meal cooked over
the camp stove rather than the quicker rations typical of a long
travel day. Osei took the opportunity to experiment with a spice
combination he'd been wanting to try since before departure, a small
gamble that paid off well enough that two of the junior surveyors
asked him to write the recipe down for them before the expedition
ended, a request he promised to honor once he'd refined the
proportions a little further on a future attempt.

#### Day 4

The fourth day returned the convoy to the main corridor track after
the ridge detour, and travel resumed at a steadier pace through
terrain that Aurelio described as broadly similar to what the team had
crossed on Day 2. No major incidents were recorded, though the convoy
did pause briefly when the third vehicle's radiator temperature gauge
briefly spiked on a steep grade, prompting a short rest to let the
engine cool before continuing; the gauge returned to normal readings
within minutes and no further issue was noted for the remainder of the
expedition with that vehicle, much to the relief of its driver, who
had spent the rest of the climb watching the gauge more closely than
the road ahead.

Late in the day, the team reached the site selected for the
expedition's first extended base camp, where they planned to remain
for two nights to allow a wider radius of foot-based survey work
around a fixed point rather than continuing to advance the convoy
daily. Aurelio was visibly pleased with the site's geological
diversity within easy walking distance, describing it in his log as
"about as good a base camp as this corridor was ever going to offer,"
a judgment the rest of the team took as high praise given how rarely
Aurelio allowed himself unqualified enthusiasm about anything before a
full survey of an area had actually been completed.

Setting up the extended base camp took longer than a normal overnight
stop, since the team erected the larger communal tent used for
stationary stops of more than one night, along with a proper equipment
shelter to keep sensitive gear out of the weather during the two days
of foot-based work ahead. Marguerite oversaw the setup personally,
having learned on a previous expedition that an improperly staked
communal tent could become a serious problem if the wind picked up
overnight, an experience she was in no hurry to repeat on this survey
or any future one.

#### Day 5

With the convoy stationary at the extended base camp, the survey team
split into two smaller groups for the day, each covering a different
radius of foot-based transects around the camp. Aurelio led the first
group toward a formation visible from camp that he'd been eager to
examine since spotting it the previous evening, while a second,
smaller group under one of the junior surveyors, Tomasz Iversen,
covered a wider but geologically less promising area to the north,
mostly to rule it out for future planning purposes rather than in
expectation of significant findings, a task Tomasz approached with
good humor despite knowing it was unlikely to produce anything
noteworthy for the survey's eventual report.

Fuel and supply management continued as a background task throughout
the stationary day, running alongside the foot-survey work rather than
pausing for it. The expedition's generator, used to recharge field
equipment batteries and power the base camp's lighting overnight, was
checked and topped off during the afternoon by Marguerite personally,
with the fuel gauge reading recorded at 156 liters remaining in the
generator's dedicated tank after the top-off, a figure she logged
carefully since the generator's fuel supply was separate from the
vehicles' own tanks and needed independent tracking against the days
remaining before the expedition's first scheduled resupply. She noted
that this reading, taken here at the extended base camp, would be her
reference point for calculating the generator's burn rate over the
following stretch of the expedition, a calculation she planned to
revisit once the team had moved on and the generator had run for
several more consistent days without a top-off to distort the figures.

The two foot-survey groups reconvened at camp well after dark, both
reporting a productive if unremarkable day. Tomasz's northern group
had, as expected, found little of geological interest, though one of
his surveyors had spotted an old, weathered trail marker of unknown
origin that nobody on the team could immediately place against any
historical survey records they carried, a small mystery Tomasz noted
in his log mostly out of curiosity rather than any expectation it
would matter to the expedition's actual findings.

#### Day 6

The second day at the extended base camp continued the foot-survey
work from Day 5, with the two groups largely swapping areas so that
Tomasz's team could examine the formation Aurelio's group had covered
the previous day, while Aurelio himself pushed slightly further north
than the previous day's northern group had gone, curious about the
weathered trail marker Tomasz had mentioned the night before. The
exchange proved useful: Tomasz's team found a small but interesting
mineral vein Aurelio's group had apparently walked past without
noticing the previous day, a detail Aurelio was good-humored about
when it was pointed out to him back at camp that evening, admitting
that a second pair of eyes on the same ground was exactly why the
team rotated coverage between stationary days in the first place.

The expedition's radio operator, a quiet man named Bassim Farhan who
had joined the team specifically for this expedition after two
previous surveys with a different outfit, maintained the scheduled
check-in with the forward staging depot as he did every evening,
reporting the team's status and general location without incident. He
noted in his own log that atmospheric conditions had made the
connection unusually clear that evening, a small detail he recorded
mostly out of habit, since propagation conditions varied enough from
night to night that tracking them occasionally proved useful for
planning future expeditions' communication windows, particularly for
expeditions through corridors with similarly uneven terrain shadowing
the radio signal.

Marguerite also logged a second generator fuel check this same
evening, a follow-up to the previous day's top-off now that the unit
had run for a full day powering the base camp's lighting and battery
charging overnight. The gauge read 158 liters, a small increase
Marguerite attributed to a slightly imprecise gauge reading rather
than any real change, since the generator had not been refueled
between the two checks; she noted the apparent rise as an example of
the gauge's normal measurement noise rather than a meaningful
discrepancy worth investigating further, and used the previous day's
figure as her authoritative reference point for the burn-rate
calculation going forward.

Aurelio, for his part, spent a good portion of the evening examining
the mineral vein Tomasz's team had found, cautiously optimistic that
it might be worth a more thorough sampling pass before the convoy
moved on the following morning, a plan he ran past the rest of the
team over dinner and which was met with general enthusiasm, since it
meant one more night at a base camp everyone had, by this point, grown
rather fond of.

#### Day 7

The convoy departed the extended base camp on the morning of Day 7,
resuming its advance along the main corridor track after two
productive stationary days, though not before Aurelio spent a final
hour at first light taking the extra samples from the mineral vein he
had discussed the previous evening, a decision that delayed departure
only slightly and which nobody seriously objected to given how
promising the vein looked in the morning light. Before departure, the
team's communications role for the expedition was formally logged for
the first time in this logbook, though Bassim had of course been
performing the role since Day 1: the expedition's comms officer for
this survey, tasked with all scheduled check-ins and any emergency
communications, is Petra Lindqvist, working alongside Bassim's
radio operation duties in a combined communications role the
expedition's staffing plan had assigned to the two of them jointly.
Petra's specific responsibility, as distinct from Bassim's
equipment-focused radio work, covered message drafting, check-in
scheduling, and liaison with the forward depot's own communications
desk, a division of labor the two of them had worked out between
themselves during the depot staging days before the expedition ever
departed.

Travel on Day 7 was uneventful, with the convoy making solid progress
along a stretch of track that Aurelio noted matched the satellite
imagery closely, a small reassurance given how often imagery and
ground truth had diverged on previous expeditions through similarly
remote corridors, sometimes badly enough to require an unplanned
detour when a mapped track turned out to have washed out entirely
since the imagery was captured. The evening camp was a simple one,
set up quickly to make the most of the remaining daylight for a final
equipment check before what the team expected to be a more demanding
stretch of travel over the following two days, based on the terrain
visible in the planning imagery for the section immediately ahead.

Osei, meanwhile, had begun quietly rationing the more interesting
spices in the camp kitchen's supply, having realized partway through
the expedition that his earlier experimentation on Day 3 had used more
of one particular ingredient than he'd budgeted for the whole trip, a
minor logistical error he was determined to manage discreetly rather
than admit to the team and risk becoming a running joke for the
remainder of the survey.

#### Day 8

Day 8 lived up to the previous evening's expectation, with the convoy
crossing a stretch of badly eroded track that required careful,
low-speed driving and, on two occasions, brief manual clearing of
loose rock that had washed across the route since the corridor was
last surveyed. Progress was slower than any previous day of the
expedition, and Marguerite's running fuel-consumption figures for the
vehicles showed a noticeable uptick for the day, consistent with the
additional low-gear driving the terrain demanded, a figure she noted
without particular alarm since the expedition's fuel planning had
built in a margin for exactly this kind of demanding stretch.

Aurelio, riding in the lead vehicle for this stretch rather than
walking alongside as he often did on easier terrain, used the slower
pace to record detailed notes on exposed rock faces visible from the
track itself, building out his geological log even without the chance
to stop and sample directly. He remarked that the day's slow progress
at least gave him an unusually thorough windshield survey of a
stretch he might otherwise have passed too quickly to document well,
a small silver lining he was quick to point out to the rest of the
team once it became clear the day's schedule was slipping regardless
of anyone's efforts to keep pace with the original plan.

By evening, the team had cleared perhaps two-thirds of the eroded
stretch that the planning imagery had flagged as a likely trouble
spot, and Marguerite's log for the day noted the team's intention to
push through the remainder early the following morning while the
drivers were fresh, rather than risk the same low-speed, high-attention
driving late in the day when fatigue might turn a manageable obstacle
into something more serious.

The evening camp that night was a subdued one, the whole team
noticeably more tired than on any previous evening of the expedition,
and conversation over dinner ran shorter than usual before people
began drifting off to their tents. Osei kept the meal simple and
quick to prepare, sensing correctly that nobody had the energy for
anything more ambitious after a day spent alternately gripping the
wheel through loose rock and clearing debris by hand in the heat.
Petra's evening check-in with the forward depot was likewise brief,
limited to the essential status update rather than the more detailed
exchange that had characterized some of the smoother earlier evenings,
and Bassim noted in his own log that the depot's operator on duty that
night seemed to sense the team's fatigue and kept the exchange
efficient without any unnecessary chatter.

Tomasz, despite the long day, used a few minutes before turning in to
check the samples collected during the Day 3 and Day 5-6 stops,
making sure nothing had been jostled loose or damaged during the
day's rough driving, a precaution he'd taken to heart after hearing
about a previous expedition that had lost a portion of its sample
collection to exactly this kind of oversight on a similarly difficult
stretch of track. He found everything secure and turned in himself
shortly after, glad the following day's stretch of eroded track was
expected to be shorter than the one just completed.

#### Day 9

The eroded stretch continued into the morning of Day 9 before finally
giving way to easier terrain by midday, much to the relief of the
drivers who had spent two full days navigating carefully around loose
rock and washed-out sections. The convoy made up some of the lost time
once the track improved, though Marguerite noted in her log that the
two slow days had eaten meaningfully into the schedule buffer the
expedition had built in during planning, a buffer she'd been quietly
tracking since Day 1 for exactly this kind of contingency, and one she
was now watching more closely than she had at any earlier point in the
survey.

It was on this day, the 9th of Aurelmonth by the expedition's own
calendar convention (a fictional in-story calendar used consistently
throughout this logbook), that the team recovered an item of note
along the improved stretch of track: a piece of survey equipment,
evidently left behind by an earlier expedition through this same
corridor, its equipment tag still legible despite years of weathering.
The recovered unit was logged under its printed serial, TR-88214-B,
and Aurelio noted with interest that the model matched equipment used
by a survey outfit that had, as far as he knew, not operated in this
region for a considerable number of years, making the find something
of a small mystery the team did not attempt to resolve further, beyond
noting it for the expedition's final report as a curiosity rather than
a scientific finding.

The rest of Day 9 passed without further incident, the convoy settling
back into the steady rhythm of travel that had characterized the
earlier, easier stretches of the corridor before the erosion zone
disrupted it. Petra's evening check-in with the forward depot reported
the equipment recovery as a minor item of interest alongside the
routine status update, more out of thoroughness than any expectation
the depot would have follow-up questions about a decades-old piece of
abandoned gear from an unrelated survey outfit.

The afternoon's easier driving also gave the lead driver a chance to
rotate tires between two of the vehicles, a routine maintenance task
that had been on Marguerite's schedule since before departure but
which the erosion zone's demanding conditions had made impractical to
perform safely at the roadside. The rotation went smoothly, and the
driver reported all four vehicles in good working order afterward,
with no wear patterns severe enough to cause concern before the
expedition's next scheduled maintenance window back at the depot
following the return transport. Osei used the same stretch of easier
travel to restock the camp kitchen's water supply from one of the
vehicle's larger tanks, topping off the smaller containers used for
daily cooking so that the following morning's breakfast preparation
would not be delayed by a supply run first thing.

Aurelio spent a good part of the evening turning the recovered
equipment over in his hands by lamplight, comparing its housing and
dial layout against a reference catalog of older survey instruments he
happened to carry in his personal field library, a habit some of the
younger team members found endearing and others found faintly
excessive given how unlikely it was that the exercise would produce
any conclusion beyond what he'd already noted in the log. He
eventually satisfied himself that the unit was, as he'd first
suspected, a model retired from production well before the current
expedition's youngest member had even been born, and packed it
carefully away for transport back to the home institute, where he
thought a colleague specializing in the history of regional survey
equipment might find it a mildly interesting addition to a small
collection of similar curiosities gathered over the years.

Tomasz, meanwhile, used the quiet evening to get a head start on
organizing the sample log for the stretch of track just completed,
cross-referencing collection points against the convoy's odometer
readings so that the eventual final report would be able to place
every sample precisely along the surveyed corridor rather than relying
on rougher day-by-day location estimates. It was tedious work, and he
admitted in his own log that he found it considerably less exciting
than the fieldwork itself, but necessary all the same for a report
that the home institute would expect to hold up to scrutiny from
other researchers who might want to revisit specific sites in a future
survey.

Marguerite closed the day's log with a brief note on morale, observing
that the team seemed to have shaken off the fatigue of the erosion
zone more quickly than she'd expected, helped along by the small
novelty of the equipment recovery and the general relief of easier
driving ahead. She also used the evening to recalculate her running
fuel-consumption figures now that a full stretch of both difficult and
easy terrain had been logged, arriving at a blended distance-per-liter
figure she considered far more reliable than the early, single-day
estimates from Day 2, and which she planned to use for the remainder
of the expedition's fuel planning rather than continuing to refine it
further unless conditions changed significantly.

Bassim, before turning in, ran a final equipment check on the radio
gear ahead of the following day's travel, a habit he kept up
regardless of how routine the previous several check-ins had been,
reasoning that the cost of a quick nightly check was trivial compared
to the trouble an undetected fault could cause if it went unnoticed
until an actual emergency communication was needed. He found nothing
amiss, logged the check as routine, and noted that the team was now
more than halfway through the expedition's planned itinerary by his
own rough count of days elapsed against days remaining, a small
milestone he mentioned to Petra before they both turned in for the
night.

#### Day 10

With the eroded stretch fully behind them, Day 10 saw the convoy make
strong progress along a wide, well-maintained section of the corridor
track, the best driving conditions of the expedition so far according
to the lead driver, who had grown visibly more relaxed after two
grinding days navigating the earlier washouts. Aurelio used the
smoother travel to catch up on log entries he'd deferred during the
slower, more demanding days, cross-referencing his rock-face notes
from Day 8 against fresh samples collected during a brief stop midday,
satisfying himself that the windshield-survey notes had held up well
against direct examination once he finally had the chance to compare
them properly.

The evening check-in, handled jointly by Petra and Bassim as had
become routine since Day 7, reported the team's position and status to
the forward depot without incident, and Petra noted in her own log
that this was the smoothest check-in of the expedition so far, with no
repeated transmissions needed to get a clean signal through, unlike
several earlier evenings when atmospheric conditions or terrain
shadowing had required a second attempt before the message went
through cleanly.

Osei marked the smoother day with a slightly more ambitious dinner
than the expedition's usual travel-day fare, drawing on the spice
supply he'd been quietly rationing since Day 7 a little more freely
than his self-imposed budget technically allowed, reasoning that
morale mattered enough after the previous two demanding days to
justify the small indulgence, a decision the rest of the team
appreciated without ever learning about the rationing that had
preceded it.

Tomasz spent part of the smoother afternoon compiling his own running
notes from the two stationary days at the extended base camp,
cross-referencing his sketches of the mineral vein against Aurelio's
more detailed sampling notes to make sure the two records agreed on
the vein's approximate extent. He found a minor discrepancy in his
own pacing measurements, off by what he estimated was no more than a
few meters, and corrected his sketch accordingly rather than let the
small error propagate into the expedition's final report. Aurelio,
when shown the correction, agreed it was a sensible fix and thanked
Tomasz for catching it before the notes were finalized, remarking that
this kind of quiet cross-checking between team members was exactly
why the expedition's records tended to hold up well under later
scrutiny back at the home institute.

The convoy's mechanical condition remained a running concern for the
lead driver despite the smoother terrain, and he used part of the
afternoon's easier driving to listen carefully for any unusual noise
from the third vehicle, the one whose radiator gauge had spiked
briefly back on Day 4. He reported nothing of concern by evening,
noting that the vehicle had performed without complaint through both
the Day 8-9 erosion zone and the smoother terrain since, and that
whatever had caused the brief temperature spike on Day 4 appeared to
have been a one-time issue with the grade rather than a developing
mechanical problem worth flagging for the depot's maintenance crew
ahead of the next expedition.

Bassim used part of the evening, after the joint check-in with Petra,
to perform routine maintenance on the radio equipment itself, cleaning
a connector that had been giving him a faint intermittent crackle on
receive for the past day or two. The fix seemed to resolve the issue
entirely, and he noted with satisfaction that the equipment had given
the expedition no serious trouble at all across the ten days since
departure, a record he attributed partly to careful maintenance and
partly to simple good fortune, since field radio equipment could be
unpredictable even with the best care.

Marguerite closed out Day 10's log with a broader review of the
expedition's progress against the original schedule, noting that
while the Day 8-9 erosion zone had cost real time, the smoother
driving since had recovered a meaningful portion of it, and that the
buffer she'd been protecting since Day 1 remained intact enough that
she was no longer worried about the expedition's ability to reach the
corridor's terminus and still make the planned return-transport
pickup window without requesting an extension from the forward depot.

#### Day 11

Day 11 brought the survey's second major detour, this time to examine
a formation south of the main track that Aurelio considered second in
importance only to the Day 3 ridge system for the expedition's overall
scientific goals. The detour required careful route-finding across
terrain with no established track at all, relying entirely on
Aurelio's satellite-imagery planning and the lead driver's careful
reading of the ground ahead, a slow and exacting process that tested
the whole team's patience more than any single stretch of the main
corridor track had managed to.

The team reached the formation by early afternoon and spent the
remainder of daylight on sample collection and measurement, with
Aurelio visibly energized by what he called "the best exposure I've
seen on this whole survey" in his log entry for the day, a claim that
prompted some good-natured skepticism from Tomasz given how many times
Aurelio had said something similar about earlier finds, though even
Tomasz admitted after seeing the formation himself that this one
seemed to genuinely deserve the praise.

Marguerite, meanwhile, continued her routine tracking of provisions
and fuel, noting no significant deviations from the expedition's
planned consumption curve despite the demanding off-track driving, a
testament, she wrote, to the buffer built into the original planning
figures even after the schedule pressure introduced by the Day 8-9
washouts. She also used the evening to plan the following day's return
to the main track, working out the most efficient route back given how
far off the established corridor the detour had taken the convoy.

#### Day 12

Returning to the main corridor track after the Day 11 detour, the
convoy reached a waypoint the survey plan had flagged well in advance
as a key reference point for the corridor's southern half: waypoint
52-J07-S, logged as the convoy passed it in the early afternoon. This
waypoint marked, per the original survey plan reviewed by the whole
team before departure, the approximate three-quarter point of the
corridor being surveyed on this expedition, with a final push toward
the corridor's terminus still to come over the following days.

The team also marked, from this waypoint, a measured distance back to
the ridge system examined on Day 3, using the convoy's odometer
readings cross-referenced against the waypoint logs from both days:
the distance from the Day 3 ridge system to this waypoint was recorded
as 41.6 km along the traveled route, a figure Marguerite noted
primarily for the expedition's final route-summary map rather than for
any operational purpose during the remaining travel. She double-checked
the figure against a second odometer reading before logging it, a habit
she'd developed after a discrepancy on a previous expedition had cost
the team an afternoon of confused backtracking.

The evening at this waypoint was a relatively relaxed one, with the
team taking stock of how much of the corridor remained and Aurelio
reviewing his full sample collection so far against his original
sampling plan, satisfied that the survey was on track to meet its
scientific goals even after the schedule pressure of the erosion zone
earlier in the trip.

#### Day 13

Day 13 continued the push toward the corridor's terminus, with travel
conditions remaining favorable and no incidents of note beyond a brief
stop to assist a support vehicle from a different, unrelated survey
team the convoy happened to encounter coming the other direction,
exchanging brief pleasantries and a rough condition report for the
track each team had already covered, a small and pleasant surprise
given how rarely the corridor saw any other traffic at all during this
season. Aurelio used the day's otherwise uneventful travel to finalize
his notes from the Day 11 detour, cross-referencing sample numbers
against his field sketches before the details faded from memory, a
task he preferred to complete within a few days of collection whenever
the schedule allowed it.

Elsewhere in the day's records, the team logged a separate distance
measurement unrelated to the Day 12 figure: the distance from a
different reference point, an old survey cairn noted on the original
corridor maps, back to the Day 3 ridge system, came out to 41.2 km by
the same odometer cross-referencing method, a figure Marguerite
recorded alongside the Day 12 measurement in her route-summary notes
without further comment, since the two figures described different

The evening brought another routine equipment inventory, the first
