### Town of Duskvale — Residential Small Wind Turbine and Backyard Generation Code

Adopted by the Duskvale Town Council as Ordinance No. 337, this code
governs the installation, registration, and operation of residential
small wind turbines within town limits, and establishes restrictions on
turbine operation during declared noise-advisory levels. This document
consolidates the original ordinance with Amendment 1 and Amendment 2,
both adopted by subsequent council action, and includes the fee schedule
currently in effect at Appendix A. Where a later amendment conflicts with
an earlier provision, the later amendment controls, as each amendment
below states explicitly which prior clauses it supersedes.

Duskvale adopted this code after a cluster of neighbor complaints about
rotor noise from early backyard turbines placed strain on the town's
nuisance-mediation process. The Town Council's stated goal in adopting
the original ordinance was to encourage responsible residential
generation from small wind turbines while preserving the town's ability
to impose stricter operating limits during periods of heightened
noise sensitivity, such as overnight hours near dense housing. The
registration and rebate provisions added by Amendment 1 and later revised
by Amendment 2 were intended to offset some of the upfront cost of
installing a compliant turbine, in the hope that a lower net cost would
encourage broader voluntary adoption across the town's residential
properties.

This document is organized as follows: Section 1 defines key terms;
Section 2 sets out the permit requirement; Section 3 establishes the
baseline operating restrictions tied to declared noise-advisory levels;
Section 4 lists the exceptions to those restrictions; Sections 5 and 6
present Amendment 1 and Amendment 2 respectively, each amendment stating
which earlier provisions it changes or supersedes; Appendix A sets out
the current fee schedule; and Sections 7 through 10 cover enforcement,
administration, the relationship of this code to other town codes, and
effective dates.

#### Section 1: Definitions

For purposes of this code, "small wind turbine" means a horizontal- or
vertical-axis wind-driven generator mounted on a residential parcel and
rated at or below ten kilowatts, used to generate electricity for
on-site residential use, and does not include utility-scale or
commercial wind installations, nor rooftop photovoltaic solar arrays,
which are governed by separate town regulations not addressed in this
code. "Rotor diameter" means the diameter of the circle swept by the
turbine's blades, expressed in meters for purposes of the fee schedule in
Appendix A. "Turbine operation" means the turbine spinning under wind
load to generate electricity, and does not include a stationary or
feathered turbine, a turbine undergoing manual maintenance, or energy
storage, none of which are addressed by the operating restrictions in
this code. "Registered turbine" means a small wind turbine for which the
owner has completed the registration process described in Section 2 and
has been issued a registration certificate by the town's building and
zoning office.

This code applies to residential properties within Duskvale town limits.
It does not apply to commercial, industrial, or municipal properties,
whose generation and turbine practices, if any, are governed by separate
provisions not part of this code.

"Noise-advisory level" means one of three escalating sound-sensitivity
classifications declared by the building and zoning office under the
town's community noise management plan: level 1 (mild sensitivity,
voluntary quieting plus scheduled overnight operating limits), level 2
(moderate sensitivity, mandatory daytime-only restrictions with narrow
exceptions), and level 3 (high sensitivity, mandatory shutdown with no
exceptions). "Rated capacity" means the manufacturer's nameplate power
rating of the turbine. "Base fee" and "rotor-diameter surcharge" have the
meanings given in Appendix A.

#### Section 2: Permit Requirement

Every property owner installing a small wind turbine within town limits
must obtain a permit from the building and zoning office before the
turbine begins operation. The permit application must include a diagram
of the proposed tower and rotor placement, the intended setback from lot
lines, and an attestation that the turbine will comply with the
restrictions in Section 3. No exception in Section 4 waives this permit
requirement; the exceptions in Section 4 apply only to the
operating-time restrictions described in Section 3, not to the
requirement to obtain a permit before a turbine begins operation. A
property owner operating a small wind turbine without first obtaining a
permit under this section is in violation of this code regardless of
whether the operation itself would otherwise have been permitted.

Permit applications are reviewed within twenty business days of
submission. Once a permit is issued, the owner may separately apply for
registered-turbine status, described further in Amendment 1 and Amendment
2 below, which affects the applicable fee under Appendix A but is not a
substitute for the permit itself.

The permit review generally involves a desk review of the submitted
diagram and setback description; a site visit is only required if the
building and zoning office identifies a potential conflict with Section
3's restrictions or if the proposed tower is unusually tall. Most
residential applications for a single-family property are approved on
desk review alone. A permit, once issued, does not expire and does not
need to be renewed, though the owner must notify the building and zoning
office of any material change to the turbine's configuration, such as a
replacement rotor larger than what was originally described in the
application.

#### Section 3: Operating Restrictions

During any period in which the town has declared a noise-advisory level
under its community noise management plan, small wind turbine operation
is restricted according to the declared level. During noise-advisory
level 1, turbine operation is permitted on the schedule set by the
building and zoning office, typically with operation curtailed during
designated overnight hours. During noise-advisory level 2, turbine
operation is prohibited outside daylight hours entirely, except as
specifically permitted under the exceptions listed in Section 4 below.
During noise-advisory level 3, all turbine operation is prohibited
without exception, and Section 4's exceptions do not apply during a
declared level 3 advisory.

Outside of any declared noise-advisory level, small wind turbine
operation from a permitted turbine is allowed without time-of-day or
day-of-week restriction, subject to the general nuisance and setback
provisions of the town's zoning code, which are outside the scope of this
document.

The building and zoning office publishes the current noise-advisory
level, if any, on the town's public advisory page, updated whenever the
level changes. Property owners are responsible for checking the current
level before operating; ignorance of a declared level is not a defense to
an enforcement action under Section 7. Noise-advisory levels are declared
based on measured ambient sound data, seasonal occupancy patterns, and
the density of nearby housing reviewed monthly by the building and zoning
office, and a level may be escalated or de-escalated at any monthly
review or, in an emergency, on shorter notice at the director's
discretion.

#### Section 4: Exceptions

The following exceptions apply only during a declared noise-advisory
level 1 or level 2, and only to the operating-time restrictions in
Section 3; none of these exceptions waives the permit requirement in
Section 2.

(a) Manual maintenance spin-test. A property owner may spin a turbine
briefly for a maintenance test during a declared noise-advisory level 1
or level 2, provided the test is initiated and supervised in person by a
person present at the turbine, using a manual release or hand-operated
control held and operated by that person. This exception does not extend
to operation initiated by an automated timer, a remote scheduler, or any
unattended control system, even if that operation is nominally labeled a
maintenance test.

(b) Certified installer commissioning period. A newly installed turbine
commissioned by a certified wind installer may operate during its first
sixty days after installation, regardless of declared noise-advisory
level 1 or level 2, provided the installer files a commissioning-period
notice with the building and zoning office before operation begins under
this exception.

(c) Emergency backup power. A turbine may operate without restriction to
supply emergency backup power at the direction of the town emergency
management office, regardless of declared noise-advisory level.

No other exceptions to the Section 3 operating-time restrictions exist
under this code as currently amended. In particular, no exception exists
for grid-export or feed-in operation, for battery top-up, or for any use
not specifically described in subsections (a) through (c) above, and no
exception exists based on financial hardship alone; the exceptions in
this section are defined by the nature of the operation and, where
applicable, the method of control, not by the property owner's
circumstances.

The building and zoning office may publish additional interpretive
guidance clarifying how these exceptions apply to specific control
methods not explicitly named above, such as wind-speed governors or
manual furling controls, but any such guidance must be consistent with
the plain text of subsections (a) through (c) and cannot itself create a
new exception beyond what the Town Council has adopted by ordinance or
amendment.

#### Section 5: Amendment 1

Amendment 1, adopted by the Town Council to encourage registration of
small wind turbines, established the following provisions, effective as
of its adoption date:

(a) Registered-turbine rebate. A registered turbine, as defined in
Section 1, receives a 20% rebate off the combined base fee and
rotor-diameter surcharge described in Appendix A.

(b) Scope clarification. Amendment 1 also clarified that the definition
of "small wind turbine" in Section 1 includes vertical-axis turbines in
addition to horizontal-axis turbines, resolving an ambiguity in the
original ordinance text about whether vertical-axis designs were covered.

Amendment 1's scope clarification in subsection (b) remains in effect and
has not been altered by any subsequent amendment. Its rebate provision in
subsection (a), however, is addressed by Amendment 2 below.

At the time of its adoption, Amendment 1's registration program was
projected to encourage roughly one hundred fifty additional households to
register their turbines over its first two years, based on a survey of
resident interest conducted by the building and zoning office. Amendment
1 also directed the building and zoning office to report back to the Town
Council after eighteen months on registration uptake and program cost,
which led directly to the budget review referenced in Amendment 2 below.

#### Section 6: Amendment 2

Amendment 2, adopted by the Town Council after a budget review of the
building and zoning office's registration-program costs, makes the
following changes:

(a) Replacement of Amendment 1's rebate clause. Amendment 2 explicitly
replaces Amendment 1's registered-turbine rebate clause (Section 5,
subsection (a)) in its entirety. Effective as of Amendment 2's adoption
date, a registered turbine receives a 35% rebate off the combined base
fee and rotor-diameter surcharge described in Appendix A, superseding the
20% figure previously established by Amendment 1. This revised rebate
applies to all registered turbines as of Amendment 2's effective date,
including turbines that registered under Amendment 1's original terms
before Amendment 2 was adopted; Amendment 2 does not grandfather any
turbine into the superseded 20% rate, and the 20% figure in Section 5(a)
should be read as historical only and is no longer in effect for any
turbine as of this amendment.

(b) Scope clarification on shared turbines. Amendment 2 further clarifies
that a single small wind turbine serving multiple dwelling units on one
lot (such as a duplex, triplex, or small apartment building) is treated
as one turbine for purposes of the fee schedule in Appendix A; the
applicable fee depends on rotor diameter and registration status as
described in Appendix A, and does not vary based on the number of
dwelling units served by the turbine.

Amendment 2 does not alter the permit requirement in Section 2, the
operating restrictions in Section 3, or the exceptions in Section 4.

The Town Council's stated rationale for increasing the rebate from 20% to
35% under Amendment 2, despite the budget review that prompted the
amendment, was that registration uptake following Amendment 1 had been
lower than the building and zoning office's original projection, and the
Council concluded that a larger rebate was a more effective incentive
than the smaller one originally set. The budget review's cost analysis
showed that the administrative cost of processing a registration was
largely fixed regardless of the rebate percentage applied, so increasing
the rebate was not expected to materially increase the office's
per-registration processing cost.

#### Appendix A: Fee Schedule

The permit fee for a small wind turbine is calculated as follows.

Every turbine pays a base fee of 90 currency units.

A rotor-diameter surcharge of 50 currency units applies to any turbine
with a rotor diameter over 3.0 meters. Turbines with a rotor diameter at
or under 3.0 meters pay no rotor-diameter surcharge.

For a registered turbine, as defined in Section 1, the combined total of
the base fee and any applicable rotor-diameter surcharge is rebated at
the rate currently in effect under Amendment 2, Section 6(a) — a 35%
rebate — rather than the superseded 20% rate originally set by Amendment
1.

For example, a registered turbine with a rotor diameter over 3.0 meters
pays a base fee of 90 plus a rotor-diameter surcharge of 50, for a
combined subtotal of 140, rebated by 35% under the currently effective
rate, yielding a final fee. Fee amounts should always be calculated using
the currently effective rebate rate under Amendment 2 rather than any
earlier rate that appears elsewhere in this document for historical
reference only.

Fees are payable in full at the time of permit issuance and are not
prorated or refunded if a turbine is later deregistered or removed. The
building and zoning office may adjust the base fee, surcharge, or rebate
rate by future amendment; any such change would be reflected in a
subsequent revision of this appendix and would supersede the figures
above in the same manner that Amendment 2 superseded Amendment 1's rebate
rate.

The fee schedule applies uniformly across all residential property types
within the scope of this code, including single-family homes and
multi-unit residential buildings served by a single shared turbine, per
the clarification in Section 6(b) above. The building and zoning office
accepts payment by check, money order, or the town's online payment
portal; partial payments are not accepted, and a permit application is
not considered complete until the applicable fee is paid in full. Fee
amounts are reviewed by the building and zoning office every two years as
part of its budget cycle, though no change to the base fee or surcharge
has been made since the original ordinance's adoption; only the
registered-turbine rebate rate has changed, first by Amendment 1 and then
by Amendment 2 as described above.

#### Section 7: Enforcement and Penalties

A property owner found to be operating a small wind turbine without a
permit, or violating the operating restrictions of Section 3 without a
qualifying exception under Section 4, is subject to a warning for a first
violation and a civil penalty for any subsequent violation within a
twelve-month period. Enforcement is carried out by the building and
zoning office, which may refer repeated or willful violations to the town
code enforcement division. This code does not authorize enforcement
action against uses not addressed by this code, such as rooftop solar
arrays, utility-scale wind installations, or commercial and industrial
generation practices, none of which fall within the scope of this
ordinance as defined in Section 1.

Enforcement actions under this section follow a standard process:
inspection or complaint intake, a documented warning with a stated
corrective period for a first violation, and a civil penalty hearing
before the code enforcement division for any subsequent violation within
the twelve-month look-back period described above. A property owner may
appeal a civil penalty to the code enforcement division within thirty
days of the penalty notice. Nothing in this section limits the building
and zoning office's discretion to work informally with a property owner
to bring a turbine into compliance before escalating to a formal
enforcement action, and in practice most first violations are resolved
through the warning process alone without proceeding to a civil penalty.

#### Section 8: Administration

The building and zoning office is responsible for administering the
permit and registration process described in Sections 2, 5, and 6, for
publishing the currently effective fee schedule under Appendix A, and for
declaring noise-advisory levels under the town's community noise
management plan referenced in Section 3. The office publishes an annual
report summarizing permits issued, turbines registered, and enforcement
actions taken under this code, which is presented to the Town Council
each spring alongside the office's budget request.

The building and zoning office also maintains a public registry of
registered turbines, searchable by address, which property buyers and
sellers may consult to confirm whether a turbine's registered status will
transfer with the property. Registered-turbine status is tied to the
property rather than to the individual owner, so a new owner of a
property with an existing registered turbine continues to receive the
rebate under Appendix A without needing to re-register, provided the
turbine itself is not materially altered in a way that would require a
new permit application under Section 2.

Questions about whether a specific use case falls within the scope of
this code, including uses this code does not address, should be directed
to the building and zoning office for an administrative determination;
this document is not exhaustive of every possible generation scenario a
property owner might encounter; and where the ordinance is silent, the
building and zoning office may issue guidance, though such guidance does
not itself amend this code and any actual expansion of scope requires a
future amendment adopted by the Town Council in the same manner as
Amendment 1 and Amendment 2 above.

#### Section 9: Relationship to Other Town Codes

This code addresses only small wind turbines and turbine operating
restrictions as defined in Section 1. Rooftop photovoltaic solar arrays,
including any panels or solar-collection equipment mounted on a roof or
elsewhere on a parcel, are governed by a separate town solar-installation
code and are not addressed by any provision of this document.
Utility-scale and commercial wind installations, including any turbine
serving commercial operations or feeding the wholesale grid, are likewise
governed by separate commercial-generation provisions not addressed by
this code. Property owners with questions about generation outside the
scope defined in Section 1 should consult the relevant separate code or
contact the building and zoning office directly, since this document does
not speak to those situations one way or the other. This code does not
attempt to enumerate every generation scenario that falls outside its
scope; the definitions in Section 1 and the description of covered uses
above are the governing test for whether a given scenario falls within or
outside this code, not any illustrative list.

#### Section 10: Effective Dates

The original ordinance took effect thirty days after adoption. Amendment
1 took effect on its own adoption date, several years after the original
ordinance. Amendment 2 took effect on its own adoption date,
approximately eighteen months after Amendment 1. All provisions in this
consolidated document reflect the code as currently in effect, with
historical rates and clauses that have since been superseded retained in
the text only for reference and explicitly marked as superseded where
applicable, as with Amendment 1's rebate rate in Section 5(a).

This consolidated document is republished by the building and zoning
office whenever a new amendment is adopted, so that residents,
contractors, and town staff can consult a single current text rather than
needing to cross-reference the original ordinance against a separate list
of amendments. Prior consolidated versions, including the version
published after Amendment 1 but before Amendment 2, remain available in
the town clerk's public records archive for historical reference, but the
version currently in effect is always the most recently published
consolidated text, which is this document.

## Scenarios

- **Scenario 1**: A homeowner wants to install a residential small wind
  turbine with a rotor diameter of 2.4 meters and does not plan to
  register the turbine. Is this permitted, and what fee applies?
- **Scenario 2**: A homeowner installs a residential small wind turbine
  with a rotor diameter of 3.6 meters and registers it under the
  currently effective registration program. Is this permitted, and what
  fee applies?
- **Scenario 3**: During a declared noise-advisory level 2, a homeowner
  wants to run their turbine outside daylight hours on an automated timer
  set to a short nightly "maintenance test" cycle, relying on the manual
  maintenance spin-test exception. Is this permitted?
- **Scenario 4**: A commercial wind-farm operator wants to know whether
  this code permits operating a utility-scale turbine that feeds the
  wholesale grid. Is this permitted?
- **Scenario 5**: A homeowner wants to install a rooftop photovoltaic
  solar array to power an indoor battery system. Is this permitted under
  this code?
- **Scenario 6**: A homeowner registered their small wind turbine under
  Amendment 1's original terms, before Amendment 2 was adopted. Their
  rotor diameter is 3.2 meters. What rebate rate applies to them now that
  Amendment 2 is in effect?
- **Scenario 7**: A homeowner installed a small wind turbine without
  obtaining a permit under Section 2, and now claims the situation is
  covered by a "temporary noise-advisory hardship," pointing to the
  Section 4 exceptions. Is this permitted?
- **Scenario 8**: A small apartment building with 5 units shares a single
  registered small wind turbine. The turbine's rotor diameter is 3.9
  meters. Is this permitted, and what fee applies?
