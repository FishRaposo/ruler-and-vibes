### Meridian Strait Ferries — Second Quarter 2026 Operations Report

Period covered: April 1 - June 30, 2026. Prepared by the Office of Fleet
Operations for the board of directors and the regional transit authority.
This report summarizes fleet status, maintenance activity, on-time
performance, ridership, safety incidents, personnel assignments, and
capital investment across the Meridian Strait Ferries network for the
second quarter of 2026. It is intended as the primary quarterly reference
document for board members, route planners, and the regional transit
authority's oversight staff, and it is cross-referenced by the annual
report published each December.

#### Section 1: Overview

Meridian Strait Ferries operates a network of scheduled passenger and
vehicle ferry routes connecting the coastal communities surrounding the
Meridian Strait, a narrow tidal channel separating the mainland from
Saltmere Island and the smaller outlying communities of Tillcombe and
Corvid Bay. The company was founded in 1987 as a single-route operator
running one wooden-hulled vessel between the mainland pier and Saltmere
Island; it has since grown into the largest regional ferry operator on
this stretch of coast, carrying both commuter and tourist traffic across
a network that now spans five principal routes and several seasonal
feeder services.

As of the close of the second quarter, the company's active fleet
consists of 14 active vessels, ranging from small passenger-only
catamarans to larger vehicle-and-passenger ferries capable of carrying
freight trailers. Of these, 11 vessels were in continuous revenue
service throughout the quarter, two underwent scheduled dry-dock
maintenance for part of the quarter, and one remained laid up pending a
propulsion overhaul that is expected to conclude early in the third
quarter. Fleet planners note that this level of availability is
consistent with the company's long-term maintenance rotation, under
which no more than three vessels are expected to be out of revenue
service at any one time during a normal quarter.

The overview below covers the roster, maintenance, on-time performance,
ridership, safety, personnel, and capital sections in turn, followed by
two appendices and supporting footnotes. Readers preparing summary
materials from this report should take particular care with the
maintenance-spend figure in Section 3, which is superseded by a
corrigendum in Appendix B, and with the two similarly named vessels
described in Section 2.

The quarter was broadly stable operationally, with weather-related
disruption below the five-year seasonal average and no unplanned
extended route suspensions. Storm activity in April was limited to two
days of reduced-schedule operation on the more exposed north route, and
no route required a full-day suspension at any point in the quarter. The
most notable operational event of the quarter was a corrigendum to the
previously circulated maintenance-spend figure, detailed in Appendix B
below, which readers should treat as authoritative over the figure
printed in Section 3. The finance office has asked that all downstream
materials, including board packets and the transit authority's quarterly
filing, cite the corrigendum's figure rather than the original Section 3
number.

#### Section 2: Fleet Roster

The fleet roster below lists each vessel currently registered to
Meridian Strait Ferries, along with its passenger capacity and home
route. Several vessels carry similar names inherited from the historic
naming convention, under which all vessels are named for coastal
landforms in the region, and readers should take care to distinguish
between them, particularly where two names differ only by a suffix.

The vessel Aldercrest, a vehicle-and-passenger ferry serving the
mainland-to-Saltmere Island crossing, has a rated passenger capacity of
412. It is one of the two largest vessels in the fleet and was
commissioned in 2011 as part of a fleet-modernization program that also
introduced the Windflaw. Aldercrest's capacity reflects a vehicle deck
configuration that trades some passenger seating for freight-trailer
space, and it is the vessel most commonly assigned to the primary north
route during peak summer travel.

The vessel Alderpoint, a smaller passenger-only catamaran serving the
Corvid Bay commuter route, has a rated passenger capacity of 380. Despite
the similarity in name to Aldercrest, Alderpoint is a distinct vessel
built for a different route profile, with a shallower draft suited to
the tidal flats near Corvid Bay, and it carries no vehicle deck at all.
Fleet planning materials sometimes abbreviate both vessel names to
"Alder-" internally, which has occasionally caused confusion in informal
correspondence; this report always uses the full vessel name to avoid
that ambiguity.

Other fleet vessels include the Tidewright (capacity 260, Tillcombe
route), the Saltgrass (capacity 190, Corvid Bay secondary route), the
Windflaw (capacity 340, mainland-to-Saltmere secondary crossing), the
Grayling Run (capacity 150, seasonal charter service), and the Cormorant
Reach, a vehicle-and-passenger ferry serving the Saltmere-to-Tillcombe
inter-island crossing.[^1] The Cormorant Reach was the second vessel
commissioned under the modernization program mentioned above and
entered service one year after Aldercrest. The remaining seven vessels
in the fleet are smaller passenger tenders assigned to feeder routes not
separately itemized in this report; their combined capacity is tracked
in the internal fleet database rather than in quarterly filings.

Each vessel undergoes an annual class survey and quarterly safety
inspection; results of the second-quarter inspections are summarized in
Section 6 below. No vessel failed its second-quarter inspection outright,
though two vessels received minor corrective-action notices unrelated to
the incidents described in Section 6.

The roster is reviewed annually by the board's fleet committee, which
this year recommended no additions or retirements for the coming fiscal
year. The committee's report noted that the fleet's average age remains
within the target range set by the ten-year fleet renewal plan adopted
several years ago, and that no vessel currently in service is expected
to require replacement before the plan's next scheduled review. The
committee also noted that naming continuity, including the pairing of
similarly named vessels such as Aldercrest and Alderpoint, is a
deliberate historical convention rather than an oversight, and asked
that future reports continue to spell out full vessel names in all
board-facing materials to avoid ambiguity in correspondence and in
insurance filings.

#### Section 3: Maintenance

Scheduled maintenance activity during the second quarter included two
dry-dock overhauls, four routine engine services, and one unscheduled
hull repair following a minor dockside collision at the Tillcombe
terminal in May. Total scheduled maintenance spend for the quarter, as
originally tabulated by the maintenance office at the close of the
quarter, was $48,150. This figure covered parts, contracted labor, and
dry-dock facility fees across all maintenance events during the period,
and it was the figure presented at the initial internal budget review
meeting held in the first week of July.

Readers should note that this Section 3 figure was subsequently found to
omit a late-arriving invoice from a subcontractor; see the corrigendum in
Appendix B, which supersedes the figure above and should be treated as
the authoritative quarterly maintenance-spend total for all reporting
purposes going forward.

The unscheduled hull repair, the only non-routine maintenance event of
the quarter, was completed within nine days and did not require the
affected vessel to be withdrawn from service outside its normal
overnight layover window. The two scheduled dry-dock overhauls each took
approximately three weeks and were completed on the maintenance
schedule set at the start of the year. The four routine engine services
were completed within their normal single-day maintenance windows and
did not affect the schedule.

#### Section 4: On-Time Performance

Fleet-wide on-time performance for the second quarter was 91.4%, measured
as the share of scheduled departures leaving within five minutes of
posted time across all routes and vessels. This represents a modest
improvement over the same quarter last year, driven largely by improved
terminal staging procedures introduced in the first quarter, and it
continues a gradual upward trend that fleet operations attributes to the
staging changes rather than to any change in vessel reliability.

Performance varied by route. The Corvid Bay commuter route posted the
strongest on-time performance in the fleet, aided by its short crossing
time and low weather exposure, and it has consistently led the fleet in
this measure for the past several quarters. The Tillcombe route posted
performance in line with the fleet average, benefiting from a relatively
sheltered crossing and predictable tidal conditions.

The north route, which comprises the mainland-to-Saltmere Island
crossing and its secondary crossing through the Meridian Strait's
northern channel, posted an on-time rate of 87.2% for the quarter, the
weakest of any route group. Fleet operations attributes this shortfall
to increased tidal current variability in the northern channel during
May and June, which required more conservative scheduling margins on
that crossing. A working group has been asked to review whether the
current scheduling margin on the north route should be permanently
widened, with a recommendation expected before the fourth quarter.

#### Section 5: Ridership

Total ridership across all routes for the second quarter was 612,400
passengers, an increase over the prior quarter consistent with seasonal
demand patterns. The Corvid Bay commuter route carried the largest single
share of ridership, reflecting its role as the primary weekday commuter
link between Corvid Bay and the mainland. Seasonal charter service on
the Grayling Run began in late May and contributed a modest but growing
share of quarterly ridership, with bookings trending ahead of the same
period last year.

Vehicle transport volumes, tracked separately from passenger counts,
also rose during the quarter, consistent with increased tourist and
freight traffic ahead of the summer season. Route planners expect
ridership to continue climbing into the third quarter as the peak summer
travel period begins, and have flagged the Saltmere Island terminal's
vehicle queuing capacity as a possible constraint, discussed further in
Section 8.

#### Section 6: Safety

The quarter saw 2 reportable safety incidents, both classified as minor
under the regional transit authority's incident-severity scale. The
first involved a minor dockside collision at the Tillcombe terminal
(referenced above in Section 3) that required hull repair but caused no
injuries; the vessel involved was back in scheduled service within the
nine-day repair window described in Section 3. The second involved a
passenger slip-and-fall on a wet vehicle deck aboard the Windflaw, which
resulted in a first-aid treatment on scene and no lost-time injury.

Both incidents were reviewed by the safety office and closed with
corrective-action items completed before the end of the quarter. No
incidents during the quarter met the regional transit authority's
threshold for mandatory external investigation, and the safety office's
quarterly summary to the board characterized the quarter as within
normal safety performance bounds for the fleet.

The safety office also completed its scheduled quarterly review of
emergency-equipment compliance across the fleet, finding all 14 active
vessels current on life-raft certification and fire-suppression
inspection. This review is conducted independently of the class survey
mentioned in Section 2 and is reported separately to the regional
transit authority each quarter as part of the authority's ongoing
oversight of passenger-vessel safety across all operators in the
region, not only Meridian Strait Ferries.

#### Section 7: Personnel

Fleet operations is organized under a senior master mariner assigned to
each vessel as captain of record, supported by rotating relief captains
and deck crews. Staffing levels remained stable during the quarter, with
one new relief captain completing certification and joining the roster
in June, and no captain vacancies remained unfilled at quarter's end.

The captain of record for the Cormorant Reach is Captain Elena Vosk, who
has served with Meridian Strait Ferries for eleven years and has held the
Cormorant Reach assignment since 2022. Captain Vosk also chairs the
fleet's quarterly safety review committee referenced in Section 6, and
was recognized this quarter for the committee's work closing out both
incident reviews ahead of the board's July meeting.

The captain of record for the Aldercrest is Captain Dov Marren, and the
captain of record for the Alderpoint is Captain Nils Aggerholm. Relief
captain assignments for the remaining vessels are documented in the
internal crewing system and are not itemized in this report. Captain
Marren has held the Aldercrest assignment since the vessel's
commissioning in 2011, making him the longest-serving captain of record
currently active in the fleet.

#### Section 8: Terminal and Capital Investment

Capital investment during the quarter focused on terminal infrastructure.
The board approved a dock refurbishment budget of $215,000 for the
Tillcombe Terminal, covering pile replacement, fendering upgrades, and
resurfacing of the vehicle staging apron. Work is scheduled to begin in
the third quarter and is expected to take approximately ten weeks, with
minimal disruption anticipated to the Tillcombe route's regular schedule
since work will proceed alongside, rather than in place of, normal
terminal operations.

A smaller capital allocation was approved for signage upgrades at the
Corvid Bay terminal, and a feasibility study was commissioned to assess
expanding the Saltmere Island terminal's vehicle queuing capacity ahead
of anticipated ridership growth over the coming two years. The
feasibility study is expected to report back to the board in the fourth
quarter, alongside the north-route scheduling review mentioned in
Section 4.

Together, the Tillcombe dock refurbishment, the Corvid Bay signage
upgrade, and the Saltmere Island feasibility study make up the entirety
of this quarter's approved capital program; no other capital requests
were before the board during the second quarter. The finance office
notes that the Tillcombe allocation is funded from the terminal capital
reserve rather than from the operating budget referenced in Section 3,
so the corrigendum in Appendix B has no bearing on the capital figures
reported in this section.

#### Appendix A: Route Table

The route table below summarizes scheduled service frequency across the
network's principal routes, as maintained by the route-planning office.

The mainland-to-Saltmere Island crossing (north route, primary) runs 12
scheduled crossings per day. The Corvid Bay commuter route runs 18
scheduled crossings per day on weekdays and 10 on weekends, reflecting
its role as the fleet's highest-frequency service. The Tillcombe route
runs 6 scheduled crossings per day. The Saltmere Passage route, which
connects Saltmere Island to the smaller outlying community of Wrenfell
Cove and is served by the Cormorant Reach, runs 9 scheduled crossings per
day.

Seasonal charter service on the Grayling Run does not follow a fixed
daily schedule and is booked on demand during the summer charter season,
so it is not included in the daily-crossing figures above. The
route-planning office reviews this table at the start of each quarter
and adjusts frequencies as ridership and vessel availability warrant.

#### Appendix B: Corrigendum to Section 3

After this report's initial internal circulation, the finance office
identified a subcontractor invoice for hull-repair welding materials,
dated late in the quarter, that had not been incorporated into the
Section 3 maintenance-spend figure. Incorporating this invoice, the
correct, final maintenance-spend figure for Q2 2026 is $48,730,
superseding the figure printed in Section 3 above. All downstream
financial summaries and board materials should cite this corrected
figure as the quarter's maintenance spend, and the finance office has
updated its internal ledger accordingly.

No other figures in this report are affected by this corrigendum. The
finance office has confirmed that ridership, on-time performance, and
capital investment figures elsewhere in this report were unaffected by
the late-arriving invoice.
